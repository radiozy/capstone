
Secretary of Defense William S. Cohen has postponed his trip next month to China, citing tension between Washington and Beijing, the Pentagon announced today.
On the day Congress released its report on Chinese nuclear espionage, Kenneth H. Bacon, the Pentagon spokesman, said Mr. Cohen believed that this was "not the easiest time to visit China."
"He did have tentative plans to go in June," Mr. Bacon said. 
"He feels that it may be better to go later. 
He remains, of course, steadfastly committed to engagement with China as a long-term policy."
China temporarily broke off all military-to-military programs with the United States after the accidental bombing by NATO of the Chinese Embassy in Belgrade on May 7, which killed three Chinese journalists and wounded 20 other Chinese officials.
NATO's Supreme Commander, Gen. Wesley K. Clark, delivered his report today on the bombing accident, saying it had been caused by faulty maps prepared by the United States.
