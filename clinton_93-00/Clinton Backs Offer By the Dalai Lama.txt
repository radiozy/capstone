
President Clinton told the Dalai Lama on Thursday that he supported the Tibetan religious leader's offer to meet with senior Chinese officials, the White House said.
The Beijing Government criticized Mr. Clinton and Vice President Al Gore today for having met with the exiled Tibetan, saying this constituted "serious interference in China's internal affairs."
It also repeated that Tibet was a part of China.
The White House said Mr. Clinton and Mr. Gore had discussed violations of religious and cultural rights in Tibet with the Dalai Lama, who was ending a two-week visit to the United States.
"These issues are of concern to the Administration, especially as it reviews the conditions required for renewal of most-favored-nation status for China," the White House said.
The Administration, concerned about human rights abuses in China, must decide by June 3 whether to renew its trade status.
The Dalai Lama has been living in exile since a 1959 uprising against Chinese rule failed. 
The White House statement welcomed his offer in a speech in New York on Wednesday to meet with senior Chinese leaders to discuss differences.
