
China has tightened its grip over the northwestern region of Xinjiang, transferring a team of crack troops to a city rocked by riots in 1997 and arresting hundreds of suspected Muslim separatists, an official newspaper reported today.
Some 1,000 soldiers were transferred to Yining city last month, the Xinjiang Legal Daily said in an issue that arrived today in Beijing. 
The unit includes troops mobilized to quash riots in 1997 that killed nine.
Three weeks after the riots, nine other people were killed and dozens wounded by home-made bombs in Urunqi, Xinjiang's capital.
Xinjiang, dominated by Uighur Muslims, has been rocked by rioting, bombings and assassinations since the mid-1990's. 
Uighur militants have agitated for an independent state in Xinjiang.
Amnesty International today accused Beijing of stepping up persecution of Uighurs since the riots in 1997. 
Scores of Uighurs were apparently detained in villages near Yining in April 1998 after security forces reportedly shot dead six Uighur youths, Amnesty said.
A Foreign Ministry spokeswoman called the accusation baseless.
