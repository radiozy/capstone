
China's legislature approved prison terms and stiffer fines for copyright violators today, meeting a principal United States demand five days after Washington threatened trade sanctions.
The new rules were approved by the National People's Congress and take effect immediately. 
The rules for the first time include prison terms. 
Violators of copyright laws face up to seven years in jail, the state-run New China News Agency reported.
The United States said last Thursday that it would give China six months to curb what Washington said was widespread piracy of intellectual property, like computer software and movies.
If the United States remains unsatisfied, it could impose punitive tariffs on up to $800 million worth of Chinese goods -- the amount that Washington says American companies are losing each year from Chinese piracy.
The regulations approved today also require violators to compensate copyright owners for losses, according to the Chinese news agency. 
American companies had complained that violators previously had to pay only modest fines.
