
The Year of the Monkey in the Chinese calendar will give way on Jan. 
23 to the Year of the Rooster, and that may mean looking out carefully to keep your head off the chopping block.
"Roosters zip back and forth, this way and that, so the Year of the Rooster will be a time of instability and perhaps bad luck," explained an elderly Beijing woman with bound feet.
Some couples are racing to get married before the Year of the Rooster begins, for who wants a wedding linked with instability?
No one dares guess whether the instability will affect the heartbeat of 88-year-old Deng Xiaoping, whose imminent death has been rumored for more than a half-dozen years. 
All Western diplomats will say is that one day the rumors may be true.
While the coming year will be known in English as the Year of the Rooster or the Year of the Cock, in Chinese it is simply Ji Nian, which translates directly as Year of the (unisex) Chicken. 
Perhaps to flatter the masculinity of men born under this sign (1981, 1969, 1957, 1945 and every 12 years before), Chinese pictures of the zodiac always show a proud rooster rather than a hen.
