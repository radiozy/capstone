
Speaker Newt Gingrich plans to travel to China in early April to meet with top Chinese Government officials.
Mr. Gingrich's spokeswoman, Christina Martin, said the Speaker, joined by other lawmakers, was making the trip because Congress would soon be reviewing President Clinton's expected renewal of most-favored-nation trading status for China.
The visit, which people familiar with the planning said was arrangedin part by former Secretary of State Henry A. Kissinger, "is well timed in allowing members to assess the current state of U.S.-Asian relations," Ms. Martin said.
Mr. Gingrich and other Republican leaders have generally supported the Clinton Administration's continuation of most-favored-nation status for China. 
But the trip by the Speaker -- as well as one by Vice President Al Gore, who is to visit China later this month -- comes at a time when conservatives in Congress have been calling for steps against the Chinese over human rights issues. 
Indeed, some are calling for Mr. Gingrich to reconsider going.
A top Congressional leadership aide said that Mr. Gingrich and the Chinese officials would discuss a range of issues and that human rights was "expected" to be on the table.
Gary L. Bauer, head of the conservative Family Research Council, wrote Mr. Gingrich a letter today asking him to make human rights issues his top priority on the visit.
