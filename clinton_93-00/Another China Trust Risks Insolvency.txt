
Another municipal investment trust in south China appears to be on the verge of bankruptcy, and officials may let it go under rather than attempt a rescue, Hong Kong newspapers said today.
The assets of the Guangzhou International Trust and Investment Corporation are still sufficient to cover all borrowing, but the balance could shift significantly as a result of recent sales and trouble with debtors' repayments, said Deputy Mayor Shen Bainian of Guangzhou, the city once known as Canton that is the capital of Guangdong province.
A preliminary audit by the Guangzhou city government found that the trust had debts of $2.3 billion and assets of more than $2.4 billion. 
The city is trying to help the company sell assets but will not provide any cash if the company's debts become unmanageable, Mr. Shen said.
If the Guangzhou trust is allowed to fail, it would be the second time a state-backed trust in China has been refused a bailout. 
In October, Beijing closed Guangdong International Trust and Investment, the investment arm of Guangdong province. 
It had debts of $4.3 billion and assets of $2.6 billion. 
And last week, the investment trust of a northern city, Dalian, reportedly missed payments on millions of dollars.
