
A Chinese dissident on a weeklong hunger strike to protest China's crackdown on a fledgling democratic party was released from Bellevue Hospital Center yesterday after being taken there Saturday, a hospital spokesman said.
The dissident, Wang Xizhe, one of China's first pro-democracy activists, began his hunger strike during a protest on Dec. 
27 in front of the United Nations headquarters, said Wang Bingzhang, an organizer. 
Wang Xizhe returned to his home yesterday, but hopes to continue his hunger strike for at least two more days, Wang Bingzhang said.
Last month, the Chinese Government sentenced three men in China to prison terms of more than a decade each after they tried to organize the Chinese Democracy Party as a formal opposition to the Chinese Communist Party. 
Wang Xizhe, the secretary of the overseas committee of the Democracy Party, was joined by about 15 others who staged shorter hunger strikes last week, said Wang Bingzhang, no relation, who is an adviser to the Democracy Party.
"We organized this hunger strike to protest the arrests by the Chinese Government and to urge the United Nations and international society to do something to stop these persecutions," he said.
Pro-democracy activists had hoped that the Chinese Government would relax political controls after President Clinton's trip to China in June, and leaders began applying to register their party with the Government. 
But those expectations were quickly abandoned after the first arrests and a speech last month by President Jiang Zemin, who said that challenges to one-party rule in China would be "annihilated."
Wang Bingzhang said he and Wang Xizhe were to testify about the arrests later this week before the House of Representatives.
"We advised him to eat," Wang Bingzhang said. 
"He has to recover his health to go to Washington."
