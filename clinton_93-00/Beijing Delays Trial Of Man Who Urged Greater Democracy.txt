
Chinese officials have postponed the trial of a democracy advocate, which was scheduled to start this week, after the United States Embassy here urged them to drop or postpone the case.
Last week, Beijing courts said the trial of the advocate, Fang Jue, on charges of fraud and illegal business activities would begin on Tuesday. 
That was the day set for the start of Prime Minister Zhu Rongji's official visit to the United States, which is intended to patch up growing conflicts over human rights, trade, missile defenses and charges of Chinese nuclear espionage.
On learning of the trial date, the American Embassy urged China to release Mr. Fang and drop the charges, an embassy official said. 
"If they couldn't do that, we noted that the timing was very inauspicious," said the official. 
And if a trial does occur, the embassy told the Chinese, it would like to observe the proceedings firsthand, something that has never been permitted in a political trial.
Mr. Fang, 44, was formerly a government planner in the southern province of Fujian but broke off to start his own trading company. 
He had never been openly involved in dissident activities, but in early 1998 he distributed to foreign journalists a manifesto calling for democratic government, negotiations on autonomy for Tibet and other changes. 
He said the document reflected conversations among many younger Government and Communist Party officials, though he refused to name any.
Exiled dissidents in New York championed his report, and he was detained last July. 
Eight months later he was told of the charges against him, relating to alleged acceptance of company payments while he was still an official in 1995. 
He has denied the charges and his sister says they were fabricated.
