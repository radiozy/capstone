
A senior Commerce Department official will travel to China next week for the first round of economic talks between the two nations since NATO's bombing of the Chinese Embassy in Belgrade, the Clinton Administration said today.
David Aaron, Commerce Undersecretary for International Trade, will be in Beijing on July 26-27 for discussions with senior Chinese trade and industry officials.
Administration officials said they hope Mr. Aaron's trip will help restart talks Beijing broke off in anger following the May 7 bombing, which NATO insists was an accident. 
He will be the first American official since the bombing to make a trip to Beijing that is not directly related to the bombing. 
Other American officials have visited to explain to China that NATO mistook the embassy for a Yugoslav military site and to discuss compensation for the Chinese who were killed or injured.
The United States would like to resume talks on China's 13-year effort to join the World Trade Organization. 
For that to occur, China will have to strike a deal lowering numerous barriers to sales of American manufactured goods and farm products in China.
