
China indicated last Monday that it intended to put the genie of Hong Kong's civil liberties back in the bottle of colonial rule, by bringing back laws restricting freedom of assembly and demonstration.
Tung Chee-hwa, who will become the chief executive in Hong Kong on July 1, when China resumes sovereignty, said "social order" must prevail over "individual rights."
Beijing intends to revoke the right to stage peaceful protests without the permission of the police and the right to form political organizations without governmental approval. 
Mr. Tung said, "the issue is not about the freedom of expression or freedom of assembly."
In Hong Kong, China's action provoked a hurricane of protest. 
Martin Lee, the chairman of the largest party in the elected legislature, a body that Beijing will abolish and replace with its handpicked members on July 1, denounced China's move. 
"We believe it has everything to do with human rights," said Mr. Lee."Mr. Tung should learn to defend Hong Kong."   EDWARD A. GARGAN
