
The Clinton Administration expects to name on Friday at least $800 million in Chinese products that could face trade sanctions because of China's failure to curb unauthorized copying of American products, officials said today.
Prime targets for punitive tariffs include shoes, toys, sporting goods, silk clothing, radios and suitcases made in China. 
These products make up a large part of China's export trade with the United States, which is already running at a record level of $32.4 billion through October.
The Administration broke off talks with the Beijing Government on Dec. 
14, charging that the Chinese were not negotiating seriously. 
No further talks are scheduled before the Friday deadline, which sets in motion a process that could ultimately lead to higher tariffs on selected Chinese goods.
In January, the Administration settled a dispute with China over textile trade by threatening to sharply reduce the United States quota for Chinese textile imports.
The sanctions will not take effect before the end of January, at the earliest. 
If past trade showdowns are any guide, further negotiations between the United States and China will probably take place during that month.
The $800 million of products on the retaliation list is about equal to the damage American companies claim they suffer annually because of failure by the Chinese Government to enforce the copyright portion of a 1992 agreement with the United States to prevent intellectual property violations. 
Patent and trademark violations could boost the value of the list several hundred million dollars.
