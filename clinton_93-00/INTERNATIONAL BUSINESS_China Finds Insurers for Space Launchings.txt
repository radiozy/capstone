
China will resume its commercial space program in July after international insurers agreed to underwrite the next launching, a spokesman for China's rocket launcher, the China Great Wall Industry Corporation, said today.
China's satellite program has been held up since February, when a Long March 3B rocket carrying an Intelsat satellite exploded seconds after takeoff from southwest China's Xichang space base.
Munich Re, the world's biggest reinsurer, will lead the international group of companies backing the launching of the Apstar-1A satellite owned by the APT Satellite Company. 
APT's shareholders include Singapore Telecom, the Charoen Pokhpand Group of Thailand, and commercial arms of the Chinese Army, space and telecommunications authorities.
An official with Munich Re in Germany said the insurer was satisfied that the Long March 3B's problem "would not repeat itself."
The Great Wall spokesman said the launching, likely to scheduled for early July, was insured last Saturday.
The satellite is made by the Hughes Space and Communications division of the General Motors Corporation. 
It has already been delivered to the Xichang launching site.
