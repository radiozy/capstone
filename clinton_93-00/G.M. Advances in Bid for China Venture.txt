
The General Motors Corporation has been named the top candidate in competition to negotiate a joint venture with the Chinese Government to build small cars in China, the company said today.
General Motors and the Beijing Government recently signed a memorandum of understanding that essentially makes the company the sole negotiating partner for the venture, a G.M. spokesman, Michael Meyerand, said.
Honda Motor, Hyundai of South Korea and the German auto makers BMW and the Mercedes-Benz unit of Daimler-Benz were competing for the project. 
It would provide a major foothold in what is expected to become one of the world's fastest-growing and largest automotive markets in the next century.
If successful, G.M.'s Opel AG European subsidiary will probably operate its end of the 50-50 joint venture. 
It is expected to build Opel Corsas or Astras, although the exact product is subject to negotiation, the G.M. spokesman said.
The product could end up being a variant of the new mini-car that G.M. this week said it planned to build in Brazil for the South American market, he said.
Production could reach as many as 100,000 units a year. 
The project would involve updating a plant in the southern city of Guangzhou. 
Peugeot Citroen, which holds a 22 percent stake in the plant, has been seeking to leave the venture.
G.M.'s project, if approved, would be the auto maker's third major production operation in China. 
It has a truck-assembly joint venture that is under construction in the northeastern city of Shenyang, and it recently got approval for a $1 billion joint venture scheduled to begin building midsize Buick sedans in Shanghai in late 1998.
