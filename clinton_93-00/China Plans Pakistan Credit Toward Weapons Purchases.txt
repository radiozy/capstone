
China will extend credit to Pakistan so it can buy weapons under an agreement that the two sides signed today, the Pakistani Government said.
Although the Government did not specify the amount of the credit or the type of the weapons Pakistan would buy, it said in a statement that the agreement would "further promote mutual cooperation and strengthen the bonds of friendship between the armed forces of the two countries."
The agreement was signed here by Gen. Zhang Wannian, chief of general staff of the Chinese Army.
The United States stopped all military assistance to Pakistan in 1990 on the belief that Pakistan was trying to make nuclear weapons, a charge denied by Islamabad.
