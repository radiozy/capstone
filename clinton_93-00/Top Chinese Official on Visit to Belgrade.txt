
Li Peng, No. 2 in the Chinese Communist Party hierarchy, arrived in Belgrade today for a three-day official visit, the Yugoslav state news agency Tanjug said.
Mr. Li, president of the National People's Congress, is the most senior international politician to visit Yugoslavia since NATO's air war a year ago. 
China, along with Russia, strongly opposed the 78-day bombing campaign to drive Yugoslav forces out of Kosovo, during which NATO bombs hit the Chinese Embassy in Belgrade, killing three Chinese people.
His visit is widely seen as a sign of support for President Slobodan Milosevic, who is isolated by the West.
Tanjug said that Mr. Li would meet top leaders and address Parliament on Monday, as the United Nations marks its first year in charge of Kosovo.
In Kosovo, where there have been continued episodes of violence between Kosovo Albanians and Serbs, a group of people shot dead two ethnic Albanian men and wounded a third in northwestern Kosovo today, a United Nations spokesman said. 
Initial reports that attackers spoke Serbo-Croatian, were not confirmed.
