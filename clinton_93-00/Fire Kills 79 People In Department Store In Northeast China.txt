
A fire killed 79 people in a department store in the northeastern city of Tangshan on Sunday, the official New China News Agency reported today. 
A city fire department official in Tangshan, 100 miles east of Beijing, said 51 people had been injured.
The fire ripped through the three-story Linxi department store during peak shopping hours Sunday afternoon, transforming the store into an inferno, the report said.
"Inside, the three-story building was completely gutted," the news agency said. 
"Outside, walls were blackened by smoke and most windows broken."
The report attributed the fire to workers who neglected safety precautions while welding in the department store.
 
Concern Over Safety
Safety standards have become the object of increasing concern as China embarks on a nationwide construction boom, fueled by the country's rapid economic growth. 
New buildings and factories are being built quickly, while existing stores and restaurants are being remodeled to serve the emerging consumer society.
Official news reports have said accidents on building sites are on the rise, and safety precautions are said to be often ignored in the race to get things built in short order.
Department stores are seen as particularly dangerous since they are often packed with shoppers, crowded with new sales counters and flammable items, and frequently lack sufficient access to exits.
 
Delay in Reporting
Sunday's fire was not reported in tonight's main evening television news because China's official news organizations customarily delay reports about domestic disasters.
In 1991, China reported one of its worst factory fires when a blaze struck a rainwear factory in the southern province of Guangdong, killing at least 66 workers who were sleeping in dormitories attached to the factory building.
In 1976, Tangshan was struck by a major earthquake, measuring 7.8 on the Richter scale, that killed an estimated 250,000 people and destroyed huge sections of the city.
