
Washington's top textile negotiator began talks here today aimed at ending a dispute over United States penalties on Chinese textile imports, American officials said.
Rita Hayes, textile negotiator of the United States trade representative's office, was also to discuss a new China-United States textile accord in talks with China's Ministry of Foreign Trade and Economic Cooperation, an official of the United States Embassy said.
The United States set penalties of $19 million on Chinese imports in September, saying Beijing was shipping textiles through third countries to avoid quota restrictions.
China has threatened to retaliate by temporarily banning imports of some United States textiles, farm goods and alcoholic drinks, with the curbs to go into effect on Dec. 
10.
The two sides must also agree on a new textile accord to replace the pact that expires at the year's end.
China's textile sales have been a sensitive point for American negotiators eager to cut the United States' trade deficit with China, estimated at $35 billion for 1995.
