
Prime Minister Morihiro Hosokawa of Japan pressed Chinese leaders today to exercise greater influence on North Korea so it would give up any nuclear ambitions, saying he was "gravely concerned" about the looming crisis.
"More than any other country, China has the deepest relations with North Korea," Mr. Hosokawa said at a news conference today after talks with President Jiang Zemin and Prime Minister Li Peng. 
"Therefore China could work to move North Korea in a positive direction instead of a negative one."
But there were conflicting reports about the Chinese response. 
Japanese Foreign Ministry officials, briefing reporters on two hours of talks between Mr. Hosokawa and Mr. Li, said the Chinese Prime Minister had pledged to "persuade North Korea" to accept full inspection of its nuclear sites.
The official New China News Agency, however, reported that Mr. Li had "stressed that the issue should be solved through dialogue and negotiation," which has been China's longstanding position.
Asked if Japan would support possible sanctions, Mr. Hosokawa said his Government would respond "in a responsible way."
The Japanese leader's diplomatic initiative toward North Korea today followed an effort by his aides in November at a meeting of Asian and Pacific leaders in Seattle. 
Despite low-key and private entreaties to the Chinese leadership at the time, Japanese diplomats said later, they were rebuffed with explanations that Beijing exerts little influence on North Korea.
With Japanese and South Korean officials still hoping that China will do more, there have been indications that Chinese military leaders have stepped up their contacts with their North Korean colleagues. 
So far, though, all such efforts have been rejected by the North Korean leadership in Pyongyang, prompting Mr. Hosokawa to declare today that "I am gravely concerned about the situation."
