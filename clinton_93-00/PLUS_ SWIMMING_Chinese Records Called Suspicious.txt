
Australian Swimming's president, Terry Gathercole, has written to the sport's world governing body expressing concern over China's latest barrage of extraordinary performances, which threaten to produce another Chinese monopoly in women's swimming.
Gathercole said Australian officials were concerned at the "suspicious" results at China's National Games in Shanghai last week when Chinese women swimmers set two world records and six world-best times for 1997.
"These latest results have come from nowhere and they defy logic," Gathercole said yesterday.
"We know and understand that people are innocent until proven guilty but we, along with the rest of the swimming family, have got to be suspicious. 
Many of these swimmers who are now ranked No. 1 in the world weren't ranked in the top 50 last week."
Wu Yanyan smashed the women's 200 meters individual medley world record Friday, four days after Chen Yan shattered the 15-year-old world mark in the women's 400 meters individual medley. 
Neither swimmer made the final of either event at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics.
