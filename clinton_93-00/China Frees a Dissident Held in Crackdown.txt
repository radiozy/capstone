
A prominent Chinese dissident detained nearly two weeks ago was freed today but ordered not to leave his home without permission from the police, a human rights group said.
The dissident, Wang Donghai, returned to his home in Hangzhou, the city in eastern China where he was hauled away on July 10 for trying to form an opposition political party, according to the Information Center of Human Rights and Democratic Movement in China.
With his release, 4 of at least 15 people rounded up in a crackdown that began on July 10, a week after President Clinton wrapped up a nine-day tour of China, remain in custody.
The brunt of the crackdown has fallen on dissidents trying to form the opposition party, called the China Democracy Party. 
So far 12 people with ties to it have been detained.
