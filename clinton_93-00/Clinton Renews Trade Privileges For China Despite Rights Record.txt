
Acting on the recommendation of Secretary of State Warren Christopher, President Clinton renewed trade privileges for China today despite objections to its human rights record.
The decision to renew the trade privileges, known as most-favored-nation status, had been disclosed last week by the President's national security adviser, Anthony Lake.
Last year Mr. Clinton separated the trade issue from human rights, partly at the urging of the business sector.
Michael D. McCurry, the White House press secretary, said in a statement that the sole requirement for renewing China's trade privileges is a Presidential determination that such renewal "will substantially promote freedom of emigration in China."
He said Mr. Clinton would notify Congress that he has concluded that this is the case, and that "China continues to allow free emigration and open travel overseas, including travel for students."
But Mr. McCurry made clear that although the United States is renewing China's most-favored-nation status, "we find China's record on human rights unacceptable."
"China continues to deny its citizens freedom of speech, association and religion and fails to guarantee humane treatment of prisoners," Mr. McCurry said.

Dissident in House Arrest
BEIJING, June 2 (AP) -- A dissident accused of being the planner of the 1989 pro-democracy protests in Tiananmen Square has been placed under virtual house arrest by police, a human rights group said today.
The police have told the dissident, Chen Ziming, not to leave his home, even for short walks in his yard, the group Human Rights in China reported. 
Mr. Chen was released from prison last year on medical parole after serving part of a 13-year sentence.
The Chinese authorities are trying to prevent a resurgence of campaigning for democracy in advance of the sixth anniversary on Sunday of the military crackdown on protesters in Tiananmen Square.
