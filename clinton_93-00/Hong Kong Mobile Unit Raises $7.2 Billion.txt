
China Mobile (Hong Kong) Ltd. has raised $7.2 billion selling debt and equity to help pay for new wireless networks, in the biggest sale of shares to overseas investors by a state-owned Chinese company.
The company, the largest mobile-phone carrier in China, raised $6.6 billion selling stock and $600 million from bonds that are convertible into stock, according to Goldman, Sachs, one of the investment banks involved in the transactions. 
The shares were sold at a price of 48 Hong Kong dollars, or $6.15, a 4.5 percent discount to the closing price here today.
China Mobile needs the money to finance part of the cost of its $34 billion purchase of seven mobile networks from its state-run parent, China Mobile Communications.
David Donnelly, who oversees a $27 million hedge fund at Gordon House Asset Management, said China Mobile did not face the high costs that are dampening the growth ambitions of European cellular telephone companies. 
Nor has China Mobile been deterred by the market sell-off that led global phone companies, like Verizon Wireless, to shelve their own plans to issue stock.
The mainland acquisitions will increase China Mobile's customers to 39.4 million from a recent 24 million.
