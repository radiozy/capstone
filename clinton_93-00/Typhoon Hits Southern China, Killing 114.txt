
A typhoon in southern China has killed at least 114 people, left 110 missing and destroyed more than 200,000 houses, Chinese officials said today.
Typhoon Sally hit the province of Guangdong on Monday, local officials said. 
Chinese Army troops were dispatched to Zhanjiang, where nearly all the trees in the city were uprooted by the winds.
Economic losses in the two hardest-hit cities, Zhanjiang and Maoming, were estimated at $1.5 billion.
Fierce winds caused more than 200,000 houses to collapse in Zhanjiang, an official said.
About 700 fishing boats were damaged around Zhanjiang alone, officials said, adding that more than 60 sank around Maoming.
A warehouse in Maoming for a Chinese automobile maker, Three Stars, collapsed, and a furniture market in Maoming covering an area of 430,000 square feet also was destroyed.
Rail and highway traffic have been suspended, and water and electricity have been cut off in the region.
The storm moved into Vietnam but weakened as it hit the country's northern provinces, officials said.
