
Hong Kong port companies have broken a deadlock over expanding the British colony's container port and will present their plan to the next meeting of a Chinese-British panel, a Hong Kong Government official said today.
Hutchison Whampoa Ltd., Jardine Matheson Holdings Ltd. and other port operators have agreed on a way to assign berths in Container Terminal 9, Donald Tsang, Hong Kong's Financial Secretary, said. 
Construction has been delayed since 1992 because of objections from China and infighting among the companies.
"The consortium has come to an agreement on how various berths of C.T. 
9 should be best utilized," Mr. Tsang said.
Hong Kong hopes to complete the plan at the next meeting of the Joint Liaison Group, a Chinese-British advisory panel on Hong Kong's return to Chinese rule, Mr. Tsang said. 
The group plans to hold a three-day meeting next week.
A deal to reallocate berths in C.T. 
9 and proceed with construction would end the dispute that has become one of the most visible symbols of China's ability to meddle in Hong Kong's business affairs.
China initially objected to C.T. 
9 because the British-controlled Jardine supported democratic reforms in Hong Kong. 
Jardine's founders helped wrest Hong Kong from China in the 1800's.
