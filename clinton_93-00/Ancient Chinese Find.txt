
Archeologists have found remains of a sacrificial site near the tomb of China's first emperor and said the discovery was as significant as the famed terracotta warriors who guard it, the New China News Agency said today.
Offerings of food were placed four times a day and a grand rite held once a month at the sacrificial building that is part of the sprawling mausoleum of Qin Shi Huangdi, historical records showed.
The emperor, credited with unifying China in 221 B.C., ruled with an iron fist until his death 11 years later. 
China has said it lacks the resources to excavate his tomb, which according to legend is filled with booby traps to deter robbers.
