
China's police have freed a dissident, Wang Youcai, after questioning him and warning him to drop a plan to register a political party, a Hong Kong-based rights group said today.
Last Thursday Mr. Wang and two other dissidents in the eastern city of Hangzhou tried to register a political party to challenge Communist rule. 
That was the day President Clinton arrived in China for a nine-day visit.
The police summoned Mr. Wang for questioning Monday evening and released him six hours later, said the rights group, the Information Center of Human Rights and Democratic Movement in China.
Security officials also warned him that they would take further action if he did not drop his plan to register the group, the Chinese Democratic Party, the center said.
But Mr. Wang told the police that he would try again to register the party with the Zhejiang Province department of civil affairs, the group said.
Mr. Wang, a former student leader, was jailed for three years for his role in the Tiananmen Square demonstrations for democracy, which were brutally crushed by the army in Beijing on June 3 and 4, 1989.
Mr. Clinton's visit, the first by an American President since the shooting in Tiananmen, has been clouded by a public debate over the detention of several dissidents in the cities he was to visit.
Mr. Wang's party application marked the first time Chinese dissidents have openly tried to gain Government approval for an opposition party.
