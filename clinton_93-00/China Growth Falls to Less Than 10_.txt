
China's economic growth slowed during 1995's first nine months and losses at the nation's companies widened, the State Statistics Bureau said today, but the Government was not expected to ease its tight grip on credit.
Gross domestic product -- the value of the nation's output of goods and services, excluding income from abroad -- grew 9.8 percent in the nine months from the corresponding period a year ago. 
That was the first time the growth rate has fallen below 10 percent since 1991.
The economy expanded by 11.8 percent last year and 10.2 percent in 1995's first half.
The slowing economic expansion caused inflation to moderate. 
Retail prices rose 16.6 percent in the nine months, below the rate of 18.5 percent in the year's first half.
At the nation's companies, a combination of tight credit, falling demand and rising prices led to total losses of 65.5 billion yuan, or $7.9 billion, during the nine months, up 28.9 percent from a year earlier.
