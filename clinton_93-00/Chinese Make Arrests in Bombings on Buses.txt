
The Chinese Government said today that several suspects had been arrested for bomb attacks last week that killed 9 people and wounded 74 in the far western region of Xinjiang, where Muslim separatists are waging a campaign for independence.
Those in custody were suspected of having made and planted the bombs that exploded on buses in the Xinjiang capital, Urumuqi, or of having sold detonators for the explosives, the Xinjiang Daily said.
The police were hunting for others still at large, the newspaper said. 
It said the bombings were the work of a "terrorist group."
Muslim separatists want to set up an independent state of "East Turkestan" in Xinjiang, home to many Turkic-speaking people, including those of the Uighur ethnic minority.
On Tuesday exiled Uighur nationalists in neighboring Kazakstan claimed responsibility for the bombings and for an attack on Monday.
Three bombs exploded within minutes in Urumuqi on Feb. 
25 in an attack that coincided with funeral rites in Beijing for Deng Xiaoping. 
A fourth bomb failed to explode. 
Most of the victims were said to be students on their way home from school.
