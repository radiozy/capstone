
China is planning a broad expansion of its stock market experiment this year, allowing each province to select companies for listing on the country's two stock exchanges, the Xinhua News Agency reported on Saturday.
But the Government has sharply reduced its target for the amount of stock to be issued, compared with the level of 1992.
"The securities administrative authorities will allow each of the provinces on the mainland to select one or two of their share-holding companies with a good business performance to be listed in the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges within this year," the agency quoted an official as saying.
Stock fever has been one of the most obvious and popular results of the market-style economic changes that the Communist Party has put into effect.
Companies across the country have issued stock, but in most cases they have been able to sell the certificates only to employees.
Xinhua quoted the unidentified official as saying, "In consideration of the progress in adopting the share-holding system, the demand of the growing stock market and other factors, the Government has initially set the figure of public issuance of stocks on the mainland at around 5 billion yuan this year."
This figure, equivalent to about $880 million, is far below the 13 billion yuan ($2.3 billion) floated last year on the country's two exchanges.
Last year, the number of shares listed in Shanghai and Shenzhen increased to 70, from fewer than 20 in 1991.
Currently, the total value of stocks on the two exchanges stands at more than 100 billion yuan ($18 billion). 
The volume for all of 1992 is expected to have reached 100 billion yuan, nearly 30 times the 1991 figure.
