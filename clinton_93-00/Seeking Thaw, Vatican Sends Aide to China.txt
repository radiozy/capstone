
Hoping for a thaw in its chilly ties with China, the Vatican today sent a senior diplomat, Roger Cardinal Etchegaray, to a sporting event that begins Saturday in Beijing. 
He is the highest ranking Roman Catholic Church official to visit China since the Communists took over in 1949.
[Cardinal Etchegaray arrived in Beijing early Friday, The Associated Press reported.]
The invitation came from the organizers of the National Athletic Games in China, the Vatican said. 
It added however that Cardinal Etchegaray, 70, a Frenchman and senior member of the Congregation for the Oriental Churches, would also meet with "Government personalities."
The visit evoked for many here China's invitation to a United States table tennis team that accompanied the thaw in Chinese-American relations leading to President Nixon's visit in 1972. 
It comes in the face of increasing signs that both Chinese and church leaders are seeking to renew a relationship that has been frozen for decades.
In a conciliatory gesture, the Vatican said that "from the start of his pontificate," Pope John Paul II had frequently expressed "esteem for the Chinese people" and "affection for all the Catholics of this great country."
