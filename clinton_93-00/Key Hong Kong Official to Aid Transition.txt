
Hong Kong's top civil servant agreed today to stay on after the British hand over the colony, a move that is expected to diminish fears about the future Chinese administration.
Anson Chan, deputy to the outgoing British Governor, Chris Patten, said she would continue in the Administration of Tung Chee-hwa, who has been chosen as chief executive.
Neither side spelled out what role Mrs. Chan would play in the new government, which is to take over next July 1, but she is expected to become Mr. Tung's deputy.
Mrs. Chan, 56, is a staunch defender of Hong Kong's civil liberties and legal system. 
She joined the civil service more than 30 years ago, working her way up to become the first ethnic Chinese -- and first woman -- to serve as chief secretary.
In that job, Ms. Chan heads the civil service, and her appointment is expected to ease the fears of senior civil servants that their loyalties may be tested when a provisional legislature set up by China starts discussing laws. 
The colonial government opposes the provisional legislature and has ordered civil servants not to cooperate with it.
The 190,000-strong civil service is the backbone of Hong Kong's efficient business environment, and its smooth transfer to Chinese sovereignty is seen as critical to preserving Hong Kong's powerful economy.
Mr. Tung's acceptance of Mrs. Chan is expected to be seen as a show of open-mindedness.
"He understands the difficulty we are now facing," said Peter Wong, head of the Chinese Civil Servants Association. 
"We are caught in the middle."
