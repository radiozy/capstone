
Authorities in China banned an officially sponsored gathering of poets and arrested three organizers after discovering that dissident writers had been invited, a human rights group said today.
Government officials feared that the forum would demand an easing of Communist Party controls over literature, according to the Information Center for Human Rights and Democracy, which is based in Hong Kong.
The group said about 200 poets had been expected at the conference, originally planned for Nov. 
6-11 at a hotel in Beihai, in Guangxi Province.
Discussion had been expected to focus on developments in modern Chinese poetry and the formation of a "poet's village" in Beihai, the group said.
The gathering was co-sponsored by the Beihai branch of the Communist Youth League and a Beihai poetry review, the center said.
On Nov. 
4, police arrested two poets from Beihai, Wei Manzeng and Jiang Nan, and Wang Changhuai, from Hunan Province, for planning the "illegal gathering," the Information Center said. 
They are being held in Beihai's jail, it added.
