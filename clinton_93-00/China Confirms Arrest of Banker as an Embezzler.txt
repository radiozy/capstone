
China confirmed today that Jin Deqin, former vice chairman of the China International Trust and Investment Corporation, had been arrested for embezzlement, nearly a year after his detention.
The official New China News Agency quoted a prosecutors' statement as saying that Mr. Jin had used his post to "embezzle tens of million of yuan in an abominable crime and grave plot."
"Jin Deqin is a senior cadre with more than 60 years as a Communist Party member who ignored national law and government and party discipline to commit the odious crime of large-scale embezzlement, causing grave economic damage and an extremely bad political impact," the statement said.
The Chinese Cabinet and Communist Party stripped Mr. Jin of all official and party posts, the New China News Agency said in the first confirmation of the banker's arrest since Hong Kong Kong news media reported his detention on April 28, 1998.
The report gave no more details on the charges against Mr. Jin, who resigned as honorary chairman of the Ka Wah Bank of Hong Kong, which is controlled by China International Trust and Investment, known as Citic, on April 15, 1998.
The Hong Kong reports said last year that Mr. Jin had been detained for misusing Ka Wah Bank funds, including making an $8 million loan to his son. 
The arrest makes it highly likely that he will go to trial and face charges that could lead to a long prison sentence. 
China has on occasion executed officials for economic crimes.
China International Trust and Investment reports directly to the Cabinet and is one of the biggest investors in the country. 
It owns Chinese roads, bridges and power plants and is a significant investor in Hong Kong as well, with stakes in such businesses as Cathay Pacific Airways and the China Light and Power Company.
