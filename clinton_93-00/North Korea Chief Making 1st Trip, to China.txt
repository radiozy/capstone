
The North Korean leader, Kim Jong Il, is expected to visit China in September on his first official trip out of the isolated Communist state, South Korean television reported today.
Mr. Kim, 57, who was formally made North Korea's leader in September but has not assumed the title of President, will meet President Jiang Zemin and other top Chinese Government officials during the trip, MBC television said.
"This official visit marks a watershed in relations between Beijing and North Korea which have been rather cool for the last few years," the station said, quoting South Korean officials.
The visit will be the first by a North Korean leader since Mr. Kim's father, the late President Kim Il-Sung, visited China in 1991.
Kim Jong Il -- who is believed to have left North Korea only twice in his life -- traveled in his father's delegation to Beijing in 1983 in an unoffical capacity.
There has reportedly been immense pressure from China for a visit. 
North Korea's economy and infrastructure are crumbling amid a devastating famine.
