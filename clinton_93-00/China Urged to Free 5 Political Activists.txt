
A human rights group based in Hong Kong said today that 100 Chinese dissidents had called on Chinese leaders to free five activists who were detained after trying to register an opposition political party.
In a letter addressed to President Jiang Zemin and Prime Minister Zhu Rongji, the dissidents expressed outrage that the authorities had taken 10 people into custody for expressing political views. 
The Communist Party bans the formation of political groups that it has not approved.
Five of the 10 activists have been released in recent days, said the human rights group, the Information Center of Human Rights and Democratic Movement in China. 
But five remain in custody in Zhejiang Province, four of them in the city of Hangzhou and one in Wenzhou.
"We call on the central Government to rectify the violation of human rights by the Zhejiang police and to release the five men," said the letter, which carried 79 signatures and claimed the support of another 21 unidentified sympathizers.
Chinese authorities frequently detain people they consider politically suspect, often releasing them after a few days with a stern warning, but sometimes holding them far longer. 
Whether any of the five detainees in Zhejiang will face charges or a prison sentence remained unclear.
