
The Dalai Lama warned today that pressures from Tibetans who are tiring of his efforts to negotiate with China for broader rights for Tibet may force him instead to seek international backing for an independent Tibet.
In a statement to mark the 35th anniversary of the Tibetan uprising against the Chinese occupiers in 1959, the exiled spiritual leader said he was reassessing his approach to the Chinese Government. 
His plan envisions extensive autonomy within China, including the right of Tibetans to choose their own government, with China retaining control of foreign relations and defense.
In New Delhi, a few hundred Tibetans, some hoisting banners calling for independence, protested Chinese rule. 
Among them were student leaders who have been increasingly critical of the Dalai Lama, faulting his commitment to nonviolence. 
Some militants have advocated a guerrilla war.
Although his statement marked a break with the conciliatory tone that has marked his pronouncements in the past, the Dalai Lama stopped short of embracing the policies urged on him by militants.
"I firmly believe that the day is close when our beloved land of snows will no longer be politically subjugated, culturally ravaged and economically and environmentally exploited and devastated," he said. 
"However, it is important that our struggle must be based on nonviolence."
