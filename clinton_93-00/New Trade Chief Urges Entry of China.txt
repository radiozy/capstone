
The new chief the World Trade Organization, Mike Moore, gave strong backing today to China's joining the global forum, declaring that "China ought to be member of the W.T.O."
Mr. Moore, endorsing Chinese admission, said the question "is not if, but the question is when and how."
Speaking to reporters at the group's headquarters on Lake Geneva, he added: "Every member of the W.T.O., and outside, wants China to sit at the table as she should. 
China is a superpower."
Clinton Administration officials said today that negotiators would begin talks next week on the bid by Beijing to join the World Trade Organization, resuming a process that was derailed in May by the accidental bombing of the Chinese Embassy in Belgrade, Yugoslavia.
Mr. Moore has indicated that he would like to see China admitted in time for the late November ministerial meeting of the trade organization in Seattle, but that may be difficult in light of the ups and downs of China's long-running admission effort.
One chance to close a deal could come when President Clinton and President Jiang Zemin of China meet at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation talks this month in Auckland, New Zealand.
China was among the 23 original signers in 1948 of the World Trade Organization's predecessor body, the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade. 
Its membership lapsed after the Communist revolution the following year.
