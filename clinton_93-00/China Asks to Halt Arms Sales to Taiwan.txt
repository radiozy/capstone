
China's foreign minister today blamed the United States for creating tensions between Taiwan and China and demanded that it halt all weapons sales to Taiwan.
American arms and other support for the island have "inflated the arrogance of the separatist forces in Taiwan," the foreign minister, Tang Jiaxuan, said at a news conference. 
"Therefore, the United States bears unshakable responsibility for the tension in the Taiwan Strait."
China considers Taiwan to be a region of the motherland, separated 51 years ago by civil war. 
"If the United States had not sent in the Seventh Fleet in the 1950's, then the question of Taiwan would have long since been resolved," Mr. Tang said.
The United States officially agrees with the mainland position that there is only "one China" and does not give diplomatic recognition to Taiwan. 
But it maintains close informal ties with the island and is pledged by law to help Taiwan defend itself against attack. 
The Chinese are worried by Taiwan's increasingly separate identity and have warned that not only a declaration of independence, but also indefinite stalling of reunification talks could lead to war.
In Hong Kong today on an Asian tour, Defense Secretary William S. Cohen told reporters, "We will continue to insist that the Taiwanese pursue negotiations peacefully and not seek independence, which we do not support."
