
Opening the annual session of the National People's Congress today, Prime Minister Li Peng told nearly 3,000 delegates that China would proceed cautiously after the death of Deng Xiaoping with heightened security against crime and ethnic instability, tight controls over the economy and more government intrusion into almost every sector of social and cultural life.
Mr. Li said the reform of China's state-owned industries would go forward, but the blueprint he mapped out was significantly more modest than the plan the party drafted and approved in November 1993, when Deng was still directing policy.
In his address today, Mr. Li sounded a familiar authoritarian tone when he said, "Safeguarding social and political stabilityI is indispensable to the modernization drive of our country."
He said a nationwide crackdown on "criminal activity" would continue and implied that this would include pressure on political dissidents and on religions to submit to state supervision or suffer persecution.
"We should unswervingly safeguard the unification of the motherland and ethnic unity and resolutely oppose any words or acts designed to split the country or damage ethnic unity," he said, adding that religious groups would have to "adapt themselves to socialist society."
