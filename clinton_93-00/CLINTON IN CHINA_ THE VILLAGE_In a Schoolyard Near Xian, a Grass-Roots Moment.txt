
The President led a schoolyard discussion today in Xiahe, about 30 miles east of Xian and adjacent to the famed archeological site of the terra-cotta warriors buried in the tomb of Emperor Qin Shihuang.
The President heard from a carefully selected group of citizens of Xiahe and nearby towns: a schoolteacher, a clinic worker, a restaurant owner and the manager of a factory that produces souvenir replicas of the terra cotta warriors.
The President listened intently, asking whether Government policies had led to growing prosperity in the village, educational levels and the challenges of running a small business in the booming economy.
Xiahe is a village of 400 people. 
One man told the President that his family's annual income had exploded to 30,000 yuan from 1,000 yuan before 1982.
Mr. Clinton also took questions. 
A restaurant owner asked him why he was holding such a discussion.
"For two reasons," the President answered. 
"First of all, I think it's important for people in positions like mine, in the United States and China, understand how people live at what we call the grass-roots level and understand how the policies we make affect the way people live and the lives of ordinary citizens."
He continued, "And secondly, because the American people are very interested in learning more about the Chinese people as a result of my trip. 
So when we do this there will be pictures and reports of this meeting in America so people just like you in America will have a feeling for what it's like to be a teacher or run a restaurant or work with old people."
Asked about Taiwan, Mr. Clinton said, "It's obvious there will be no change in our position one way or the other on this trip."
Next on the itinerary for the Clintons is a tour of Emperor Qin's tomb.
