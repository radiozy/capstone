
Two Chinese dissidents rumored to be Nobel Peace Prize nominees, Wei Jingsheng and Wang Dan, live in exile beyond the reach of the Chinese police. 
But not their families.
Today, as the world awaited the announcement of the award for 1999, the police forced their way into the home of Mr. Wang's parents. 
Mr. Wang's mother, Wang Lingyun, said in a telephone interview that the police had tried to make her go to a police station. 
She was allowed to answer the phone, but could not make comments, because the police were listening in.
Later, Cheuk Kwan, a spokesman for Mr. Wang in Boston, said the police had returned and had taken Mrs. Wang away about a half-hour before the prize announcement. 
"He heard noises, his mother yelling, and suddenly there's no more sound," Mr. Kwan said.
New calls to the Wangs' house did not go through.
The police have long sought to prevent the family from communicating with foreign reporters, often detaining journalists as they approached the house.
Mr. Kwan said Mr. Wei's brother, Wei Yaotao, had also been detained, a report that could not be immediately confirmed.
Acting on speculation that Mr. Wang and Mr. Wei might win the prize, Chinese officials lobbied the Norwegian Government and the Nobel Committee, saying Mr. Wang and Mr. Wei were "common criminals" who had plotted to overthrow the Government.
Mr. Wei, 49, and Mr. Wang, 30, represent different generations of dissidents. 
Mr. Wei is a former soldier and electrician whose faith in the Communist order was shaken by Mao Zedong's ruinous radical policies. 
Mr. Wang, from a more peaceful era, was a student leader of the pro-democracy protests in 1989.
