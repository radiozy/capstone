
A consortium led by the Custom Coals Corporation of Pittsburgh and MRL Ltd. of Australia signed an agreement with the Government today to build a $888.6 million coal slurry pipeline in northern China.
The 500-mile-long underground pipeline will run from landlocked Shanxi Province to Shandong Province, on the coast.
The pipeline, expected to be completed in late 1997, will help relieve pressure on China's overburdened transportation system, said Peter Oei, chairman of China Strategic Holdings, the parent company of MRL.
The consortium, China Pipeline Holdings Ltd., will own 51 percent of the project, with the remainder to be held by the China Coal Construction and Development Corporation, a unit of the Ministry of Coal Industry.
China, the world's largest coal user, consumed about 1.2 billion tons in 1993. 
Since more than 80 percent of the coal burned in China is not cleaned of pollutants, air pollution has become a severe problem.
The pipeline will remove pollutants like sulfur from the coal.
