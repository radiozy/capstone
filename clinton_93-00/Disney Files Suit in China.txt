
The Walt Disney Company is seeking $77,000 in damages from three Chinese publishing houses accused of pirating Mickey Mouse, Peter Pan, Cinderella and Snow White, an official report said on Friday.
The copyright infringement case is the largest brought by a United States company in China this year, the English-language newspaper China Daily reported. 
Chinese businesses are widely accused of pirating copyrighted and patented material, and United States companies say such infringements cost them $800 million last year.
Disney is suing the publishing companies and accusing them of violating its copyrights in nine picture-story books that feature the four Disney characters, the newspaper reported. 
The publishers reportedly produced and marketed 300,000 copies of the books in 1991, 1993 and 1994.
Hearings began on Wednesday and more are scheduled before a verdict will be announced, the newspaper said. 
Disney is also seeking to stop the printing.
At the hearing, the lower court determined that there was evidence of copyright violation and agreed to hear a case.
According to the newspaper, the publishers say the books were published under an agreement with the Grand World Publishing Corporation, which is said to have signed a publishing contract with an unidentified British corporation based in Hong Kong. 
The newspaper did not say where Grand World Publishing was based.
