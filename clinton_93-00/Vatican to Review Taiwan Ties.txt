
A top Vatican official said today that the Holy See would reconsider its ties with Taiwan in order to secure better relations with China.
The comment, by the Vatican's Foreign Minister, Archbishop Jean-Louis Tauran, was timed to coincide with the visit to Italy of President Jiang Zemin of China. 
Mr. Jiang has no plans to visit the Vatican, and relations between the Roman Catholic Church and the Chinese Government are strained.
But the Pope, who plans to visit Asia this year to conclude a synod of Asian bishops, is eager to normalize relations with China and improve the rights of Catholics there. 
China has long insisted that the Vatican must cut ties to Taiwan as a condition for talks. 
China does not allow its Catholics to recognize the Pope's authority, and views the Vatican's desire to appoint the bishops as interference in China's internal affairs.
"We are aware that in order to normalize our relations with Beijing, we will have to modify the form with Taipei," Archbishop Tauran told the Italian daily Corriere della Sera, referring to the form of relations. 
"We are willing to negotiate."
It is not the first time that the Vatican has expressed its eagerness to open diplomatic relations with China; Pope John Paul II wrote a personal letter to Deng Xiaoping in 1983 asking for direct contacts.
China, however, does not appear ready to bend. 
Asked about the Archbishop's comment, a Chinese Government spokesman, Zhu Bangzao, said: "Words are not enough. 
You have to follow through with deeds."
There are signs of disagreement over the Vatican's China policy even within the church. 
Fides, the news agency of the missionary arm of the Vatican, published an editorial by the Rev. Bernardo Cervellera, director of Fides, in which he criticized China's demands that the Vatican break ties with Taiwan and highlighted persecution of Catholic clergy in China. 
Father Cervellera demanded that China free two Chinese Catholic Bishops who disappeared three years ago.
