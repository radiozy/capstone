
In a major step toward greater cooperation by China in rebuking North Korea at the United Nations, the Security Council expressed its "serious concern" today about the intrusion of a North Korean submarine into South Korean territory last month.
The statement, the first to address the general situation on the Korean peninsula since the end of the Korean war in 1953, also obliquely rejected North Korea's claims that the armistice agreement that ended the Korean War 43 years ago was no longer in force. 
Nearly every word of the statement was closely scrutinized by China before it was adopted.
China's willingness to agree to a Security Council statement, a decision ultimately made by the Chinese Foreign Minister, Qian Qichen, marked another small step away from its once-close ties to the North Koreans, diplomats said.
"It is important that the Security Council spoke with one voice, with China coming on board," the South Korean delegate, Park Soo Gil, said after the vote.
