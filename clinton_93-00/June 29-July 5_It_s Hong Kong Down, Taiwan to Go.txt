
No sooner had China taken possession of Hong Kong last week than President Jiang Zemin turned his attention to Taiwan, the "lost" province of China that Beijing's Communist Party leaders have wanted to reclaim ever since it became the exile home of Chiang Kai-shek and his Nationalist Government in 1949.
President Jiang told a huge crowd at the Worker's Stadium in Beijing that the successful recovery of Hong Kong "will set an example for a smooth return of Macau and for the final solution to the Taiwan question."
He called on Taiwan's leaders to abandon any notions of independence and to "take concrete steps" toward "the complete reunification of the country."
But Taiwan took a step the other way. 
The ruling party proposed to scrap Taiwan's provincial government -- which largely duplicates the central government and maintains the fiction that Taiwan is still a province of mainland China.
