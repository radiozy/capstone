
Counterfeiting of products is "out of control" in China and inadequate measures to stop it could complicate Beijing's efforts to join the World Trade Organization, a senior American trade official said.
Sanctions against China are not being considered, the official, Joseph S. Papovich, an assistant trade representative, said. 
He offered praise for a recently renewed nationwide crackdown on bogus products in China.
But he said Washington had become increasingly worried about trademark counterfeiting in China, and that Beijing had failed to take timely action. 
"It's gotten out of control now," Mr. Papovich told reporters after meetings with Chinese officials.
Beijing and Washington twice went to the brink of a trade war over intellectual property protection, in 1995 and in 1996, and then negotiated two agreements on enforcement. 
While China has closed down some copycat producers since then, fake brand-name goods ranging from pharmaceuticals to car parts and sports wear remain readily available.
