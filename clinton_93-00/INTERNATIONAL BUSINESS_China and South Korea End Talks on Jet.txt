
Talks between South Korea and China on a project to develop passenger jets collapsed today.
An official at the South Korean Trade Ministry said the negotiations in Beijing, which opened on Monday, had broken down over differences on where to base final production.
China demanded that the assembly line be in Shanghai, while South Korea pressed for split production between the two countries, he said. 
Beijing officials also tried to reduce South Korea's stake in the project.
The breakdown in the talks could signal the start of a competition between China and South Korea to become the first to build a midsized passenger jet. 
The two countries agreed in March 1994 to jointly develop a 100-seat passenger jet.
Samsung Aerospace, a unit of the giant Samsung industrial group, was to be the lead company in the South Korean consortium, which also included Daewoo Heavy Industries, the Korean Airlines Company, the Hyundai Aerospace Industrial Company and the Goldstar Precision Company.
