
China announced its third interest rate cut in 14 months today, hoping to stimulate the economy and achieve this year's growth target of 8 percent.
The People's Bank of China, the central bank, will reduce bank lending and deposit rates by half a point as of Monday, the official New China News Agency said.
The official interest rate for one- year loans will fall to 6.39 percent from 6.93 percent, while one-year deposit rates will drop to 3.78 percent from 4.77 percent, Agence France-Presse reported, citing an earlier report carried by state-run China Central Television.
The agency said the reduction was made possible by rate cuts in Western countries and would ease the debt repayment of state-owned enterprises by about $2.8 billion a year.
Despite the Asian financial crisis, the Chinese economy grew 7.2 percent in the first nine months of the year. 
The Government has created a big fiscal stimulus package, pouring money into investment projects that include building roads and bridges. 
But many economists have called for further rate cuts.
The latest cuts are intended to increase lending by commercial banks, said Zheng Haihang, an economics professor at Capital University of Economics and Foreign Trade.
On Friday, banks in Hong Kong announced a cut in the prime lending rate by a quarter point, effective Monday. 
The move follows a similar cut on Nov.
