
A United Nations investigator said today that China had agreed to allow him to examine allegations of torture in China.
The investigator, Nigel Rodley, said China had invited him to visit and that he planned a tour early next year. 
Mr. Rodley, a British law professor, began asking China four years ago to allow a visit.
In his annual report to the United Nations Human Rights Commission, Mr. Rodley said dozens of people had reportedly been tortured in China.
He cited Tibetan religious figures, including two nuns who he said had been "subjected to harsh interrogation and ill treatment," including beatings and solitary confinement.
Mr. Rodley said he would ask China to allow him to visit Tibet.
Human Rights Watch, the advocacy and monitoring organization, suggested that China's agreement to allow the trip was "part of a careful game of modulated concessions" intended to avert censure at the commission.
Because of China's recent crackdown on pro-democracy advocates, the United States plans to sponsor a resolution by the commission criticizing China's human rights policies.
