
This weekend is the last chance to see "Splendors of Imperial China: Treasures From the National Palace Museum, Taipei" at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, before it moves on to Chicago, San Francisco and Washington.
First-time visitors will find work so reticent that it may come as a shock to the city-jangled nervous system. 
Those returning for a second look are in for surprises of a different kind: since mid-April more than 50 light-sensitive paintings in the original installation have been replaced by comparably astonishing works.
The new arrivals include a visionary Sung hanging scroll of deer grazing among autumn trees, a Yuan-period portrait of the Empress Chabi (Mrs. Kublai Khan; her husband was on view the first time around) and Hsia Kuei's scroll "Streams and Mountains, Pure and Remote," which is, quite simply, among the world's greatest landscape paintings.
Any of these works exhibited alone would draw crowds, but here they are just team players in an enormous, exhaustive show. 
Most of the objects are small; each demands attention, and they are worth every minute spent with them.
 
The Metropolitan Museum of Art, on Fifth Avenue at 82d Street, is open from 9:30 A.M. to 8:45 P.M. today and tomorrow, and from 9:30 A.M. to 5:15 P.M. on Sunday. 
Suggested admission: $7; $3.50 for students.
