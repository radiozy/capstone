
China granted licenses to four foreign insurance companies today on the eve of Prime Minister Zhu Rongji's departure for a 14-day visit to the United States and Canada. 
He hopes to win a deal in Washington for China's entry to the World Trade Organization, and insurance and other financial services are a sticking point in the protracted negotiations.
The licenses were issued to the Chubb Group and the John Hancock Mutual Life Insurance Company, both of the United States. 
The other two were the Sun Life Assurance Company of Canada and Prudential P.L.C. of Britain, which is not related to the American company of the same name. 
They join nine other insurers doing business in China.
China requires all foreign life insurance companies to find Chinese partners before they can sell insurance here. 
Foreign companies are not allowed to hold a controlling stake and life insurance companies may sell only individual life insurance plans.
China also limits foreign insurance sales to the cities of Shanghai and Guangzhou. 
But industry executives say insurance regulators are prepared to open as many as 10 more cities for foreign insurers soon.
