
Kawasaki of Japan is prepared to license a Macao-Chinese joint venture called the Benteng Motorcycle Company to produce 300,000 of its motorcycles annually in South China by 1996, the official newspaper China Daily reported today.
Benteng will build a $138 million factory in the Zhuhai Special Economic Zone, in the booming southern province of Guangdong, to produce 100cc, 125cc and 250cc Kawasaki motorcycles, a Benteng official told the paper.
Honda, Yamaha, Suzuki of Japan, Kwang Yang Motor Company, Sanyang of Taiwan and the Chia Tai Group of Thailand have all already set up joint motorcycle ventures in China.
Despite China's huge population, there are only eight million motorcycles on Chinese roads and annual production is fewer than two million a year. 
Motorbikes are starting to cram roads in booming south China, however, where licensing restrictions on private motorbike ownership have gradually been eased.
Kawasaki signed a technical cooperation agreement with Benteng in July and a contract will be signed soon, Lu Zhongyin, the Benteng vice president, told the paper. 
The paper did not say who are Benteng's shareholders.
Kawasaki will initially supply most of the parts for the bikes, but after 1996 Benteng expects to be able to produce engines, the paper said.
