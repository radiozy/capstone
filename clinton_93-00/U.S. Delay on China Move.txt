
The decision by the United States to wait an additional 60 days to decide whether to brand China a copyright pirate was made in order to allow time for more negotiations, Treasury Secretary Lloyd Bentsen said today.
On Saturday, the United States trade representative, Mickey Kantor, said the piracy determination had been postponed for 60 days, until June 30. 
The President must submit to Congress each year a list of the worst violators of American copyrights on books, music, movies, computer software and other intellectual property rights.
Mr. Kantor said the delay was to avoid complicating President Clinton's decision on whether to downgrade China's access to United States markets. 
Mr. Clinton must decide by June 3 whether Beijing has improved its human rights performance enough to warrant extending its "most favored nation" trade privileges.
"I don't want the intellectual property issues becoming confused with the other questions of human rights and 'most favored nation' renewal," Mr. Kantor said. 
He added that China's leaders should not construe the postponement as a sign of weakness or lack of determination to follow through on complaints that American companies are losing as much as $800 million a year in pirated sales.
The Administration also delayed similar decisions on whether to cite India and Argentina for violating American intellectual property rights.
