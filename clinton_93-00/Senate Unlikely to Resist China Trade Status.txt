
Key Senators indicated today that despite a huge American trade deficit with China and concerns over Beijing's policies, there would be little resistance to the renewal for China this year of the trading status that the United States extends to most countries.
As Secretary of State Madeleine K. Albright and the American Trade Representative, Charlene Barshefsky, made their annual call for renewal of the trade status with China, members of the Senate Finance Committee said they either supported it or saw no point in opposing it.
President Clinton renewed the status on June 3, but lawmakers have 90 days in which they can overturn the decision, which grants Chinese exports the same tariffs allowed other countries.
The committee's Republican chairman, William V. Roth Jr., of Delaware, said that maintaining normal trade relations with China was "in America's economic and strategic interest and central to any responsible China policy."
