
China is declaring the success of a recent clampdown on copyright violations, after the United States pressed it to improve protection of foreign intellectual property rights.
The authorities seized about six million books and magazines that violated copyright laws or spread what the Government considered violence or pornography in the first half of 1994, the New China News Agency said today. 
It did not specify how many were copyright violations.
Some 200,000 pirated compact disks and 750,000 video and audio tapes were also seized, it said, with about 7,000 people arrested and 56 factories and shops closed.
In June, the United States trade representative, Mickey Kantor, said he had placed China on a watch list of countries suspected of tolerating intellectual property violations of American trade law.
China is subject to an inquiry that could lead to $800 million in retaliatory sanctions -- the estimated extent of United States losses -- if violations are verified.
