
Addressing a gathering of 10 Southeast Asian nations on the final day of a summit meeting, Prime Minister Zhu Rongji of China today pushed the prospect of a regional free-trade zone that would potentially include a market of 1.7 billion people.
Mr. Zhu said the closer economic links between China and the 10 Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation nations made it necessary for the region "to further facilitate the unfettered exchange" of goods, capital and information. 
He said it might be advisable "to explore the establishment of a free-trade relationship."
With China's membership in the World Trade Organization in sight after an agreement Beijing worked out with the United States, and with a regional free-trade area "by and large established in 2002, there is a good opportunity for us to enhance our cooperation," he said.
He suggested setting up a working group to study greater integration. 
China is not a member of the group involved in the free-trade area, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, or Asean, but Beijing is included, along with Japan and South Korea, in a "plus three" partnership.
Seeking to allay concerns that China would draw away foreign investors and eat into the group's export markets, Mr. Zhu called China's accession to the World Trade Organization a "win-win situation for all."
He made no mention of expanding Asean into a powerful East Asian political and trading bloc, but he did call for greater dialogue on both political and security matters.
