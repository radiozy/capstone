
''The Secret Life of Textiles: Animal Fibers'' at the Metropolitan Museum of Art delves deep into the history and beauty but also the often inscrutable facts of textiles. 
Its two dozen items range across several cultures and centuries, including Chinese textile fragments from the fourth century B.C., Indian and Russian shawls, men's hats from Tibet and Korea, and a 19th-century French Jacquard-weave portrait of George Washington. 
Building on nature's genius, these often labor-intensive artifacts use fibers from yaks, sheep, goats, horses, cows and silkworms. 
One of the few intact garments in this show is a lavish ceremonial robe from 19th-century China discreetly trimmed in cat fur. 
Especially enlightening are the works' labels, which parse the different fibers' morphologies and the techniques that transformed them, aided by greatly magnified photographs.

20; 212-535-7710, metmuseum.org.)
