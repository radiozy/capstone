
Plunging stocks have rattled investors on Monday, but Donald J. Trump sees them as one more reason he is needed in the Oval Office.
As the Dow Jones industrial average fell more than 1,000 points in early trading on Monday, the billionaire developer who is seeking the Republican presidential nomination said that such volatility was another sign that the fortunes of the United States are too closely tied to the rest of the world. 
The declines of U.S. stocks have followed a market correction in China, which has also put pressure on European markets.
As I have long stated, we are so tied in with China and Asia that their markets are now taking the U.S. market down. 
Get smart U.S.A.
- Donald J. Trump (@realDonaldTrump) August 24, 2015
Mr. Trump, who made most of his money in real estate rather than investing, argued that markets are crashing because the United States has planned poorly and allowed China and Asia to dictate the global agenda.
"This could get messy," he said.
Mr. Trump has said that bad trade deals with China and Mexico are to blame for a sluggish American economy and weak job creation. 
He has promised to make "great" deals with other countries to protect American workers and has threatened to raise taxes on imports to the United States to bolster domestic production.
His remarks are something of a change in tune, with Mr. Trump previously highlighting how the United States is constantly falling behind China as it ascends economically. 
In the Aug. 
7 Republican debate, he declared that the United States "is in big trouble."
He added: "We don't win anymore. 
We lose to China."
