
BEIJING (AP) -- China's inflation surged in February, adding to pressure on Beijing to prevent overheating and keep the country's economic recovery on track.
Consumer prices rose 2.7 percent in February from a year earlier, an increase from January's 1.5 percent rise, the National Bureau of Statistics reported on Thursday in Beijing.
The statistics bureau said the latest price increase was a result of bad winter weather that led to a 6 percent increase in food costs and said pressure should ease once spring harvests come in.
A spike in inflation over the last four months is forcing Beijing to divide its focus between growth and preventing overheating. 
Communist leaders are trying to do that without raising interest rates, a move that could slow growth and affect China's trading partners by reducing demand for foreign consumer goods and industrial materials.
''There are lots of signs that price pressure is building in the Chinese economy sooner than expected, and that's going to be a concern for the government,'' said Tom Orlik, an analyst in Beijing for Stone & McCarthy Research Associates.
Prime Minister Wen Jiabao said last week that Beijing hoped to hold overall consumer price rises to 3 percent this year.
''So far there is no overheating in the economy,'' a bureau spokesman, Sheng Laiyun, said at a news conference. 
''Although February C.P.I. rose faster than before, it is still mild to moderate.''
Still, the data showed February's wholesale inflation accelerating to 5.4 percent, up from 4.3 percent in January, which could lead to still higher retail prices.
Housing price inflation also accelerated in February, with costs in 70 Chinese cities rising by 10.7 percent from a year earlier, up from January's 9.5 percent rate, the government reported earlier.
''Inflation is expected to trend higher in the next several months before peaking around midyear,'' Jing Ulrich, J.P. 
Morgan's chairwoman for China equities, said in a report.
