
It looks as though Sotheby's and the art dealers who show up at the European Fine Art Fair every March in Maastricht, the Netherlands, are not such great pals after all.
At this year's fair it was announced that the show's organizers were in talks with Sotheby's about holding a high-end art and antiques fair in China. 
The new event, which would have been called Tefaf Beijing 2014, was to have been a collaboration between Maastricht and Sotheby's joint venture with China's state-owned Beijing Gehua Cultural Development Group. 
(Sotheby's was to have gotten a percentage of the fair's sales.) But on Thursday the fair put out a statement saying ''a high-end art fair, as presently envisaged, in Beijing is not viable at the current time.''
Many dealers did not like the idea of giving a percentage of their sales to an auction house. 
There was also concern that such a fair would be expensive and risky especially since much of the art and objects popular in Maastricht -- old master paintings, antiquities and fine antique furniture -- are not the kind of things that most Chinese collectors are interested in right now.
