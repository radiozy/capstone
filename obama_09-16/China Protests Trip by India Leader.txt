
A Chinese official said Tuesday that a recent visit by the Indian prime minister to a Himalayan territory in northeastern India that China claims as its own land has jeopardized China-India relations.
The official, Ma Zhaoxu, a spokesman for the Chinese Foreign Ministry, was criticizing a visit on Oct. 
3 to the state of Arunachal Pradesh by Manmohan Singh, the Indian prime minister.
''China expresses its strong dissatisfaction on the visit by the Indian leader to the disputed area in disregard of China's grave concerns,'' Mr. Ma said at a news conference on Tuesday.
China claims that a mountainous northern swath of Arunachal Pradesh, called Tawang, has historically been part of Tibet and so should belong to China, which invaded Tibet in 1951.
The Indian government and Tibetan leaders in exile say a self-governing Tibet signed a treaty with Britain in the early 20th century that ceded Tawang to British-ruled India.
China has sharpened its claims to Arunachal Pradesh this year and tried in the spring to block a loan from the Asian Development Bank to India, because India had planned for part of the loan to support flood control projects in the disputed territory.
