
Alibaba's runaway initial public offering has turned the spotlight back onto SoftBank's valuation quandary. 
Following the Chinese e-commerce company's successful New York listing, the Japanese conglomerate's 32 percent stake eclipses the value of its other businesses. 
The 5 percent drop in SoftBank's shares on Monday is a reminder that the investment is both blessing and burden.
SoftBank's $20 million investment in Alibaba in 2000 probably ranks as one of the greatest ever. 
Based on Alibaba's first-day closing share price of $93.89, the stake is valued at almost $75 billion. 
SoftBank could not sell at that price even if it wanted to. 
Even after applying a 20 percent discount, the shareholding is now probably worth about $60 billion.
The company run by Masayoshi Son has stakes in other large listed entities too. 
The most significant are Sprint, Yahoo Japan and the online games maker GungHo Online Entertainment. 
The market value of these shareholdings - all of which SoftBank controls - adds up to around $35 billion. 
Combined with Alibaba, SoftBank's four main publicly traded investments exceed the Japanese group's $92 billion market capitalization.
That suggests investors ascribe little or no value to SoftBank's profitable Japanese telecommunications business, says Jefferies. 
The brokerage firm also points out that Alibaba is just one of the 1,300-plus investments that SoftBank has in an array of Internet companies at various stages of growth. 
Some of these may also turn out to be gems.
The worry is that investors will be reluctant to give SoftBank credit for these investments as long as it maintains such a sprawling portfolio. 
There are few obvious synergies between Alibaba and the company's other operations. 
SoftBank previously offered investors indirect exposure to the Chinese e-commerce behemoth. 
Now they can now buy the shares directly.
Mr. Son has made it clear that he intends to maintain SoftBank's stake in the Chinese company. 
Unlike Yahoo, the Japanese company did not sell any shares in the I.P.O. 
But unless SoftBank can find a way to simplify its structure, however, its true value may remain clouded.
