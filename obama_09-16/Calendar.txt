
French gastronomy and culture will be celebrated at Le Taste of France Show on Pier 54 at West Street and 13th Street, from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Saturday and from 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. on Sunday. 
French chefs working in the United States will prepare their specialties for tasting, along with French wines and liquors. 
Tickets are $30 in advance from letasteoffrance.com, $40 at the door. 
An evening dining program on Sunday from 6:30 to 11 p.m. is $149.
Amateur bakers can bring pies for the annual Pie Social at Bubby's, 1 Main Street (Water Street), Dumbo, Brooklyn, on Sunday. 
Bakers, who pay $11.54 to benefit Public School 150 in TriBeCa and the High School for Food and Finance, must arrive at 11:30 a.m., pies in hand. 
The public can taste the pies from noon to 3 p.m., $27.37. 
Tickets: bubbys.com.
A Taste of the Seaport, to benefit programs at P.S. 
397 (Spruce Street School), will be held on Sunday from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m., Front Street from Fulton to Beekman Streets. 
It will include tastings from restaurants in the South Street Seaport area. 
Tickets good for five tastes are $32.04, or $104.49 for 20 tastes, in advance, or $35 for five tastes at the event: tasteoftheseaport.brownpapertickets.com
A paella cook-off, with paellas to taste on Monday from 6 to 9 p.m. at the Beekman Beer Garden at the South Street Seaport, 89 South Street (Fulton Street), will benefit Action Against Hunger and New York City College of Technology, which has a culinary program, $51.99 for one, $92.79 for two: eventbrite.com/event/4007384194.
Le Cirque, 151 East 58th Street, will host a dinner on Tuesday at 7 p.m. to benefit the Tibet Fund and to honor Lodi Gyaltsen Gyari, a Tibetan humanitarian. 
Eric Ripert of Le Bernardin will lead a team of chefs to prepare the dinner, and the evening will also include music by Tibetan performers. 
Reservations are $1,000 and up: (212) 213-5011.
Just Food, which sponsors community-supported agriculture programs, will hold a tasting benefit, with dozens of restaurants, artisanal food producers, wineries and breweries participating, on Oct. 
3 at 7 p.m. at the Altman Building, 135 West 18th Street. 
Tickets are $184.74, or $315.99 for admission at 6 p.m.: luel.eventbrite.com.
The Gohan Society, a nonprofit organization that promotes Japanese culture, will hold a tasting event in which Japanese and non-Japanese chefs will participate, on Oct. 
4 at the Brooklyn Brewery, 79 North 11th Street (Berry Street), Williamsburg. 
Tickets are $100 for members, $125 for nonmembers; 10 to 15 percent will benefit tsunami relief: (212) 385-3550, gohansociety.org.
Look to the Water
Oysters will be served aboard the yacht Manhattan for a harbor cruise Thursday from 6 to 7:30 p.m. or 8 to 9:30 p.m. The program also includes a talk about oysters and demonstrations of molecular gastronomy and Champagne-bottle sabering. 
The boat sails from Pier 62, Chelsea Piers at 23rd Street. 
Reservations are $132.87 a person from harboroystercruises.eventbrite.com.
Competitions for oyster shuckers and eaters, cooking demonstrations and a chance to sample 16 varieties will be Saturday at the Grand Central Oyster Bar and Restaurant in Grand Central Terminal from noon to 4 p.m. Admission is free; oysters and wine will be sold.
Chinese Celebration
A Chinese harvest festival, also called the moon festival, will be celebrated on Sunday with a 10-course banquet at Lotus Blue, 110 Reade Street (West Broadway), with seatings at 6:30 and 9 p.m. The price is $88, or $68 for a vegetarian version, with wine pairings $30 extra. 
Prices do not include tax and tip. 
Reservations: (212) 267-3777.
Sip and Taste
Cocktail experts from Brooklyn's bars and restaurants will convene Tuesday from 6 to 9 p.m. at the Green Building, 452 Union Street (Bond Street), in Carroll Gardens, to pour their signature drinks with nibbles from restaurants. 
Reservations, $47.12: bkshakenandstirred.eventbrite.com.
