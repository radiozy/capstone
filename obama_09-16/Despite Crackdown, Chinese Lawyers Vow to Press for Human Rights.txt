
"We're celebrating our second birthday!"
With those words, more than 270 lawyers this week marked the second anniversary of the China Human Rights Lawyers Group, a loose alliance of lawyers who joined together to defend rights advocates - and who now find themselves the targets.
"Braving the wind and rain, we will travel together," read the statement, signed by 276 lawyers across China, including Guizhou Province in the south, Shanxi Province in the northwest and Shanghai, referring to a series of detentions and interrogations of rights lawyers that began on July 9 and that has so far netted hundreds of people.

The lawyers said they would continue to work for legal rights in China, using a phrase from the Shi Jing, or Book of Odes, a collection of poetry from around 3,000 years ago, to express their feelings about the current situation:
"The wind and rain are dark, the cocks crow incessantly."
(风雨如晦,鸡鸣不已, or "Feng yu ru hui, ji ming bu yi.")
The crackdown on lawyers prompted Zeid Ra'ad al-Hussein, the United Nations high commissioner for human rights, to say in Geneva on Monday that he was "concerned about the detention and interrogation in recent months of more than 100 lawyers in China, in connection with their professional activities, and by the adoption of new laws with far-reaching implications for N.G.O.s."
The Chinese government on Tuesday released on bail two members of a nongovernmental economic think tank, in a surprise move that rights advocates said may be tied to President Xi Jinping's state visit to the United States next week.
In their statement, which circulated on social media, the lawyers wrote:
We declare: We will continue to devote ourselves to human rights and law in China, join in individual cases, strive to realize the part of the Constitution about "respecting and protecting human rights" and work diligently to defend human rights!

Here's the latest accounting of the situation facing China's rights lawyers, according to the   China Human Rights Lawyers Concern Group, based in Hong Kong.
As of Wednesday, at least 286 lawyers, legal staff, human rights advocates and their family members have been detained, arrested, held incommunicado, forbidden to leave the country, questioned, summoned or had their freedom restricted. 
Of these:
•Twenty-seven have been criminally detained or are under "residential surveillance" in a secret location.
•At least 10 of those detained have been charged with national security crimes, none of whom has been permitted to see a lawyer.
•Those in criminal detention have been held incommunicado for 46 to 69 days.
•Eighteen people have been forbidden to leave China, including six children of lawyers.
•The largest number of people caught up in the sweep - 60 - were in Beijing. 
An additional 45 were in the southern city of Guangzhou, 25 were in Jiangsu Province, 17 in Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region and 16 in Shanghai.
