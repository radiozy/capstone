
A 26-year-old Chinese cyclist is set to be the first person from his country to participate in the Tour de France when the sport's biggest race begins next week.
Team Giant-Shimano announced on Wednesday that Ji Cheng had made its list of nine riders expected to participate in the 21-stage, 2,272-mile race. 
The 101st edition of the race begins in Leeds, England, on July 5.
Mr. Ji has competed in professional cycling's two other grand tours, the Vuelta a España in 2012 and the Giro d'Italia in 2013.
Although China was once famous for its streets clogged with bicycles, it has lagged in competitive road cycling. 
The state sports system has focused on track cycling, which sees smaller fields competing for more medals at the Olympics. 
At the 2012 London Olympics, Chinese track cyclists won two silvers and a bronze.
Mr. Ji, from the northern city of Harbin, attended a Chinese sports school as a runner before moving to track cycling and then road cycling.
While most professional cyclists come from Europe, the Americas and Australia, the sport is making inroads in Africa and Asia. 
"Having Cheng in the team as the first Chinese rider ever to ride the Tour de France will be huge for him and his country, and we look forward to seeing the impact this has on the globalization of the sport," the Team Giant-Shimano coach, Rudi Kemna, said in a statement announcing the team selection.
Although Mr. Ji's inclusion in Giant-Shimano's Tour de France team marks a high point for Chinese cycling, his role in the race will be decidedly humble. 
In previous races he has been known for his tireless work chasing breakaways to help position his team's talented sprinters for wins on flat stages.
