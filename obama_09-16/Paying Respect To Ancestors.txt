
VALHALLA, N.Y. 
-- Three generations of the Cung family gathered at Kensico Cemetery in Westchester County last Sunday to place cans of guava juice, paper money, paper dress shoes and a paper copy of a Louis Vuitton purse before the headstone of Lien Sau Khau, the family matriarch. 
The offerings were later set aflame, sending them to Ms. Lien to use in the other world.
''It's sort of a sign that you respect your ancestors,'' explained Richard Cung of Brooklyn, 14, her grandson.
The festival of Qingming, a time for tending graves, dates back over a thousand years to the Tang dynasty and is recognized as a public holiday in China. 
This year it fell on Thursday, but many families chose to honor it at area cemeteries over the weekend.
Kensico has the hilly topography favored by the Chinese tradition -- hilltop burial sites have good feng shui -- which makes it particularly busy this time of year. 
Cemetery officials managed the crowds by making all 12 miles of roads one-way, bringing in portable toilets and about a dozen extra maintenance workers. 
They also prepared to douse incense embers at the end of the day.
''People come for Easter and put flowers down,'' Chester S. Day, the cemetery's president, said, ''but this is very, very big.''
On Sunday, Richard carefully wiped his grandmother's tombstone clean.
Nearby the Tam family children placed gifts of paper clothing near a grave marker while Tony Tam, 68, of Roslyn, N.Y., prepared a whole roast pig for the picnic they would share with the deceased.
Slightly down the hill, Yon K. Lai, 52, along with nearly 50 family members, stood in front of his grandparents' grave site, adorned with food and presents.
''Sometimes, some years they give us a dream,'' he said. 
''I had a dream last month that my grandmother wants fish. 
So we brought fish.''
Mr. Lai, who lives in Chinatown in Manhattan, added: ''I was going to get the Russ & Daughters fish. 
But, unfortunately, we came a little early.''
