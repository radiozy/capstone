
SHANGHAI -- Three days after an ugly brawl ended an exhibition basketball game between Georgetown and a Chinese professional team, the Hoyas played another game here on Sunday without incident.
The game was tinged with geopolitics. 
Georgetown is on a good-will tour of China, and the brawl took place on the second day of Vice President Joseph R. Biden Jr.'s first official visit to China.
The game's public setting seemed as if it might invite further trouble. 
It was held outdoors, on a court set up under a giant tent, with free admission as part of a promotional event called the Nike Festival of Sport.
But Georgetown looked poised in a 92-69 victory over the Liaoning Dinosaurs, who play in the professional Chinese Basketball League.
The game was sloppy in the early going, but the Hoyas' trapping defense and accurate outside shooting from its guards turned it into a rout late in the second half.
Georgetown Coach John Thompson III said he was pleased with how the team played and how his players kept their focus.
He also said he met with members of the Bayi Rockets, the team the Hoyas brawled with, on Saturday in Beijing.
''We got together the following morning in the airport and sat down and talked,'' Thompson said at a news conference, adding, ''We're going to move on; it's time to move on.''
The fight, captured on amateur video, was a full-court melee that broke out at the end of Thursday's exhibition game in Beijing, with a group of Bayi players chasing a Georgetown player down under the basket and then one Chinese player repeatedly hitting him.
The images stunned many in China, some of whom criticized the behavior of the host team, whose players are drawn from China's People's Liberation Army.
Some in the crowd threw water bottles and folding chairs at the Georgetown team after the fight.
The game ended in a 64-64 tie after Thompson pulled his players off the court.
