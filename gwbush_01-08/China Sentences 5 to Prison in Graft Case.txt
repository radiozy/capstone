
The son of a former Chinese governor and five others have been sentenced to up to 15 years in prison for corruption, state news media reported today.
Li Bo, the son of former Gov.
Li Jiating of Yunnan Province, was given the heaviest sentence by the Kunming Municipal Intermediate Court on Friday, newspapers reported. 
Li Bo was accused of collecting more than $1.8 million in bribes and kickbacks involving contracts for tobacco and gasoline beginning in 1996, The Beijing Morning Post said.
Sentenced with him was the former governor's mistress, Xu Fuying, who was found guilty of orchestrating corrupt business deals and pocketing hundreds of thousands of dollars from state banks, the newspapers said. 
She was sentenced to four years in prison.
A former county chief was given the second-heaviest sentence -- 10 years -- for corruption and embezzlement.
Such graft is believed to be widespread among Communist Party and government officials, executives and heads of state banks and companies, and often involves family members who take advantage of their parents' or spouse's positions.
Li Jiating was convicted of corruption, becoming among the highest officials to fall to a periodic crackdown intended to show government concern about corruption. 
Mr. Li was given a suspended death sentence earlier this year for taking $2.2 million in bribes, but he reportedly avoided execution by cooperating with investigators.
Yunnan, in the southwest, is one of China's least developed regions, although its tobacco crop and tourist industry are important sources of government revenue.
