
Secretary of State Colin L. Powell said today that he had not raised any individual cases of people detained in China in his meetings with Chinese leaders, but had spoken generally of improving human rights and the rule of law in China.
That was a sharp shift in tone from the diplomatic drive earlier in the week to secure the release of two Chinese citizens with permanent residency in the United States.
There are many other cases in which the State Department has been asked to help get people out of Chinese jails.
In particular, the family and lawyers for Liu Yaping, a seriously ill businessman detained in Inner Mongolia, were assured that Assistant Secretary of State James A. Kelly would ask about Mr. Liu if the secretary did not.
Mr. Liu, 48, is a Chinese citizen with United States residency. 
He was detained March 8 in Hohhot, capital of Inner Mongolia, in a business dispute and has reportedly suffered a brain aneurysm.
"Whatever level can effectively work is what we want," said Jerome Cohen, the New York-based lawyer who is handling Mr. Liu's case in the United States. 
"We have to have the larger questions in mind also, but not at the cost of the freedom of these people who have been subjected to abuse."
Mr. Cohen said Senator Joseph R. Biden Jr., Democrat of Delaware and chairman of the Foreign Relations Committee, was expected to lead a group to China in mid-August and that they had promised to follow up on Mr. Liu.
Without high-level intervention, his case may not get attention. 
A Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman, asked about Mr. Liu, said he had never heard of him.
