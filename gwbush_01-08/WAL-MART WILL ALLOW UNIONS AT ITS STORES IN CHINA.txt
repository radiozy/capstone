
Wal-Mart Stores, which is opposed to trade unions for its 1.2 million workers in the United States, will allow them for its employees in China, where restrictions on foreign retailers are expected to loosen. 
''Should associates request formation of a union, Wal-Mart China would respect their wishes and honor its obligation under China's Trade Union Law,'' the company said yesterday in a statement. 
Wal-Mart, based in Bentonville, Ark., is competing with Carrefour of France and Metro Group of Germany as China prepares to allow foreign retailers to open as many stores as they like, starting on Dec. 
11. Wal-Mart plans to open up to 15 stores in China next year, for a total of 58, Joe Hatfield, Wal-Mart's chief executive for Asia, said in a Nov. 
2 interview.
