
A Chinese herbal compound, gastrodine, used for centuries to treat dizziness and headaches, may help alleviate mild dementia in stroke victims, data from a preliminary study suggest.
The research was conducted at Beijing University and the Academy of Traditional Chinese Medicine, where a number of medicines derived from herbs are being tested.
The results were presented last week at an American Heart Association conference in Honolulu.
Seventy stroke patients were given a tea made with gastrodine granule, a standardized form of a compound derived from an Asian orchid. 
All of them suffered from mild dementia caused by problems with blood flow to the brain. 
A comparison group of 50 patients received a common medication consisting of almitrine and raubasine.
After three months, the two groups showed similar improvements in cognitive abilities. 
But members of the group receiving gastrodine showed greater improvement in performing tasks of daily living. 
The study said the compound was being considered for approval by China's food and drug agency.
But Dr. Edgar J. Kenton III, a professor of neurology at Thomas Jefferson University in Philadelphia, who had no connection with the study, cautioned about making treatment decisions on the basis of its findings. 
In a culture in which herbal medicines are common, the chance of a placebo effect's skewing the findings is high, he said.
