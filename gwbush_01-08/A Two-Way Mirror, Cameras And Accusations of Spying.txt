
An accusation of spying has stirred intrigue at the Women's World Cup.
The day before their match with China, Denmark team officials found two men with video cameras sitting behind a two-way mirror in the hotel conference room where the team was about to hold a strategy meeting.
''It's like a spy movie,'' the Danish team press officer Pia Schou Nielsen said. 
She said the men were Chinese, although Denmark Coach Kenneth Heiner-Moller told reporters he did not know what nationality the men were.
Heiner-Moller refused to shake hands with China's coach, Marika Domanski-Lyfors, after Denmark lost to China by 3-2 in Group D on Wednesday night.
''I will not comment on that,'' he said when asked why he refused to greet Domanski-Lyfors after the game. 
''I'm quite sure we have made an official complaint to FIFA about things that happened prior to the match.''
On Thursday, Heiner-Moller said, ''I can assure you there are no accusations whatsoever against China or Marika,'' adding he refused to shake hands with Domanski-Lyfors because he was frustrated after a tough loss.
Domanski-Lyfors, who was Sweden's coach before joining the Chinese team in April, shrugged off the intrigue. 
''I don't know what secrets Denmark would have, because I know the whole team from before; we played them many, many times with Sweden,'' she said.
It was not clear who the men with the video cameras were, but they were taken away by the police, Danish officials said. 
FIFA, soccer's world governing body, said in a statement Thursday that it and the Danish team decided not to pursue the case.
