
Vice President Hu Jintao of China met with President Vladimir V. Putin today on the first leg of a five-nation tour where he is to be introduced to foreign leaders.
Mr.
Hu went directly from the airport to the Kremlin for a meeting with Mr. Putin on events in Afghanistan and on the strategic partnership between Russia and China.
In talks lasting 90 minutes, they discussed the international antiterrorism campaign, Afghanistan and the development of a "post-Taliban period," according to Sergei Prikhodko, President Putin's deputy chief of staff.
With stops in Russia, Britain, France, Spain and Germany, the trip is regarded as an effort to raise Mr. Hu's international profile.
Mr. Hu, 59, is expected to be selected as leader of the Chinese Communist Party next year, replacing President Jiang Zemin. 
Mr. Jiang's term as president ends in 2003, and Mr. Hu is expected to receive that title.
Mr. Hu told reporters at the Kremlin that President Jiang was satisfied with his talks with Mr. Putin at the meeting of Asia-Pacific leaders in Shanghai last week.
