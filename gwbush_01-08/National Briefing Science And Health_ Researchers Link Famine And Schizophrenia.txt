
A study of a famine in China more than 40 years ago found that children born to severely malnourished women were more likely to develop schizophrenia. 
The research bolsters evidence that environmental factors can set off the illness.
Compared with children born before or after the 1959-61 famine, those born during it faced twice the risk of becoming schizophrenic later. 
The lead author, Dr. David St. Clair of the University of Aberdeen in Scotland, conducted the study with researchers from China. 
Their findings appear in the current issue of The Journal of the American Medical Association. 
The study supports the theory that schizophrenia is caused by a genetic predisposition influenced by environmental factors that disturb the developing fetal brain, in this case nutritional deficiencies.
