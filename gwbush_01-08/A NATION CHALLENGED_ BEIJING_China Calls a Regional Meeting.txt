
China, worried about its waning influence in Central Asia, said today that it would sponsor a meeting with foreign ministers of Russia and Central Asian countries next month to discuss Afghanistan's future and the war on terrorism.
The ad hoc ministerial meeting of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization -- which also includes Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Tajikistan -- is set for Jan. 
7 in Beijing, the Foreign Ministry spokeswoman, Zhang Qiyue, said.
The meeting will also address the struggle against religious extremism, separatism and terrorism in each of the nations, she said at a news conference without giving details.
The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation was established in June when Uzbekistan joined the Central Asian security group, previously known as the Shanghai Five.
Founded to broker border disputes between China and Central Asian nations that formerly belonged to the Soviet Union, the group has shifted its focus toward fighting Islamic militants in the region. 
It plans to establish a regional antiterrorist center in Kyrgyzstan.
China blames Muslim separatists from the Uighur ethnic group in its far western region of Xinjiang for a string of bombings and assassinations in the last decade and has linked them to targets in the war on terror.
In October, the Chinese National People's Congress ratified the Shanghai Convention on Combating Terrorism, Separatism and Extremism, concluded in June with the other members of the organization.
China is concerned about the heightened American military presence in Central Asia. 
Ms. Zhang said Chinese diplomats in Kabul had met with members of the new interim government, including its leader, Hamid Karzai, and were preparing to open the embassy there as soon as possible. 
China's mission in Kabul has been closed since 1993.
