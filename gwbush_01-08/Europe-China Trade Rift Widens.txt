
The European Union is considering taking China to the World Trade Organization for the first time over its refusal to scrap export restrictions on coke.
Limits placed on the volume of coke permitted for export and a sharp rise in the cost of export licenses for the fuel are having ''a severe effect'' on European steel producers, Arancha Gonzalez, a spokeswoman for the commission, said Thursday.
China produces half the world's coke -- the baked coal essential for steel making. 
Chinese exports account for a third of all the coke used in European blast furnaces, she said.
During meetings between European and Chinese trade officials in Brussels on Thursday, China agreed to release for export from 1.5 million to 2 million tons of additional coke to bring the price down.
But this offer falls far short of what the European Union is looking for, Ms. Gonzalez said, adding that the commission is now considering taking China to the World Trade Organization in Geneva to find a solution.
The talks came as Prime Minister Wen Jiabao met with European Commission officials in Brussels on Thursday.
His visit is the first by a Chinese prime minister to the commission. 
Mr. Wen and the commission president, Romano Prodi, emphasized the importance of the relationship between the two trading blocs.
Mr. Prodi said that trade flows between the two were ''the single biggest in the world.''
