
The Sichuan Changhong Electric Appliance Company, China's leading television maker, said on Wednesday that it had ended its relationship in April with its American distributor, Apex Digital Inc., a company Changhong says owes it $467.5 million.
Changhong warned on Tuesday that it was headed for a big loss in 2004 in part because of debts owed by Apex, which distributes Changhong products in stores like Wal-Mart and was once the top supplier of DVD players in the United States.
Changhong's shares dropped their 10 percent limit for a second day, wiping out 2 billion yuan ($241.7 million) in market capital in two days.
Police in the southwest province of Sichuan, where Changhong is based, declined to comment on Chinese media reports that David Ji, the Apex chairman and a co-founder, had been detained on suspicion of financial fraud.
A Changhong spokesman, Liu Haizong, said Mr. Ji's fate had nothing to do with the company.
''Changhong has been in constant touch with Apex to try to clear its accounts,'' Mr. Liu said by telephone. 
''Our network in the United States is already very wide, so our sales in North America will continue.''
An executive in Changhong's marketing department said the company had ended its ties with Apex, based in Ontario, Calif., in April.
Apex declined to comment on its relationship with Changhong, saying this business disagreement would be addressed through the appropriate channels.
The cause of Changhong's loss was made public only after pressure from the Shanghai stock exchange, and raised questions about accountability at Chinese companies.
Changhong says it is the world's second-largest maker of TV's after Samsung Electronics.
