
There's something about the romance of train travel that continues to give it an enduring appeal for many vacationers (well, except maybe for those people forced to take Amtrak). 
That's especially true when it comes to some of the world's most classic train routes.
Smithsonian Journeys is now offering three train expeditions complete with upscale meals, fine wine, deluxe sleeping cabins, expert lecturers and breathtaking scenery. 
Board the Trans-Siberian Express this September for 11 nights and take the world's longest railroad journey (6,135 miles) -- from the Sea of Japan to the Kremlin, crossing the Ural Mountains, the natural border between Asia and Europe, before ending up in Red Square -- all under the leadership of a Fulbright Scholar. 
Rates start at $8,695 a person based on double occupancy.
In October, a trip through the Middle Kingdom of China on the newly refurbished China Orient Express is being offered under the guidance of an art historian. 
The weeklong train journey links Hong Kong to the Himalayas and includes a cruise down the Li River; a visit to the Stone Forest, known as the first wonder of the world; and a stop in Inner Mongolia for dinner at the home of a local family. 
The trip ends with three days in Beijing. 
Rates are $8,790 a person based on double occupancy.
Those wishing to explore South Africa with two professors emeritus from the University of Cape Town may want to take a 16-day journey in April on the Rovos Rail. 
The expedition covers Victoria Falls in Zambia, above; a safari and a river cruise in Botswana; and a stop at the prison where Nelson Mandela was incarcerated. 
Rates begin at $10,450 a person based on double occupancy.
For information, call 877-338-8687 or visit www.smithsonianjourneys.org.
