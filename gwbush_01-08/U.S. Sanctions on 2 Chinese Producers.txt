
The United States will impose duties up to 215 percent on imports of a type of coke from China that is used by American iron foundries to make products ranging from car and truck engine blocks to cast-iron sewer pipes.
The International Trade Commission, a United States agency that considers the impact of imports of domestic companies, found that the Chinese product is hurting producers including Erie Coke of Erie, Pa., and ABC Coke of Birmingham, Ala.
The Commerce Department has also found that Chinese companies, including Sinochem International and the Minmetals Development Company, were selling in this country below their cost of production.
China now has 40 American antidumping orders in place against it, on products ranging from paper clips to crawfish. 
Once it joins the World Trade Organization, expected later this year, the rules of global trade will permit broader actions by the United States if it thinks that the Chinese are in violation.
