
Floods caused by torrential rain have turned vast tracts of farmland in south China into muddy lakes, razed mountain villages and destroyed more than 36,000 homes, killing perhaps hundreds of people.
State radio said at least 88 people were killed and 73 left missing by floods and mudslides that were caused by three days of rain in the provinces of Hunan, Sichuan and Guizhou, and downpours in the northwestern Xinjiang region.
''It looks like people's houses were just swept away by a broom,'' Zhou Deqing, manager of a mine in the town of Taizhimiao in Hunan Province, said in a telephone interview on Friday.
''Houses along the riverbanks have been totally destroyed,'' Mr. Zhou said. 
''Even the foundations of some homes have been washed away and trees near the river have been uprooted.''
In the Xinshao region, the worst hit, mountain torrents killed 32 people and left 45 missing, state television said Friday, showing pictures of flattened wooden buildings and villagers sorting through the debris.
The flooding is also believed to have wiped out mountain villages yet to be reached by rescuers, China Central Television said.
''We are still searching for people missing, and many troops from the armed police are involved,'' an official from Taizhimiao said in a telephone interview.
State television reported that the authorities were shipping supplies to affected villages.
