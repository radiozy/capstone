
Love is a universal language, at least according to the cockeyed optimists. 
But what about ambivalence?
Is paralyzing romantic two-mindedness -- the kind Stephen Sondheim hymned as the ''God-why-don't-you-love-me-oh-you-do-I'll-see-you-later blues'' -- the same in every culture?
Anthropologists in search of answers may want to check out the latest incarnation of the long-running Off Broadway musical ''I LOVE YOU, YOU'RE PERFECT, NOW CHANGE'' at the Westside Theater. 
Starting Wednesday this popular 11-year-old date show about the mixed blessings of singleness and marriage will be interpreted by a Mandarin-speaking Chinese cast, in alternating performances with the existing English-language production. 
Like the original ''I Love You,'' this imported version, which recently ended a run in Shanghai, is directed by Joel Bishoff, but it has been jiggered to reflect daily life in metropolitan China. 
It will be performed with English supertitles and features Lin Yilun, a pop star and television host known as ''the Latin prince of China.''
Through June 3, 407 West 43rd Street, Clinton, (212) 239-6200, telecharge.com; $66 to $100 for the Mandarin show, $75 for the English-speaking cast. 
A revival of ''BEYOND THERAPY,'' the 1981 CHRISTOPHER DURANG play about neurotics in uncertain pursuit of commitment, makes the case for the theory that semi-love (or is it semi-hate?) goes beyond words. 
Or spoken words anyway. 
This take on Mr. Durang's story of a man, a woman, his boyfriend and their crazier-than-thou therapists is presented in American Sign Language by the New York Deaf Theater. 
Garrett Zuercher directs. 
Two voice actors will be on hand to interpret for nondeaf audience members. 
Through May 20, Actors Theater Workshop, 145 West 28th Street, Manhattan, third floor, (212) 868-4444, smarttix.com; $25.
