
Jessica Smith, a textile artist and designer in Philadelphia, above right, has taken the toile de Jouy form and made it modern, witty and political. 
Her hand-printed toile wallpapers offer lighthearted social commentary.
''Eighteenth-century French toile depicted pastoral scenes with classical ruins, ladies in frilly dresses on swings,'' Ms. Smith said. 
''I wanted to make my own pastoral countryside, and the countryside of America is the suburbs.''
Trash Day, top right and detail top left, is a homage to the day when suburbanites put the trash on the street for collection. 
Spying on China, above center, shows an American spy plane over a Chinese landscape, a reference to a collision between such a plane and a Chinese jet in 2001 off the coast of Hainan Island. 
Cars Go Beep, above left, has delicately rendered Hummers on the New Jersey Turnpike.
Ms. Smith is just one artist revamping wallpaper design. 
In ''On the Wall: Wallpaper by Contemporary Artists,'' a 2003 show at the Rhode Island School of Design in Providence, the curator, Judith Tannenbaum, exhibited Renee Green's red-and-white toile, which had a floral motif interspersed with political images. 
''The artists are playing with the tradition of wallpaper and are making it more relevant,'' Ms. Tannenbaum said.
