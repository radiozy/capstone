
This week brought some good news and bad news for Bank of America's beleaguered investment banking unit.
First, the good news.
So far this year, the unit is the only major investment bank to post an increase in revenue over the comparable period in 2007, according to Dealogic. 
For the year to date, it ranks second by revenue among the big investment banks, trailing only Citigroup but beating Goldman Sachs and Merrill Lynch.
But now, the bad news. 
About 46 percent of the year-to-date revenue came from the commission for managing a $6.9 billion offering of convertible preferred stock that priced Jan. 
24.
And who sold that convertible stock?
Bank of America, the investment bank's parent company. 
The sale was part of a plan to bolster Bank of America's capital ratios after its $21 billion acquisition of the LaSalle Bank and big mortgage-related losses. 
With Bank of America itself footing the bill, that commission doesn't seem like such a coup.

Shooting Rabbits
Sovereign wealth funds were the buzz of Capitol Hill on Thursday. 
A bipartisan committee heard testimony from a range of voices, from Democratic lawmakers to security analysts to regulators.
At issue were the potential risks of having government-run funds in China and other foreign countries make big investments in American businesses. 
What came across was, at best, skepticism about the funds' motives and capacity for harm.
Marcy Kaptur, a Democratic representative from Ohio, quoted the head of the China Investment Corporation in her prepared remarks: ''He said, 'If there is a big fat rabbit, we will also shoot it. 
Some people will say we were shot by Morgan Stanley.
