
The price of copper in New York plunged the most in 14 years yesterday, part of a global decline in base metals, on concern that a slowing economy in China would erode the demand that led to a 15-year high in prices last week.
Copper use in China, the world's biggest buyer, fell 21 percent in July from a year earlier, leading a worldwide decline of 3.3 percent, the International Copper Study Group, which is based in Lisbon, said yesterday. 
Nickel prices in London dropped the most in 16 years, and aluminum, zinc, lead and tin tumbled.
''At some point, and who knows when, there's going to be a hiccup in China, and the huge consumption everybody's expecting for the next six months or a year is not going to be so huge,'' said Ron Fisher, a basic-materials analyst in New York at Stein Roe Investment Counsel, which sold its copper holdings in March. 
''The China factor is very big in the metals.''
Copper futures for December delivery fell 16 cents, or 11 percent, to $1.288 a pound on the Comex division of the New York Mercantile Exchange, the biggest drop since April 1990. 
Prices have still gained 45 percent in the last year. 
A futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell a commodity at a specific price and date.
The price decline accelerated as the December contract fell below 25-day and 30-day moving averages. 
As of Oct. 
5, hedge funds and other large speculators had bought 28,247 more copper futures than they had sold, the most since Feb. 
17, a report on Friday by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission said.
''Much of the move has been prompted by fund liquidation,'' said John Kemp, an economist at Sempra Metals in London.
Copper for delivery in three months declined $334, or 11 percent, to $2,760 a metric ton ($1.25 a pound) on the London Metal Exchange, the world's largest metals market. 
Nickel plunged 17 percent, aluminum dropped 6.1 percent, lead fell 5.3 percent, tin retreated 6.3 percent and zinc tumbled 7 percent.
