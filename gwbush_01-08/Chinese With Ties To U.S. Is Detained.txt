
Another Chinese-born intellectual with permanent residency status in the United States has been detained by Chinese security forces, human rights groups and acquaintances of the man said today.
Qin Guangguang, a pharmaceutical company executive in Beijing who lived in the United States from 1989 to 1994, was detained at the Beijing airport in December on suspicion of giving state secrets to an overseas intelligence agency, the Information Center for Human Rights and Democracy said.
Mr. Qin was a well-connected newspaper editor in China until 1989, when he left the country and served as a visiting scholar at several American universities, including Stanford. 
He also lived in Flushing, N.Y., for several years.
It was not clear if his departure from China was related to the military crackdown on pro-democracy protesters at Tiananmen Square in 1989 -- an event that sent thousands of Chinese fleeing overseas. 
His father-in-law, Feng Yuanxin, was a prominent official in Sichuan Province at the time of the protests and is currently an official in Beijing.
While in the United States, Mr. Qin and his wife received green cards, and in 1994 he returned to China as a businessman. 
The police confiscated the passports and green cards of Mr. Qin's wife, Feng Li, and daughter around the time he was detained.
The American Embassy said it had not been asked to intervene in the case, and American officials said China would not notify them of such a detention because he was a green card holder, not a citizen.
