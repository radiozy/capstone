
One of China's largest government-owned companies has pleaded no contest to a criminal violation stemming from a 1995 shipment of machine tools from the McDonnell Douglas Corporation.
In a deal with prosecutors, TAL Industries Inc. waived sovereign immunity and was convicted of a felony charge of violating United States export laws, the Justice Department said on Friday.
TAL, owned by the government-owned China National Aero-Technology Import and Export Corporation, was sentenced to pay a $1 million criminal fine and to spend five years on corporate probation, the department said. 
The Chinese companies also agreed to pay $1.3 million to settle 24 other charges related to the machine-tool shipments.
The government has also indicted McDonnell Douglas, now a part of the Boeing Company, in connection with the machine tool exports. 
That case is pending before a federal judge, the department said.
This is the first time a government-owned Chinese company has waived its immunity and been convicted of a crime against the United States, the department said. 
The indictment stems from a case accusing the Chinese companies of conspiring to acquire equipment for military purposes.
Officials at McDonnell Douglas have denied the charges.
