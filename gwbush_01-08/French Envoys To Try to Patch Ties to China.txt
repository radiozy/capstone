
After a surge of anti-French protests in China, the French president, Nicolas Sarkozy, is sending three current and former officials there this week in an effort to calm the controversy surrounding the Olympic Games in Beijing.
Thousands of protesters rallied at outlets of the French supermarket chain Carrefour in China over the weekend, demonstrating against what they see as French support for pro-Tibet agitators.
France has become the main focus of the protests in China, particularly since the wide dissemination of images of a Chinese athlete in a wheelchair protecting the Olympic torch from protesters in Paris this month.
One of the three French representatives, Christian Poncelet, the president of the Senate, was headed for Shanghai on Monday, to be followed by a former prime minister, Jean-Pierre Raffarin, on Thursday and On Friday, it will be the turn of Mr. Sarkozy's chief diplomatic adviser, Jean-David Levitte, a former ambassador to the United States, to reassure the Chinese leadership that France has no intention of straining relations. 
In the wake of China's clampdown on Tibetan protests in March, Western governments are under pressure from the public and the news media to use the Olympic Games to extract concessions from Beijing on human rights and Tibet.
Jose Manuel Barroso of Portugal, president of the European Commission, the executive arm of the European Union, will raise the topic of human rights when he visits Beijing on Thursday, a spokesman said Monday.
Mr. Poncelet carried a letter from Mr. Sarkozy to President Hu Jintao of China, in which he apologized for the events in Paris and invited the Chinese athlete who protected the torch against protesters in Paris, Jin Jing, to come to the Elysee Palace.
