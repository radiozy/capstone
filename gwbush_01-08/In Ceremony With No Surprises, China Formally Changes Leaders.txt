
China's legislature officially elected Hu Jintao as the new president of China, replacing Jiang Zemin, in a previously expected vote that completed China's top leadership transition. 
Mr. Hu, 60, had already replaced Mr. Jiang as Communist Party secretary last November and so now holds the top posts in both the government and the party.
There was only one candidate for the position of president, a largely ceremonial post, but the nearly 3,000 legislative deputies voted nearly unanimously for Mr. Hu, with 2,937 votes for, 4 against and 3 abstentions.
Although Mr. Jiang gave up the presidency, other voting today ensured that he will remain a potent force in Chinese politics, especially in foreign policy. 
The delegates re-elected Mr. Jiang as head of the Central Military Commission and his longtime protege, Zeng Qinghong, to be the deputy president.
Since taking up the position of party secretary last November, Mr. Hu has focused largely on domestic policy, particularly in addressing the needs of China's farmers and laid off workers. 
He has left most of the foreign policy decisions to Mr. Jiang, who will presumably continue to exert great leverage in this area.
As in the presidential vote, there was only one candidate put forth for the military commission and vice presidential positions. 
Voting is generally nearly unanimous by the deputies, who are screened by the party before they can be elected. 
But when the votes were tallied it was clear that at least a healthy minority of the deputies had reservations about Mr. Jiang and his allies holding on to power.
While 2,578 deputies cast their vote for Zeng Qinghong as vice president, there were 177 votes against and 190 abstentions. 
And in the vote for chairman of the Central Military Commission Mr. Jiang received 2,726 votes, but also 98 against and 122 abstentions. 
Also, there were 36 write in votes for Mr. Hu.
