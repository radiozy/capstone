
A week after putting up for sale a historic painting of Mao Zedong, a Chinese auction house has canceled the June 3 auction of the portrait, below, saying it will probably be sent to a national museum in China. 
The Huachen Auction Company, which had said it planned to auction a painting that served as the model for the large portrait now hanging in Tiananmen Square, said the owner withdrew it after the government intervened this week. 
The potential auction of Mao's portrait, painted by Zhang Zhenshi, one of his official painters in the 1950's and 1960's, drew widespread criticism on the Internet in China and raised concerns about the historic image being sent overseas, even though it is already owned by an unidentified Chinese-American. 
In a statement yesterday the auction house said that after the government made ''suggestions,'' the owner withdrew the painting from Huachen's spring auction in Beijing and began negotiating with Chinese museums about a sale or donation. 
Measuring 36 inches by 27 inches, the portrait, believed to have been painted in the 1950's or 1960's, has been widely reproduced in China and was expected to sell for at least $120,000.
