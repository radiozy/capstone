
China successfully launched its third unmanned spacecraft tonight, the official New China News Agency said.
China's president, Jiang Zemin, watched as the spacecraft, called Shenzhou III, was launched at the Jiuquan Launch Center in the northwestern province of Gansu, the news agency reported.
China's Long March II-F rocket carried the Shenzhou III into outer space about 10:30 p.m. and the spacecraft went into orbit about 10 minutes later, it said.
Scientists at the Beijing Aerospace Directing and Controlling Center said later that the spacecraft's movement remained normal.
Last year, China sent a monkey, a dog, a rabbit and snails into orbit in January aboard its second unmanned Shenzhou spacecraft. 
Beijing plans to launch manned space flights by 2005 and hopes to eventually send a man to the moon.
