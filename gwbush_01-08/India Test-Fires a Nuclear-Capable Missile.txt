
India test-fired a shorter-range version of its nuclear-capable Agni missile today from a launch site off its east coast, a defense official said.
The Agni 1 missile was tested to a range of about 500 miles across the Bay of Bengal, the government said.
"It has been launched," the first word is it has been successful," a senior defense official said. 
"The first word is it has been successful.'
The surface-to-surface missile is a crucial element of India's plan to build a credible minimum nuclear deterrent against nuclear-armed neighbors China and Pakistan.
The Agni, which means fire in Sanskrit, is one of a range of missiles India is expected to test over the next few days.
While the longer-range version, intended to hit targets in China, is already operational, the shorter-range version tested for the second time today is seen as a deterrent against Pakistan.
The neighbors ended a 10-month military confrontation last year but remain tense over the disputed Himalayan region of Kashmir.
The missile test today , however, was seen as routine.
