
Distinctive Yixing teapots (pronounced ee-shing) from China, unglazed and in distinctive shapes, were often fashioned by poets and calligraphers during the Qing Dynasty (1644-1911). 
Some of these, like the teapot incised with calligraphy, right, are on display through June 16 in an exhibition, ''Tea, Wine and Poetry,'' at the China Institute, 125 East 65th Street. 
Other rare wine and water vessels, like the pewter wine cup with a jade handle, above, some of them never before shown in the United Sates, are also in the show, as are calligraphy, drawings by the artists and rubbings, with translations, of the inscriptions and art on the objects.
