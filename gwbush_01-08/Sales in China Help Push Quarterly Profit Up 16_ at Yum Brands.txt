
Yum Brands Inc. posted a 16 percent increase in third-quarter profits yesterday on resurgent growth in China combined with solid performances in the United States by its Taco Bell and KFC restaurant brands. 
The company also raised its full-year forecast.
Yum, which is based in Louisville, Ky., reported net income of $214 million, or 72 cents a share, for the three months that ended Sept. 
3, compared with $185 million, or 61 cents a share, a year ago.
Revenue rose 3 percent, to $2.24 billion from $2.18 billion last year, with United States sales at stores open at least a year increasing 4 percent. 
Analysts surveyed by Thomson Financial had expected earnings per share of 70 cents on revenue of $2.24 billion. 
More than a fourth of the company's profit came from its rapidly growing China division. 
Profit rose 32 percent, to $85 million, on a 20 percent revenue increase, to $360 million.
United States profits, meanwhile, dipped 4 percent, to $189 million, as revenue increased 2 percent from a year ago, to $1.39 billion. 
Mr. Blum said Hurricane Katrina cost the company about $3 million in sales.
The company forecast fourth-quarter earnings of 78 cents a share, excluding charges, and raised its earnings-per-share estimate for the year by 2 cents, to $2.64, excluding charges. 
For 2006, the company estimates growth of 10 percent in earnings per share.
Yum shares finished with a gain of 56 cents, or 1.2 percent, to close at $48.83 on the New York Stock Exchange. 
Its shares have traded in a 52-week range of $40.22 to $53.79.
Also yesterday, Wendy's International Inc. said that hurricanes and high gas prices had contributed to a sharp decline in its third-quarter sales.
Sales in stores open for more than a year -- or same-store sales -- fell by 5 percent for the flagship restaurants operated by the company and about 5.6 percent for franchises, the company said. 
A week after Hurricane Katrina struck the coast, 62 Wendy's restaurants remained closed. 
But Wendy's same-store sales were weak before the hurricane hit.
