
The German automaker Volkswagen reported a surprise 6.7 percent decline in second-quarter profit Friday because of one-time financial effects, but cheered investors with strong operating figures and rising sales in Europe.
Volkswagen, which makes Skoda, Audi, Lamborghini, Bentley and SEAT, earned 333 million euros ($404 million) in the quarter, down from 357 million euros a year earlier. 
Sales in the period, which ended June 30, rose almost 6 percent, to 24.89 billion euros, from 23.50 billion euros, as stronger European demand helped offset declines in the United States and China.
The company said the decline in net income was a result of one-time items in the period a year earlier that included income from affiliates and a re-evaluation of its assets.
But second-quarter operating profit, which excludes those effects, jumped 40 percent, to 911 million euros from 522 million euros, beating expectations of 730 million euros.
Earlier this year, Volkswagen approved discounts of more than 10 percent on some of its most popular models. 
Sales of its Golf and Touran van were on the upswing, as were the Polo, the new Passat model and its Fox models.
But sales in North America suffered, with executives blaming quality problems and the late start in introducing the revamped Jetta.
''We had serious quality problems in our Mexican plant,'' Volkswagen's chief executive, Bernd Pischetsrieder, said in a conference call with analysts. 
''The situation in the U.S. remains disastrous.''
The company's operating loss in the United States in the first six months rose to 596 million euros, from 503 million euros the year before.
In China, operating profit fell to 23 million euros from 251 million euros and VW said it would restructure its operations there.
Despite the troubles, investors appeared encouraged and lifted shares more than 2 percent in Frankfurt trading.
