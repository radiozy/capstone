
It has become an automatic reflex: mention the trade deficit, and the word ''China'' pops to mind. 
It is awfully easy to conjure images of wave upon wave of ''made in China'' stuff inundating our shores, dwarfing the influx from elsewhere.
Not so. 
Even though the ideas of ''made in Europe'' or ''made in Canada'' have no dire reverberations, both Europe and Canada send us far more goods than China does, as can be seen yet again in the latest numbers from the Bureau of Economic Analysis.
The problem is not what we are buying from China, but what China is not buying from us. 
You do not see a lot of ''made in U.S.A.'' labels there -- at least in part, economists say, because China's currency is kept artificially depressed, making price tags with dollar signs seem too lofty.
While the second word the deficit brings to mind may be ''oil,'' the United States' imports of all goods from all OPEC nations were half those from China. 
Then again, the data shows, ''made in U.S.A.'' barely exists in Saudi Arabia. 
HUBERT B. HERRING
