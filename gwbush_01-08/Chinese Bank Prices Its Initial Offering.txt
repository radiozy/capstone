
The Bank of Communications, China's fifth largest, priced its shares on Saturday for an initial public offering at 2.50 Hong Kong dollars (32 cents), near the top end of a range of 1.95 Hong Kong dollars to 2.55 Hong Kong dollars. 
The offering will raise at least 14.6 billion Hong Kong dollars ($1.88 billion) and as much as 16.9 billion Hong Kong dollars ($2.17 billion) if a 15 percent overallotment option is later exercised, Goldman Sachs said.
The range had been widely viewed as relatively low when it was announced on June 6.
HSBC took a 19.9 percent stake in the Chinese bank last year. 
The link to HSBC, which is a co-sponsor of the deal with Goldman Sachs, drew heavy investor interest.
The offering, which will start trading on Thursday, was 205 times oversubscribed by retail investors and 20 times oversubscribed by institutional investors.
By contrast, the China Shenhua Energy Company's initial public offering was priced toward the lower end of the range and still fell in its first day of trading last Wednesday. 
And on June 7, the China Minsheng Banking Corporation postponed its initial public offering and has not yet set a new date.
The state-owned China Cosco Holding Company, one of the world's largest shipping companies, announced on Sunday that it would proceed with its initial public offering.
