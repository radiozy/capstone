
Two prominent labor leaders who organized demonstrations on behalf of workers at a bankrupt state-run company in northeastern China have been charged with "subverting state power," an offense that could result in a life sentence or the death penalty under Chinese law, a human rights monitoring group said today.
The labor leaders, Yao Fuxin and Xiao Yunliang, helped link together disaffected workers from numerous failed state-run businesses in the northeastern city of Liaoyang last spring.
Street protests by as many as 30,000 laid-off workers calling for the state to make good on unpaid pensions and to crack down on corruption rattled local authorities because of their size and the possibility that workers from different industries had begun cooperating in seeking better living standards.
Mr. Yao and Mr. Xiao were initially held on charges of organizing illegal demonstrations, but the New York-based China Labor Watch said today the men would be tried on the more serious charge of subversion.
