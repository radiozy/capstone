
China may be set to overtake the United States in the next decade as the world's second largest consumer of luxury goods, but the question remains whether China's new money will put their yuan behind products made -- let alone designed -- in their own backyard. 
''China Design Now,'' at the Victoria and Albert Museum in London through July 13, takes an expansive view of China's burgeoning design culture. 
Still, the fact that the curators saw fit to include projects like Rem Koolhaas's CCTV headquarters and Herzog & de Meuron's Olympic stadium as well as a pair of Nike Air Force 1's could be taken as a hint. 
Meanwhile, the KT Wong Charitable Trust has commissioned the Chinese artist Shao Fan to create a garden for the Royal Horticultural Society Chelsea Flower Show in May. 
Fan's design (above, right), which brings a contemporary perspective to the traditional scholar's garden, is an elegant example of the Wong Trust's ambition to foster an East/West exchange of a decidedly more cerebral nature.
