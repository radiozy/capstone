On Monday, an affiliate company of Kirk Kerkorian, the billionaire stock trader, reported in a regulatory filing its interest in buying two of the more expensive Las Vegas properties held by MGM Mirage: the stylish Bellagio casino-hotel and the partly built CityCenter, a vast condominium-hotel project that MGM says is the most expensive privately financed project in United States history.
Analysts estimate that the two properties would cost Mr. Kerkorian's affiliate, the Tracinda Corporation, which already owns 56 percent of MGM stock, at least $12 billion. 
On Tuesday MGM's board, which has Mr. Kerkorian as a member, announced that it was forming a committee of independent directors to consider the Kerkorian offer.
The regulatory filing also mentioned the possibility that Mr. Kerkorian might seek a ''financial restructuring'' of the company in which he is the majority stakeholder. 
That has led to speculation on Wall Street and in Las Vegas about Mr. Kerkorian's true aim: in a world awash in private equity cash, is the 89-year-old wily deal maker seeking to take the company private?
