
Thirty million Chinese with nothing to do at work are threatened by a bankruptcy law going into effect Tuesday.
Diplomats are divided about whether the law, adopted after years of debate, will force any of China's 400,000 money-losing factories to close.
Some officials have warned of possible unrest if people are thrown out of work in a country with no social security system.
 
Yuan Mu, spokesman of the
State Council, said at a news conference on Friday: ''After the bankruptcy law is implemented, some backward firms will go bankrupt and the number of unemployed, currently 2 percent, will rise. 
But the numbers will be small.''
China is allocating 41 million yuan ($11 billion) this year, 15 percent of its total budget, to subsidize state concerns that are losing money. 
The Government hopes to reduce this sum.
