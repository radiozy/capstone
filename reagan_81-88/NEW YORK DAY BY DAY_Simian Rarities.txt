
In his continuing quest for pandas from China, Mayor Koch appears to have gained for the Bronx Zoo the loan of a pair of golden monkeys instead.
The golden monkeys - with long flaxen hair on back and shoulders and light blue faces - inhabit the same Sichuan forests as the giant pandas and are similarly considered an endangered national treasure by the Chinese.
After making yet another pitch for pandas yesterday, this time to a delegation of municipal officials on a visit to City Hall, Mayor Koch released a telex in which the Peking municipal government agreed to a loan of the monkeys.
''The Beijing Golden Monkey will be hopefully shown in New York for three months, for we've made great efforts about it,'' said the telex from the foreign-affairs office of the Peking Government.
Robert K. Kaplan, director of New York's sister-city program, said that he expected the monkeys sometime next year. 
Officials at the zoo said they were unaware of the details of the visit and declined further comment.
The golden monkeys were hunted over the centuries for their skins and are found only in three small mountainous areas of China.
Their first appearance outside China was at the San Diego Zoo from November 1984 to May 1985. 
According to Jean Gertmenian, a spokesman for the zoo, the golden monkeys drew crowds.
''They do not look like any other animal,'' she said. 
''They're unique.''
