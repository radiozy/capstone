
An 83- year-old Chinese man who abandoned his life as a clothing merchant to plant trees in the mountains has been honored by the Government, The China Daily newspaper reported today.
The official English-language daily said the man, Zhang Houla, working alone with a hoe, had planted more than a million trees on 50 acres in a remote valley in Shanxi Province in north-central China.
Chinese Government honors Zhang Houla, 83-year-old man who abandoned life as clothing merchant in '66 to plant trees in mountains of Shanxi Province (S)
Quoting from Outlook Magazine, the newspaper said Mr. Zhang was known as a ''forestry fanatic, madman and savage'' by local people.
In 1966, at the age of 66, he divorced his wife of more than 40 years and left his family to live in a mountain cave and plant trees. 
The report quoted Mr. Zhang's wife as telling neighbors, ''I can't stop him, nor can I stand him.''
He lived mostly on yams and wild herbs, bought no new clothes or shoes and used his own savings to buy seeds and nursery stock.
After Chinese leaders started a nationwide program to plant trees in 1981, Mr. Zhang's efforts finally gained respect. 
Local authorities decided to write his story in the county history book, award him $1,500 and appoint one of his sons as caretaker of the forest.
