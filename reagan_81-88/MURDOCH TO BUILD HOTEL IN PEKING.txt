
Rupert Murdoch, the publisher whose newspaper and magazine empire stretches from New York to London to his native Australia, will build an international hotel and news media center in Peking, a spokesman for Mr. Murdoch said yesterday.
The center, which is scheduled to be completed by 1987 at a cost of $40 million, will have 300 hotel rooms and 100 apartments for journalists and business executives, according to Howard J. Rubenstein, Mr. Murdoch's spokesman in New York.
''It will certainly fill a very, very urgent need for the foreign business community in Peking,'' said Arthur H. Rosen, president of the National Committee on U.S.-China Relations in New York, a group that organizes educational programs and exchanges between China and the United States. 
''It would also be very useful for Western media in Peking to have a modern media center, and it would probably increase television coverage of China.''
The project will be a joint venture of Mr. Murdoch's News Corporation and the Chinese Government, which will provide the land, construction assistance and pay some construction costs. 
It was not clear who would have majority ownership or control of the enterprise. 
Mr. Rubenstein said he had no further details.
Mr. Murdoch, whose holdings include The New York Post, The Village Voice and The Times of London, was traveling yesterday and could not be reached for comment. 
A spokesman for the Chinese Embassy in Washington was out of the office and could not be reached.
The project was discussed in a meeting last month between Bob Hawke, the Australian Prime Minister, and Hu Yaobang, General Secretary of China's Communist Party. 
Mr. Murdoch was in Peking in early April to discuss the venture with Chinese officials.
