
China agreed today to accept substitutes for some products of the Toshiba Machine Company, partly resolving an eight-month dispute over Japan's decision to bar Toshiba from exporting to Communist states.
The Ministry of Foreign Economic Relations and Trade, however, continued to demand compensation for losses incurred by delayed or suspended contracts, the official New China News Agency reported.
Last May, the Japanese Government imposed a one-year ban on Toshiba Machine's exports to 14 Communist nations after disclosures that Toshiba had illegally supplied the Soviet Union with milling equipment used to produce ultra-quiet submarines.
The Chinese ministry said in a news release that the May ruling victimized China in 25 contracts involving Toshiba exports and that $900 million worth of Chinese contracts signed with other Japanese concerns were stalled because of tightened Japanese export restrictions.
The direct value of the Toshiba contracts has been put at $19 million.
Last month Japan's Ministry of International Trade and Industry offered to let Toshiba Machine, a subsidiary of the giant Toshiba Corporation, fulfill seven of the 25 contracts. 
The ministry said other Japanese concerns would supply the products promised under the other contracts.
