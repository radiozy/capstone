
An American businessman has been formally arrested and charged with causing a hotel fire that killed 10 people in Harbin, his lawyer said today.
The businessman, Richard S. Ondrik, 34 years old, from Houston, had been detained for more than two months in connection with the fire.
Robert Goodwin, Washington lawyer, says he has been retained by American businessman Richard S Ondrik, who was formally arrested and charged with causing hotel fire that killed 10 people in Harbin, China (M)He was arrested Wednesday and transferred to a police detention center, according to the lawyer, Robert Goodwin.
''It is kind of like a dormitory, it is a lockup,'' Mr. Goodwin said in a telephone interview.
It remains unclear why the police waited two months to arrest Mr. Ondrik. He had been held under a law that permits the detention of suspects or witnesses during an investigation.
Mr. Ondrik, who represents a Hong Kong company called Energy Projects, Southeast Asia Ltd., was staying at the 15-story Swan Hotel when a fire destroyed the 11th floor on April 19. 
His business partner, Alan Eng, five North Koreans and four hotel workers died.
The police detained Mr. Ondrik and confined him to another hotel without formally arresting him.
Mr. Goodwin, a Washington lawyer retained by Mr. Ondrik, said he has been trying to find a Chinese lawyer to represent his client because foreigners are not permitted to practice law in China. 
The case has not been reported in the Chinese press.
Mr. Goodwin said he understood that Mr. Ondrik was charged with causing the fire through negligence and that the maximum penalty is seven years in prison. 
He declined to say how Mr. Ondrik planned to plead.
The businessman had said in earlier telephone interviews that he did not remember what happened.
American sources said Commerce Secretary Malcolm Baldrige raised the issue of Mr. Ondrik's detention during a visit here last month.
