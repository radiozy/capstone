
The Chinese Government accused Washington today of delaying approval of the sale of technology products to China and said that, as a result of this policy, the United States was missing out on export opportunities.
An editorial in the official Beijing Review magazine said the United States approved less than two-thirds of the technology exports for which China applied last year.
It added that the United States was maintaining unwarranted restrictions on the transfer of technology.
The editorial appeared on the eve of a visit by Commerce Secretary Malcolm Baldrige, who will be co-chairman of the fifth session of a joint commission on commerce and trade that will review trade relations between the two countries.
The editorial said continuing trade restrictions should have been lifted after the United States in 1983 moved China into a less-restricted category for sensitive technology items.
But the editorial charged that the United States at times approved technology sales only after learning that other countries were selling the same items to China, or delayed approval until what China finally received ''had become relatively backward.''
The American Embassy declined to comment. 
But a Western diplomat said the editorial voiced concerns that had been expressed by American manufacturers who say they lost business to companies from other industrialized countries with less restrictive export policies.
