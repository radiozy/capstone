
The Chinese Communist Party, rejecting overtures from Moscow, will not attend the celebration next week of the 70th anniversary of the Bolshevik Revolution, Soviet and Chinese officials said today.
The rejection, while not unexpected, was a pointed reminder that despite Mikhail S. Gorbachev's offer to restore normal relations between Moscow and Beijing their estrangement remains deep.
Nikolai Shishlin, deputy chief of the Propaganda Department of the Soviet Party Central Committee, said at a news conference that more than 80 Communist parties and 30 Socialist, Social Democratic and Labor parties would be represented at the two-day ceremony beginning Nov. 
2.
Asked if the Chinese would be among them, he said, ''So far we don't think that they are prepared, or have told us they are prepared, to participate.''
A Chinese official here said his country had decided definitely not to send a party representative, although a delegation from the Chinese-Soviet Friendship Society would be present.
The Chinese party has boycotted Soviet Communist Party congresses and revolutionary holidays since the 1960's, when Mosow and Beijing split over ideology and foreign policy. 
The ideological differences that arose at the time of the Cultural Revolution in China have largely dissipated, and Mr. Gorbachev has overseen a general warming of relations.
A Chinese official said no formal invitation was made to the Chinese party after Beijing rejected informal feelers put out by the Kremlin.
