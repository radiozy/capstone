
In a further opening of China to Western ideas, books published by American university presses will be exhibited simultaneously in six Chinese cities for a week beginning Nov. 
5.
''It is the first such exhibit of American scholarly works in China in more than a generation,'' said Christopher Paddack, creative arts program officer of the United States Information Agency, which is helping to finance the project.
Association of American University Presses to exhibit scholarly books published by American university presses simultaneously in six Chinese cities for week beginning Nov 5; United States Information Agency helps to finance project (S)
Almost 1,200 books from 67 publishers will be at each of the six identical exhibitions to be held in Peking, Shanghai, Canton, Chengdu, Fuzhou and Urumqi.
''The Chinese people are eager to have our books, but it has been hard to work the finances and logistics,'' said Frances Gendlin, executive director of the Association of American University Presses, which has been seeking such an exhibition for three years. 
In each of the last four years, the association printed and sent to China a catalogue of representative university press books, but there was no real mechanism to make American scholarly books better known.
 
No Restrictions Imposed
''They told us they would prefer books in natural and social sciences,'' Miss Gendlin said, ''but they imposed no restrictions on the books to be sent or the presses to be included.''
One press whose books will be represented is the Chinese University Press in Hong Kong, one of 9 international members of the 91-member association.
The United States Information Agency has agreed to produce and ship 12,000 catalogues listing each book title. 
The agency will also give $32,500 to help cover the cost of sending a delegation of press directors to accompany the exhibits.
