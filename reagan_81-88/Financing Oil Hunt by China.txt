
China will need $20 billion to finance offshore oil exploration and production by 1990, a prominent Hong Kong banker said here today.
Michael Sandberg, chairman of the Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation, told delegates to an oil conference here that ''if drilling is initiated early in 1982, at least $10 billion will be required to fund exploration and production by 1985, followed by another $10 billion by 1990.''
Michael Sandberg, chairman of Hong Kong and Shanghai Banking Corp, predicts that China will need $20 billion for oil exploration by 1990
China is expected to call for bids from foreign oil companies early next year to exploit its offshore oil resources in the South China Sea and the southern Yellow Sea.
The chairman of Hong Kong's largest bank said that the Pacific Basin banking community could manage financing packages or loan syndications for the venture.
China must develop its offshore oil to finance its modernization programs, Mr. Sandberg said. 
It is also in the strategic interests of the Pacific Basin nations to see China expand its oil production to lessen dependence on Middle Eastern sources, he added.
The Middle East provides 70 percent of the area's oil consumption of 25 million barrels a day.
