
China, to no one's surprise, captured first place today - for its fifth consecutive victory - in the 11th annual Hong Kong international dragon boat races. 
But to everyone's surprise, the False Creek Canoe Club of Vancouver, British Columbia, placed second.
The Canadian team, with Olympic canoeists among the crew of its dragon-headed racing canoe, handled the choppy water of Victoria Harbor's waterfront course easily, which allowed it to beat a fast-closing Indonesian team by five-tenths of a second. 
The Indonesians were slowed when their portside No. 2 paddler missed two strokes and lost control of his paddle in the final few yards of the race.
''It was a killer of a race,'' said Mike Flesher, one of the Canadians. 
''We were flat out in the end with nothing left. 
It's a good race when it goes like that.''
The Chinese, with their 20-man crew employing a choppy stroke, drove their cadence - kept by a drummer aboard the canoe - to 110 beats to a minute at various stages over the 640-meter distance of the race. 
The Canadians used only 19 paddlers in an effort to lighten the boat.
Don Irvine, another of the Canadians, believed that with calmer water and an equally manned canoe, his team could beat the Chinese. 
''The Chinese are well disciplined and fine athletes, but not unbeatable. 
With 20 people we could have a really good race with the Chinese. 
As it was, it was primo close.''
The Chinese won in the time of 2 minutes 34.01 seconds, 3.24 seconds ahead of Canada.
The Chinese women's team won its race in the time of 2 minutes 52.12 seconds, with Australia second and Hong Kong third.
The United States' entry, from Norfolk, Va., finished sixth, 9.17 seconds behind the winner.
''We're a little disappointed,'' Frank Havens, the American coach, said after the race.
This was the fourth year the United States has raced here, and its sixth-place showing was its worst showing yet.
