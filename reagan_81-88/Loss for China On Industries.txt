
China has 13,000 industrial enterprises operating at a yearly loss of $2.8 billion because of waste, poor planning and incompetent management, China's Finance Minister said in a report that was released today.
If these and other problems in production, construction and the movement of commodities were solved, China would gain $6.7 billion or more annually, the minister, Wang Bingqian, reported to the National Peoples Congress on Dec. 
1.
China's Finance Minister reports 13,000 of nation's industrial enterprises are operating at yearly loss of $2.8 billion
Mr. Wang said that the equivalent of $67 invested in capital construction yielded only $7.33 in state revenue, a 50 percent drop compared with $14.67 in the period of the first five-year plan, 1953-57.
''What warrants our attention is that quite a few units, to varying extents, throw lavish dinner parties at public expense or squander funds in other ways,'' he added. 
''Some units have gone so far as to divide up products illicitly and engage in smuggling, bribery, speculation, profiteering, graft and embezzlement.''
Explaining China's economic status, Mr. Wang said that debt service on foreign loans was adding to the country's budget deficits. 
In 1980, he added, foreign loans accounted for $2.9 billion, or 3.9 percent, of total Government revenue of $72.3 billion. 
In 1981, foreign loans increased to $5.3 billion, or 7.5 percent, of total revenue of $70.6 billion, he said.
The 1981 budget was cut sharply after deficits of $11.3 billion in 1979 and $8.5 billion in 1980. 
The 1981 deficit would be $1.8 billion, Mr. Wang said.
