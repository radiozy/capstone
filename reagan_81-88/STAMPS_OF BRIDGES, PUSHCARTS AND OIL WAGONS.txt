
The United Nations Postal Administration will issue six new definitives and four new postal stationery items on May 10. 
The United Nations New York stamp denominations are 22 cents and $3. 
The Geneva, Switzerland issues in Swiss Francs are 0,20 and 1,20 and the Vienna, Austria values are $4.50 and $15. 
Postal Stationery issues are a 22-cent pre-stamped envelope, Swiss francs 0,50 and 0.70 postal cards and a S4 postal card.
Designs for these stamps were selected in a worldwide competition and feature the symbolic theme of international peace and brotherhood. 
The 22c New York stamp depicts Peoples of the World, while the $3 features the words United Nations in six languages. 
The Geneva Swiss francs 0,20 and 1,20 stamps depict an international courier and peace doves, while the Vienna S4.50 and S15 stamps show a laurel-leaf boat and people from various nations under a common United Nations umbrella. 
Postal stationery designs offer a bouquet of multi-colored strips (22c envelope), the United Nations in six languages (Swiss francs 0,50), a peace dove and rainbow (Swiss francs 0,70) and a multi-colored abstract S4.

Special hand and machine cancellations will be provided for the May 10 First Day of Issue. 
Further details and order information for stamps and postal stationery can be obtained from the United Nations Postal Administration, United Nations, New York, NY 10017.
In conjunction with the First Day of Issue, the World Federation of United Nations Associations (Wfuna) will offer an exquisite First Day Cover that features a reproduction of Chinese artist Chen Yifei's ''Bridge of Peace'' oil painting. 
The painting was created expressly for Wfuna in the international peace and brotherhood theme.
Chen Yifei graduated from Shanghai Art College in 1965 and was the first artist from the People's Republic of China to study and work in the United States. 
His first one-man exhibition at the Hamnmer Galleries in New York in October 1983, sold out in the first week while the majority of works in his second exhibition, also at the Hammer Galleries, were sold before the exhibition opened. 
Mr. Chen's paintings blend romanticism and realism evoking memories of the Old Masters of Europe. 
His paintings convey a peaceful quality of life making liberal use of the canals and ancient bridges of China.
Chen Yifei will be at the Wfuna counter in the lower concourse of the United Nations General Assembly Building on May 10 to greet collectors and autograph First Day Covers. 
They feature the ''Bridge of Peace'' cachet are offered individually or with all six stamps on a special Courier Cover that will be cancelled in Geneva, Vienna and New York. 
Prices ranges from $1.10 for a single 22 cent stamps on cover to $12.95 for the Courier Cover. 
Blank covers at 5 for $1 are also offered. 
Postage and packing fee in the United States is 50 cents for up to five blank or serviced covers and 20 cents for each additional five covers.
An order form and complete information on all covers available with the Chen Yifei cachet can be obtained by sending a stamped, self-addressed envelope to Wfuna May 10 Cachet, Room DC1-1177, United Nations, New York, N.Y. 
10017. Because mail orders should reach WFUNA by May 10, collectors may want to telephone (212) 754-5610 for order information.

Two new additions to the United States Transportation Series were issued last month in Oil Center, N. M. A 10.1-cent stamp depicts an 1890's oil wagon and a 12.5-cent stamp features an 1880's pushcart. 
The stamps meet the need for bulk third-class mail rates and are the 17th and 18th stamps in a series that is noteworthy for its simplicity of design.
As with the previous 7.4-cent baby buggy issue, the pushcart design may be stretching ''transportation'' to its limits. 
As one wag put it, perhaps future design in this Transportation Series will be a pair of shoes.
The oil wagon stamps are printed in blue, while the pushcart stamps are olive. 
Each is issued in coils of 500 and 3,000 with a single digit plate number on every 52nd stamp in the roll.
The usual First Day Cover ordering terms apply. 
Collectors are encouraged to purchase stamps in advance and affix them to envelopes before requesting First Day Cancels. 
These stamps can be purchased at philatelic centers but not at local post offices, The Postal Service gives preferential treatment to such covers and there is no charge for servicing. 
Please note that covers must bear at least 22c postasge to cover the first class rate. 
All stamps affixed by the collector must have been issued no later than April 18. 
For Postal Service affixing, one oil wagon and one pushcart stamp will be applied to each cover at a charge of 23 cents per envelope submitted.
All orders must be postmarked no later than May 18. 
Cash or postage stamps are not acceptable. 
Personal checks are accepted up to a limit of 50 covers. 
The address for customer-affixed envelopes is Customer-Affixed Envelopes, Oil Wagon (or Pushcart) Stamp, Postmaster, Oil Center, NM 88266-9992. 
$10.75 Stamp On Monday in San Francisco, the United States Postal Service will issue a $10.75 stamp intended primarily for use on items weighing up to two pounds and sent via Express Mail Service. 
Available in singles or in booklets containing one pane of three stamps, the $10.75 stamp is similar in design to its predecessor $9.35 edition. 
(The eagle looks more prosperious on the $10.75 issue).

Customers can purchase stamps at most post offices on April 30th and affix them to return-addressed envelopes for preferential servicing by the U.S.P.S. 
Requests should be postmarked no later than May 29 and addressed to Customer-Affixed Envelopes, $10.75 Stamp, San Francisco, Calif. 
94188-9991.
Collectors who opt for Postal Service affixing can send covers by May 29 to $10.75 Stamp, Postmaster, San Francisco, Calif. 
94188-9992. Personal checks or money orders will be accepted at $10.75 per stamp to a limit of 50 covers.
Collectors have been asking if this $10.75 stamp is the highest value to be issued by the United States. 
The answer is yes, if counting only those stamps intended for postal use (as opposed to Revenue issues) and if the effects of inflation on the value of the dollar is ignored.
My nominee for the most expensive United States postal issue would have to be the $5 Columbian stamp issued in 1893 for the Chicago Columbian Exposition. 
If factoring in the effects of inflation, $5 in 1893 was the equivalent of $62.60 today. 
The highest face value United States issues of any sort are the $10,000 Documentay and Stock Transfer Revenue stamps issued between 1940 and 1958 to evidence payment of the appropriate taxes. 
The 1958 Documentary with perforated initial cancellations can be purchased by collectors for less than $20.
As for world wide issues, according to ''This Is Philately'' by Kenneth A. Wood, the dubious distinction for the highest denomination ever created goes to the 1946 inflation-torn Hungary five hundred thousand billion pango stamp. 
Used or unused copies sell for about 10 cents each.
