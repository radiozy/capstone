
China today declared its current five-year economic plan to be a success, setting the scene for the introduction of the next five-year plan sometime over the next two weeks.
The official Economic Daily said most of the targets in the current plan, drawn up to guide economic development from 1981 through 1985, were surpassed one or two years ago.
The new plan, covering development from 1986 through 1990, will be approved at a Communist Party meeting later this month. 
Western analysts said it would mark an important new stage in the efforts of Deng Xiaoping, the Chinese leader, to revitalize the economy.
The Economic Daily said wages, industrial and agricultural production, new housing and disposable income had all risen by more than the targeted amount during the first four years of the current plan.
But the publication pointed to four problem areas: overinvestment in capital construction, supplies of consumer goods running behind demand, transportation bottlenecks and a lack of coordination among heavy industries resulting in waste.
