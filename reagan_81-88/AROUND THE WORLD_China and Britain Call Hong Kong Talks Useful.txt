
China and Britain said today that their latest talks on Hong Kong's future were useful and constructive, and that further sessions would be held Nov. 
14 and 15.
Western diplomats were unwilling to read too much into the brief joint statement, but said the wording and the fact that the talks were to resume in less than a month were reasonably promising signs.
China and Britain call their latest talks on future of Hong Kong useful and constructive; talks will resume in Nov (S)
The statement was issued after two days of discussions in Peking, the fifth round of talks since July on the colony's future after 1997, when China seeks to regain sovereignty.
After the fourth round, in September, the two sides refused to describe the talks, implying that they were stalled. 
Business confidence in Hong Kong plummeted, and last weekend the colony's Government stepped in to bolster the Hong Kong dollar, setting an official exchange rate for the colony's two note-issuing banks.
