
China has notified Japanese clients that it wants to raise the price of its crude oil by just over $3, to $37 a barrel, oil importers said today.
The increase would be from the current $33.975 per 42-gallon barrel.
China raises price of its crude oil to $37 a barrelChina's move was in the form of a request, but United States oil industry sources said the move effectively raised the price of the country's Daiqing crude, its principal export grade.
China exports about 600,000 barrels of oil daily, with one-third of the total going to Japan, United States and Japanese sources said. 
Spokesmen for the Importers Conference of Chinese Petroleum in Japan and the International Oil Trading Company, the two major Japanese companies importing Chinese crude, said they had protested the Chinese increase and planned negotiations with China on the move. 
Following talks with the Japanese importers, China last November cut oil prices $1.50 a barrel.
Most major oil exporters have raised crude oil prices by between $2 and $6 a barrel since mid-December.
