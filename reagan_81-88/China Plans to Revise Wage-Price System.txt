
Communist Party leaders have agreed on a five-year draft plan to revise China's system of prices and wages, the official New China News Agency said today.
In a report, the agency said the three-day meeting of the party Politburo, chaired by the party leader, Zhao Ziyang, ended in the coastal town of Beidaihe on Wednesday.
Details of the plan, spanning 1989 to 1993, were not disclosed but the press agency said that ''the price and wage reforms mean an all-around deepening of China's reforms.''
The agency said that inflation, which hit an annual rate of 13 percent in the first half of 1988, would be brought under control by ''effective, comprehensive measures,'' including cutting back on construction projects.
The state would continue to control prices of a few major commodities while allowing market forces to set the rest.
Changes in state enterprises would be speeded up so that company managers would begin to take full responsibility for profits and losses, the agency said.
Diplomats said they doubted this would mean layoffs in the near future although 30 million urban workers are officially said to have jobs in which they do nothing.
Politburo members noted ''the immense significance of such reforms to the endeavor to establish a new, socialist economic order in China.''
Diplomats said China was anxious to dismiss speculation that the country's 10-year-old reform program had ground to a halt because of widespread discontent with record inflation.
The party's Central Committee will meet next month to discuss the draft five-year plan, the agency added.
