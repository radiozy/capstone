
Officials of the Big Three automobile companies will meet with a delegation from China next month to discuss establishing a large joint venture to produce cars in that country.
Auto company executives cautioned that there had been contacts with China for years and no large-scale deals had emerged.
According to news reports from Beijing, Zhu Rongji, a top official of the State Economic Commission, said that China was interested in finding an American partner for a plant that would produce 300,000 cars a year.
Officials of the three companies confirmed they were preparing to meet with Mr. Zhu and other Chinese officials.
A factory that could produce 300,000 cars a year would be larger than most assembly plants in the United States, where annual capacity is usually in the range of 200,000 to 240,000 vehicles. 
A new assembly plant in this country typically represents an investment of about $750 million.
The only Big Three company with significant operations in China is the Chrysler Corporation, which inherited a joint-venture Jeep factory in Beijing when it merged with the American Motors Corporation this summer and which had earlier sold engine-making machinery to a Chinese company.
A spokesman for the Ford Motor Company said that Chinese officials appeared interested last year in a joint venture to build a small, multipurpose van similar to one Ford builds in Europe but that nothing had come of the idea.
Although the Chinese market is potentially vast, foreign businesses have often found operations there difficult, because of the underdeveloped industrial base and intricate bureaucracy.
The Jeep operation would be dwarfed by a 300,000-a-year car plant. 
Chrysler officials said Beijing Jeep was expected to produce 3,000 Cherokee models this year, along with 23,000 units of an old, Russian-designed off-road vehicle.
