
The most senior Tibetan religious figure in China has disputed official assertions that Chinese policemen did not shoot at demonstrating monks during a riot in Lhasa last October.
China's Foreign Ministry, as well as security officials in Tibet and Beijing, have insisted repeatedly that the police never fired their weapons during the protests, in which more than 12 people were killed.
But in a report on Tibetan radio that reached here today, the Panchen Lama, the second-holiest figure in Tibetan Buddhism, said that the police fired warning shots to disperse crowds attacking a police station, and that ricochets killed and wounded several people.
The Panchen Lama lives in Beijing and is the most visible Tibetan defender of Chinese policies in the region.
The Panchen Lama's remarks, which were made on Feb. 
8 and Feb. 
9, were monitored, translated and published by a service of the BBC.
