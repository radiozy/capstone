
Amid reports of the worst unrest in Tibet in three decades, officials from the region and its capital city have been on a two- week tour of the United States.
The 13-member delegation arrived in the United States on Oct. 
7.
The delegation, which includes Luo Gar, the Mayor of Lhasa, and Thang Zhengqi, deputy secretary of the Tibetan Autonomous Region, left Boulder today for San Francisco, their final stop.
Boulder was on the group's itinerary because it established a Sister City program with Lhasa two years ago at the behest of local Buddhists. 
But some Buddhists here have denounced the visitors because they are officials of China, which annexed Tibet in 1950.
On Oct. 
1, when 14 people were reported killed in the worst clashes, the Mayor and other members of the delegation were in Chengdu, 750 miles from Lhasa, en route to the United States.
The Mayor played down the anti-Chinese unrest. 
''If it had been really violent,'' he said through an interpreter here, ''we would never have been able to leave the country.''
He said his group had not been in direct contact with colleagues in Lhasa since leaving Shanghai on Oct. 
3.
