
Archeologists have excavated the huge tomb of a rich, slave-owning duke who lived 2,600 years ago, the biggest ancient burial chamber found in China this century, a leading newspaper said today.
As deep as an eight-story building and large enough to hold 200 dump trucks, the tomb is in the shape of an inverted pyramid and contains the bodies of 180 male and female slaves, said the report in The Guangming Daily.
The report said the duke's coffin would be opened this week, climaxing 10 years of work on the site in Shaanxi province.
The tomb was discovered in 1976, and the report said the dig had been the longest and most elaborate in Chinese archeological history.
Experts think the tomb was built for Duke Jing of the state of Chin during the Spring and Autumn Period (770-476 B.C.) of the Chou Dynasty.
