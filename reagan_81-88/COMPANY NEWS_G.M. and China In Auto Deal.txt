
The General Motors Corporation announced today that it had reached an agreement to sell a used engine assembly line to the China National Automotive Industry Corporation and to cooperate with various Chinese agencies in developing an auto industry in that country.
Ultimately, G.M. hopes to be part of a joint venture to produce light trucks and cars for domestic consumption in China, which G.M. expects will lead to the broadest involvement of any foreign company in China, company officials said.
The initial agreement calls for the sale of the assembly line in Flint, Mich., for $16.5 million, the New China News Agency said. 
G.M. is also negotiating to form a foundry joint venture and is discussing projects to produce other equipment in China.
A second phase of G.M.'s involvement in China would involve manufacturing other components locally and is expected to lead to the production of complete G.M.-designed vehicles, like the Chevrolet S-10 compact pickup truck.
The Chrysler Corporation, through its acquisition of the American Motors Corporation last year, is producing four-wheel-drive vehicles in China.
