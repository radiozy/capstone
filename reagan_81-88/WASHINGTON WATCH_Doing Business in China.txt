
Senator John Heinz, Republican of Pennsylvania, who recently led a Senate delegation to China, found that, in business relations, the Chinese appear to have a strong preference for dealing with Americans over the Japanese. 
The reason, he wrote in his report on the visit, is that even though the Japanese invest much time, effort and manpower in their Chinese sales efforts, they do not set up joint ventures or share technology.
''It may well be that the willingness of American firms to stay the course of building a joint venture will give the United States a firmer footing in China than all of Japan's massive sales effort,'' he commented.
But the problems of doing business in China are ''daunting,'' he found, noting that the profitability of American businesses there was ''marginal'' and that business was ''creating some problems for itself'' by failing to understand the Chinese decision-making process.
