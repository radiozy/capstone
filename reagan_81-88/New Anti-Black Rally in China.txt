
Thousands of Chinese youths demonstrated today in Nanjing, calling on their Government to punish African students who battled Chinese over the weekend, an American witness said.
It was the third major anti-black protest in the capital of Jiangsu Province in three days.
After several days of violence, the police for the first time took stern measures to control the crowds.
Hundreds of officers, many wearing riot gear, filled the city. 
Some cruised the streets in trucks, others stood on street corners, pushing back crowds.
A band of 200 police stood guard around the city's main hotel for foreigners, said the witness, David Chen, 19 years old, of Princeton, N.J.
Chinese authorities, meanwhile, released five Americans who had been held with 140 African students in a guest house on the outskirts of Nanjing. 
Chinese authorities said the Africans were still being held because their safety could not be guaranteed. 
An American and about 20 students from Western Europe and Japan stayed with them.
The police forced the Africans and the other students to the guest house Monday night after Chinese authorities refused to allow the Africans to take trains to Beijing.
Some of the released Americans said the police dragged them and the other students from the Nanjing train station Monday night.
'They were just dragging people out,'' said Elizabeth Morrisson of Prairie Village, Kan., speaking by telephone from Nanjing. 
''Some people struggled so violently, they were hurt.''
