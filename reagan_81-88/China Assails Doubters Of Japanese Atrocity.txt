
The People's Daily published an article today denouncing Japanese who dispute that their country's army put to death 300,000 Chinese in Nanjing 50 years ago, and added that Tokyo would never again become militaristic.
The official newspaper said some Japanese newspaper articles based on ''feeble and laughable'' evidence had said that the Nanjing massacre, which began Dec. 
12, 1937, did not take place or that the number killed was lower than had been estimated.
The paper said this flew in the face of all the evidence of Chinese witnesses and foreign reporters, and quoted a report in the Japanese newspaper Mainichi Shimbun describing how soldiers competed to kill the most Chinese.
''One warrant officer killed 106, another 105, so neither was the winner, so they resolved to carry on the killing contest,'' it quoted the paper as saying.
Only when Japanese stopped trying to distort the truth ''can the spirits of the tens of thousands of dead rest in peace and the peoples of China and Japan truly cooperate,'' The People's Daily said.
A commemorative museum has been built in Nanjing, and two films on the massacre are being made for the first time, the New China News Agency said.
