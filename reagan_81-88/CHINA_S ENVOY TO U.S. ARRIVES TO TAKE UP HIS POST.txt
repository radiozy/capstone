WASHINGTON, March 4 (Reuters) - Zhang Wenjin, a veteran of toplevel meetings between China and the United States, arrived today to take up his post as Peking's Ambassador to Washington.
Mr. Zhang is a former aide to Prime Minister Zhou Enlai and took part in the initial contacts with the United States that led to the signing of the Shanghai Communique in 1972.
Mr. Zhang replaces Chai Zemin, who had been Ambassador to the United States since 1979. 
On arrival at National Airport today, Mr. Zhang was met by Assistant Secretary of State Paul D. Wolfowitz. A State Department spokesman said such a greeting for a new Ambassador was ''not unprecedented.''
The two men met during Secretary of State George P. Shultz's trip to Peking last month.
Mr. Shultz said then that Mr. Zhang took part in all the Peking meetings and would be warmly received in Washington. 
Mr. Zhang, 68 years old, who is expected to present his credentials to President Reagan in a White House ceremony soon, speaks English and was formerly Ambassador to Canada and to Pakistan.
In July 1971 he flew with Henry A. Kissinger, then President Nixon's national security adviser, on his secret mission from Pakistan to Peking. 
That trip paved the way for Mr. Nixon's journey to Peking in February 1972 and the breakthrough in relations between the two countries.
