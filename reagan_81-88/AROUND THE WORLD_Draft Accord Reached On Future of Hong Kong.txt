
After two years of negotiations on the future of Hong Kong, Britain and China have completed a draft agreement on how Peking will regain sovereignty over the British colony in 1997, the British Embassy announced today.
Negotiators meeting in Peking worked out the final details of the draft agreement on the sovereignty transfer, to coincide with the expiration of Britain's 99-year lease on the colony.
British Embassy in Peking announces that after 2 years of negotiations Britain and China have completed draft agreement on how Peking will regain sovereignty over Hong Kong in 1997 (S)
''Draft texts have now been submitted by delegations to Governments on both sides for consideration,'' an embassy statement said. 
The way is now clear for the agreement to be initialed next week.
China has pledged to maintain Hong Kong's capitalist system for 50 years after 1997. 
Hong Kong will become a ''special administrative region.''
The final disputed issues were land sales, nationality of residents and aviation rights.
No firm date has been set for the final, 23rd round of formal talks between teams led by the British Ambassador, Sir Richard Evans, and China's Assistant Foreign Minister, Zhou Nan.
The Governor in Hong Kong, Sir Edward Youde, is discussing the draft text with Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher in London.
There was no immediate confirmation by the Chinese that the draft agreement was reached.
