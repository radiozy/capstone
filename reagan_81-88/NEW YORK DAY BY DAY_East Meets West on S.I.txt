An electronic version of pen pals may be set up between students in China and at Tottenville High School on Staten Island.
Thirty Chinese visitors went to the school yesterday to see how an American high school operates, and they expressed enthusiasm for the exchange. 
They included 13 people from the consulate in New York City, a third secretary from the embassy in Washington and members of the Foundation for American-Chinese Cultural Exchanges. 
Some of the school's Chinese-American students interpreted.
Tottenville, with 4,300 students, is the city's fourth largest public high school. 
Ji Lide, China's deputy consul general, expressed surprise at finding academic and vocational programs under one roof.
The school's records are fully computerized, and three computer languages are offered, along with French, Spanish, Italian and Latin. 
In the school's television studio, two Chinese visitors took turns behind the cameras.
Besides English-language schoolbooks from China, the visitors brought lunch, with three main courses, including roast duck. 
Home economics students at Tottenville added brownies and cream puffs.
