
The American Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals urged Chinese officials yesterday to stop the mass killing of dogs, saying ''a caring society provides supports for its unfortunate human beings and support for its unfortunate animals.''
John Kullberg, the society's executive director, sent a letter to Zhang Wenjan, China's Ambassador to the United States, sharply criticizing a ban on dog ownership in Peking that was scheduled to become effective today.

''A decision to ban pets from the human condition is the first step of a decision to ban elements of the human population itself: the young, the deformed, the diseased and the old,'' the letter said.
Teams of Chinese have drowned and clubbed to death about 200,000 dogs in a drive to rid Peking of the animals, an official in the Chinese capital said Tuesday. 
He said any remaining dogs on the streets would be ''killed on the spot.''
Officials said the ban was for health and safety reasons.
Mr. Kullberg suggested a more ''enlightened'' approach. 
''Shelters are necessary in urban environments to care for animals when an excess of animals is the result of an irresponsible public attitude toward animals,'' he said.
Killing the animals should be ''a last resort'' and ''should be done only the most human and caring way possible, by injection of sodium pentobarbital,'' Mr. Kullberg said.
