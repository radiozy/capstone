
China today revealed a financial scandal involving the highest officials of the island of Hainan, off the south coast.
According to a report made public by the New China News Agency, the Hainan officials took advantage of the open-door economic policies to embezzle a sum equivalent to $1.5 billion, only half of which has been recovered.

According to the Chinese press agency, the Hainan officials used money siphoned from local banks to finance the import of foreign luxury goods. 
These were then resold at a profit, which was then divided among the officials.
Th list of imported goods, which brought gasps from viewers when read over the television news, included 89,000 motor vehicles, 2.9 million television sets, 252,000 video recorders and 122,000 motorcycles, all in a 14-month period ending in March.
The report said at least three leading Hainan officials had already been dismissed in the scandal, with 143 criminal cases under investigation.
The only official who was identified as having been dismissed was Lei Yu, a Hainan deputy party secretary and head of the island government, which is part of Guangdong Province.
Two other high officials - Yao Wenxu, the Hainan party secretary, and Chen Yuyi, another deputy party secretary and a deputy head of the island government - were said to have been given ''serious warnings.''
