
The Government announced plans today for the sale of up to $3.3 billion in treasury bonds this year, the first such bonds issued in China since the 1950's.
Purchases of the bonds will be dictated by Peking authorities and will be compulsory for selected state-owned factories, local governments, army units and wealthy communes and production brigades, according to the Ministry of Finance.
Government of China will issue up to $3.3 billion in treasury bonds, first issue since 1950
Mandatory bond purchases will sharply curtail the amount of fiscal freedom given to various factories and regional administrations in the last two years to develop and invest as they saw fit. 
With their profits siphoned off by the central Government bonds, some production units will undoubtedly have to curtail expansion plans, as well as profit-sharing and other incentives to workers.
The New China News Agency said that the Finance Ministry had authorized bonds with face values ranging from 10 yuan, equal to about $6.67, to 1 million yuan, or about $667,000, carrying an annual interest rate of 4 percent and repayable in installments beginning in 1987 and to be completed by 1990.
 
Inflation at Double-Digit Rate
The Finance Ministry said that some local governments had more money than they could usefully spend. 
Rather than let them spend it, the authorities intend, through the bond sales, to funnel the excess money into the central coffers in Peking.
With inflation running into double digits - the official estimate is 5.8 percent a year but officials concede that rates are three times that high in some urban areas - the 4 percent bonds are no bargain. 
That is why the Government made their purchase voluntary only for individual citizens and overseas Chinese and for foreigners living and working in China.
