
Greg Louganis became the first man to earn two diving gold medals in consecutive Olympics when he won a dramatic battle with Xiong Ni today by overtaking the Chinese teen-ager on the final dive of the 10-meter platform competition.
The 28-year-old Louganis, who won his first Olympic medal when Xiong was 2 years old, needed 85.57 points on the last attempt to pass the 14-year-old, who pushed the competition to the limit with an outstanding 82.56 on the next-to-last dive of the day.
Louganis did a reverse 3 1/2-somersault in the tuck position. 
Its degree of difficulty is 3.4, the highest in platform diving. 
He received 86.70 points for a winning margin of 1.14 points, then rushed into the tight embrace of the American diving coach, Ron O'Brien, and burst into tears.
Louganis finished with 638.61 points to 637.47 for Xiong. 
A week ago, Louganis won the springboard event one day after he banged his head on the board and needed five stitches to close the gash.
 
'Had to Hit Last Dive'
''I knew I had to hit the last dive,'' Louganis said. 
''I knew it was going to be tight. 
Like in '84, I reminded myself that my mother's still going to love me no matter what happens, which makes it easier. 
I think this is the closest finish I've been in, in a major international competition.''
Louganis said he probably would compete again, but might not dive in any more world-class events.
In the 10th and final round, Xiong had tried an inward 3 1/2-somersault in the tuck position with a 3.2 degree of difficulty.
''I felt the pressure was very great going into his last dive,'' said Xiong. 
''I thought that if I didn't do it well, I would not walk away with the gold medal. 
I know I'm very young. 
It's an honor for me to compete with Louganis. 
I always considered him one of my idols.''
Xiong went into the 10th round with 554.91 points. 
Louganis had 551.91. 
In the round, Xiong received two 9.0s, four 8.5's and one 7.0 from the seven-judge panel. 
Louganis's scores included one 9.0, five 8.5's and one 8.0.
 
Another Record
Louganis, who won a platform silver medal in 1976, also matched the record of Klaus Dibiasi of Italy, the only other diver to win five Olympic medals.
''That was probably the biggest dive of his career, the most pressure, the most difficult,'' O'Brien said. 
''To hit it like that, under those circumstances certainly proved that he was a champion.''
Louganis had taken the lead in the seventh round with an inward 3 1/2-somersault in the tuck position that moved him from third.
He was awarded 82.56 points for the dive, compared with 69.12 for the same dive in that round in Monday's preliminaries which also moved him into first place. 
Xiong moved from fourth to second.
But in the eighth round, Xiong outperformed Louganis as they switched places in the standing. 
Xiong received scores ranging from 7.5 to 9.0 for a reverse 2 1/2-somersault in the pike position, while Louganis's scores ranged from 6.5 to 8.5 for a forward 3 1/2-somersault in the pike position.
With two rounds left, it was Xiong with 470.76 points and Louganis with 468.75. 
Jesus Mena of Mexico was third with 449.43.
In the ninth round, Xiong again outscored Louganis, 84.15 to 83.16.
Louganis first moved from second to first in Tuesday's third round. 
He fell to fourth in the fifth round on a dive that had a relatively low 2.6 degree of difficulty.
 
Another Medal Battle
In another fight to nearly the finish, Mena won the bronze medal with 594.39 points. 
Going into the last round, Mena had 520.47 and Jan Hempel of East Germany 520.41.
Mena's last dive, a backward 1 1/2-somersault with 3 1/2 twists, earned 73.92 points. 
Hempel received 63.36 for 583.77 and dropped to fifth behind Gueorgui Tchogovadze of the Soviet Union with 585.96.
Mena's bronze medal broke a stranglehold the Chinese and Americans had on diving medals. 
In the first three events, China had two gold medals, two silvers and one bronze. 
The United States had one gold, one silver and two bronze.
The other American, Patrick Jeffrey of Madison, N.J., finished 12th and last with 483.54 points after coming in sixth in Monday's qualifying.
Li Kongzheng of China, the 1984 bronze medalist, had been in second heading into the fifth round but plunged all the way to 11th on the same dive Louganis tried, an armstand cut-through to a reverse 1 1/2-somersault. 
Li had trouble steadying his armstand, then landed short of the vertical plane. 
Li finished sixth with 543.81 points. 
Halfway through the 10-dive competition, Tchogovadze led with 260.82 points and was followed by Hempel with 257.19, Xiong with 255.96 and Louganis with 247.95.
But Louganis seemed in good shape with higher degrees of difficulty than the three midpoint leaders in the last five rounds.
He has 47 national titles, finished lower than second just seven times in 143 meets since 1975 and has been on every United States Olympic team since 1976, although he didn't compete in Moscow in 1980 because of the American boycott.
