
The Chinese Government said today that it had cut the overall crime rate in late 1983 by just under 40 percent through emergency measures enacted to end a nationwide wave of lawlessness.
''The masses' feeling of security has been strengthened,'' a report in the official weekly newspaper China Legal System said.

Special laws announced last August by the ruling Communist Party gave the police more power to search for suspects and permitted judges to order the death penalty for offenders ''who cause grave harm to the public.''
Besides killers and rapists, that category can include those who embezzle, bribe, defraud or commit other nonviolent crimes.
The newspaper said that the overall number of criminal cases dropped by 39.8 percent in September compared with the year-earlier period and by 45.7 percent in October and 42.1 percent in November.
