
The propaganda chief of the Chinese Communist Party has been forced to resign, apparently after losing a power struggle over the recent ''spiritual pollution'' campaign, a diplomatic source said today.
Deng Liqun, 68 years old, lost his job as head of the party's propaganda department, the source said, but retains membership in the party secretariat.

There was no official confirmation of Mr. Deng's ouster from the key propaganda post, but two independent sources, one a Western diplomat, reported the development. 
They spoke on condition they not be identified.
Speculation about Mr. Deng's future began in January when the campaign begun last October against spiritual pollution was curtailed.
Originally encompassing Western influences ranging from pornography and pop music to the existentialist works of Jean-Paul Sartre, the campaign alarmed intellectuals, who feared another campaign like the Cultural Revolution from 1966 to 1976. 
But the party's General Secretary, Hu Yaobang, intervened by saying the campaign should be confined to literary and ideological circles.
It was also announced that the drive would not be extended to the countryside, where 80 percent of China's 1 billion people live, nor would it affect scientific and technological fields. 
There is now little reference to spiritual pollution in the official Chinese press and broadcasting.
The leftist Hong Kong magazine Cheng Ming speculated that Mr. Deng had tried to use the campaign to discredit Mr. Hu and oust him from the post leadership.
