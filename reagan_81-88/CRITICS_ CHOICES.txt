
Broadcast TV
''Nova'' offers an extraordinary look at China on Tuesday at 8 P.M. How you interpret what you see - as appalling, sad or enormously civilized - will depend on your cultural, religious and even political persuasions. 
The ''Nova'' program, ''China's Only Child,'' examines Peking's most ambitious attempt to control over- population: its policy of allowing, or forcing - the choice of verb is arguable - each Chinese family to have but a single child.
The public-television program will be seen on Channels 13 and 49.
China, ''Nova'' tells us, has 1.2 billion people, two-thirds of them under the age of 24. 
If the birth rate is not reversed, China will have 200 million more people by the year 2000, and with them the possibility of famine. 
The ''Nova'' documentary, originally produced by the BBC, examines family-planning in Changzhou, a kind of Chinese model city. 
The documentary glides over what one suspects are the great personal traumas for many Chinese, but as a revealing look at Chinese society, it is as intimate a piece of work as we are likely to see on television.
