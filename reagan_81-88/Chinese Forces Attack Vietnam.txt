
Chinese troops launched a fresh attack on Vietnam's positions along their common border today, but the action appeared to fall short of the wider offensive that Peking has threatened over Vietnam's actions in Cambodia.
The official New China News Agency announced that troops in the southwestern province of Yunnan had ''hit back at Vietnamese armed provocation'' along the frontier, ''clearing all Vietnamese invaders and their advancing strongholds.''

The two-paragraph report gave no details, beyond claiming that the Vietnamese had caused ''great losses of lives and property'' by firing thousands of shells into Yunnan over a 10-day period.
From that bare account it appeared that today's action fell into the pattern of cautious Chinese military pressure on Vietnam in recent months. 
While Vietnamese troops have been conducting their most successful offensive in six years against Chinese-backed guerrillas in Cambodia, Peking has mounted a series of limited strikes along its own frontier with Vietnam.
