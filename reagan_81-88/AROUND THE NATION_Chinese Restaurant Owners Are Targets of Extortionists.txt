
Gangs from New York and Chicago have been linked to attempts to extort money from owners of suburban Chinese restaurants, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation is investigating, the police said today.
No payments have been made, but at least five restaurant owners in suburban Oakland County have received extortion demands, according to officials.
Five Chinese restaurant owners in Detroit (Mich) receive extortion demands from unnamed sourcesThe attempts are considered a hoax by some Detroitarea Chinese, but some merchants have applied for gun permits and want bulletproof vests, police said.
Law-enforcement agencies are concerned that bloodshed could erupt, as it has in New York, San Francisco and Chicago. 
''The line they're using is that, unless the money is paid, the restaurant will be shot up,'' an Oakland County police official said.
Five customers were killed and 11 others wounded in July 1977 when a Chinese gang sprayed automatic rifle fire into a restaurant in San Francisco's Chinatown.
According to area Chinese leaders, restaurant owners were contacted by a person speaking ''perfect Cantonese'' who demanded payments of $50 to $100 a week. 
The demands were reported in the Oakland County cities of Southfield, Birmingham, Farmington Hills and Madison Heights.
