
Several rare golden monkeys from China will visit the Minnesota Zoo next summer as a gesture of cooperation between the United States and Chinese Governments.
The zoo had hoped to persuade the Chinese to send some giant pandas to Minnesota, but China instead suggested the monkeys, among China's most endangered primates.

They have fur coats that can grow as long as 12 inches. 
The males have a distinctive bright blue coloring around their eyes. 
The monkeys' bodies are about 24 inches long, and their tails are just as long.
''They're so strikingly different from most monkeys that we're familiar with,'' said John Lewis, director of the zoo's biological programs. 
''Their golden color is so gorgeous, and they've got a real pug nose. 
They have an odd sound to them, like a cat meowing.''
No golden monkeys are on permanent display anywhere in the United States.
