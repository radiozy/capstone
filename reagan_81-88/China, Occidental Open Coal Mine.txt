
China, the world's second-largest coal producer, after the United States, has officially opened a new coal mine developed in a $650 million joint venture with the Occidental Petroleum Corporation of Los Angeles. 
The oil company, which contributed $200 million to the venture, said it planned to begin talks on tripling the mine's production capacity.
Occidental, which has drilled unsuccessfully for offshore oil near China, is expected to earn about $40 million on the coal venture, analysts said. 
The deal may be more significant for positioning Occidental for future natural resource ventures, they added. 
The mine, southwest of Beijing, is expected to produce 12 million tons of coal annually, with two-thirds of it being exported for use in electric generating plants in Japan and other Southeast Asian countries.
