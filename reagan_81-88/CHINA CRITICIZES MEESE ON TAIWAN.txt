
China sharply criticized a high-ranking White House official today for remarks he made Tuesday about United States relations with Taiwan.
The criticism of the official, Edwin Meese 3d, Counselor to the President, was followed by Peking's formal announcement of a visit to China by Secretary of State Alexander M. Haig Jr., which the State Department said yesterday would take place late next month.
Chinese Government criticizes Presidential aide Edwin Meese for remarks he made concerning US relations with Taiwan
A report by the official New China News Agency took exception to a statement by Mr. Meese that the United States stands by the 1979 Taiwan Relations Act, legislation that Peking finds repugnant.
The agency quoted Mr. Meese as telling reporters from Taiwan on Tuesday that Washington remained committed to the act, including its provisions allowing arms sales to Taiwan and allowing certain Taiwan officials into United States Government offices.
''Meese's remarks, which run counter to the basic principles laid down in the communique on the establishment of Sino-American relations, cannot but arouse serious concern among the Chinese people,'' the agency said.
China regards the Taiwan Relations Act as incompatible with the United States-China normalization accord concluded during the Carter Administration. 
Washington maintains that the two can complement each other.
China is particularly sensitive about arms sales and recently downgraded relations with the Netherlands after the Dutch Government agreed to allow the sale of two submarines to Taiwan.

Reaction by State Department
WASHINGTON, May 14 (AP) - The State Department said today that the remarks by Mr. Meese did not imply that the United States has an official relationship with Taiwan.
