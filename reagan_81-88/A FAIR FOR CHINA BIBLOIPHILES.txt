
China's first international book fair opened today in Peking, offering something for bookworms of all tastes - if they could get in.
Browsers who managed to obtain passes could skim 50,000 titles ranging from the Encyclopedia Britannica in Chinese - 10 volumes at $4 apiece - to such children's books as ''Little Panda Loses His Way.''
No books are for sale, but officials said Chinese buyers could place orders for their organizations through the national book importer.
The officials did not say how passes were distributed. 
But for those who obtained one, the weeklong fair was a bibliophile's dream since foreign books are rarely available and extremely expensive in China.
Most of the books are in English, although some of the cramped publishers' stalls at the Peking Exhibition Hall featured titles in French, Russian and other languages.
''I'm actually a fan of English literature,'' said a man leafing through Louis L'Amour's western novel ''Ride the River,'' ''but this is great stuff, too.''
''This place is amazing,'' said a woman whose taste ran to spy novels as she peered down aisles laden with copies of ''Wild California,'' a nature book; ''The Fannie Farmer Cookbook,'' and Edgar Snow's ''Red Star Over China.''
Chinese officials said they planned to hold similar fairs every two years to promote global friendship and the exchange of information. 
They said this was the first such fair in China since the 1949 Communist revolution.
At a news conference last week, the officials said they would screen all the books - 70 percent of them on technical topics - before the fair opened to make sure none contained material insulting to other countries; they did not elaborate.
A total of 1,055 publishing houses from 35 countries were taking part in the fair, with most coming from the United States, the officials said.
