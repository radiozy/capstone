
American officials reported increasing concern today about rising tension between India and China as a result of a buildup of Indian and Chinese troops in a disputed, mountainous border region near Bhutan. 
The State Department has urged both sides to keep the situation calm, officials said.
The tense atmosphere developed when China began sending additional troops into the area about two weeks ago, a State Department official said. 
Then India followed suit. 
Since last weekend, the situation has become ''more threatening,'' he said, as more soldiers have been moved in, along with logistical support, to make a total of about 30,000 troops on both sides, according to American estimates.
Most of those troops were in secondary lines of defense, one official said, with relatively few facing each other on the ridge line that marks the border.
The region, which was fought over by China and India in 1962, is dominated by mountains 14,000 to 16,000 feet high, which preclude mechanized warfare. 
One specialist said only foot soldiers can fight there, and extensive combat is possible only during short periods of the year because of the weather.
This is one of those periods, shortly after the snow has ended and before the monsoon rains begin. 
A longer period of suitable weather follows the end of the rains, in late summer. 
That is when the 1962 battles were fought.
The United States has made diplomatic approaches to both India and China in an effort to dissuade them from attacking each other, a State Department official said. 
But Washington has little influence on the issue with either country.
