
Gao Min and Li Qing, teen-age symbols of China's rise toward diving supremacy, won Olympic gold and silver medals in women's springboard competition today.
Kelly McCormick won the bronze in a battle with Li that went to the next-to-last of the day's 120 dives.
Gao, 18 years old and the only woman to score more than 600 points in a springboard event, led after each of the last five rounds of the 10-dive event and won comfortably with 580.23. 
Li, 15, had to sweat out her silver.
McCormick, 28, already had received 66.99 points for her final dive, a reverse 1 1/2-somersault with 2 1/2 twists, with a 2.9 degree of difficulty, and had finished with 533.19.
Li needed a strong dive on a back 2 1/2-somersault in the pike position with a 3.0 degree of difficulty to hang on to second and she did. 
Barely. She received scores ranging from 6.5 to 7.5 of a possible 10 from the seven judges to get 64.80 points and edge McCormick with 534.33. 
Chinese and Americans Dominate The Chinese and Americans continued their domination of diving.
In the platform event last Sunday, Xu Yanmei of China won the gold medal and the Americans Michele Mitchell and Wendy Williams finished second and third. 
In the men's springboard last Tuesday, Greg Louganis of the United States won and was followed by Tan Liangde and Li Deliang.
The remaining diving event, the men's platform, is scheduled for Monday and Tuesday.
Today, McCormick, of Long Beach, Calif., stubbornly fought four teen-agers throughout the day. 
Irina Lachko, 15, and Marina Babkova, 18, both of the Soviet Union, finished fourth and fifth. 
Lachko had 526.65 points and Babkova 506.43.
The other American, Wendy Lucero of Denver, was sixth with 498.81.
In the ninth round, McCormick dropped from second to fourth, behind Gao, Li and Lachko.
McCormick received scores ranging from 6.5 to 7.5 of a possible 10 from the seven judges for a reverse 2 1/2-somersault in the pike position. 
Lachko's scores ranged from 7.0 to 8.0 for the same dive and Li nailed a reverse 2 1/2-somersault in the tuck position and received all 8.0's and 8.5's.
After the first five rounds, in which the total degree of difficulty was limited to 9.5, Li led with 233.46 points. 
Gao was second with 227.82 and McCormick third with 223.83. 
Lachko was a close fourth at 223.53 and Lucero fifth at 218.25.
There was no limit on the difficulty of the dives in the last five rounds.
In the sixth round, Gao moved from second to first and McCormick from third to second, while Li fell from the top to third.
But McCormick faltered in the next round when she did a backward 2 1/2-somersault in the pike position with a 3.0 degree of difficulty. 
Her scores ranged from a relatively low 6.0 to 7.5.
