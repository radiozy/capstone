
Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi left India today for a five-day state visit to China, the first by an Indian head of government since 1954.
Mr. Gandhi said the most important topic of his discussions in Beijing would be the two countries' longstanding border dispute, which included a monthlong war in 1962.
China routed Indian troops and then announced a unilateral cease-fire.Diplomatic relations were severed until 1976, with the exchange of ambassadors and visits by Chinese and Indian Foreign Ministers to New Delhi and Beijing respectively.
Indian diplomats said this week that though it would be ''unrealistic'' to expect a solution to major Chinese-Indian problems, they expected a significant improvement in relations.
Mr. Gandhi is to hold extensive talks with Chinese officials, including Prime Minister Li Peng, and the border dispute is said to be central to the negotiations. 
India asserts that China is in illegal possession of 14,000 square miles of territory near Kashmir state, including 2,000 square miles that it captured in the 1962 war.
Cambodia and India's role in seeking an end to the clashes between pro- and anti-Vietnamese forces there are also expected to figure in the discussions.
