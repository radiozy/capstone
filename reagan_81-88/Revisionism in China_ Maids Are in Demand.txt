
Domestic service, once reviled as exploitation, is making a comeback in Peking as living standards rise and family life styles change.
It is also promoted as a partial answer to rising unemployment, even though Peking newspapers do not exactly say so because unemployment officially does not exist in China.

Officials say they do not know how many people are employed as maids, but Peking's first contract maid service alone has found jobs for 145 women since it was established Dec. 
21. The newspaper Economic Daily said the number of maids in Peking had tripled since 1966 and the demand for domestic help had exceeded the supply since the Housework Service Company was established.
Economic Daily said 28.6 percent of the families with maids were ''ordinary workers, a big change from the old phenomenon whereby only senior officials and intellectuals employed maids.''
