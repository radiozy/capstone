
China complained yesterday that Moody's Investors Service assigned too low a rating when the nation issued its first international bonds last October.
Moody's rated the 300-million-mark issue A-3, or lower than the Aa rating that Chinese officials said it deserved. 
The newspaper China Daily quoted a Finance Ministry spokesman in Beijing as saying it was ''unbelievable'' that China's bonds were rated lower than those of India and South Korea. 
Chinese officials said Moody's analysts were in their country only eight days.
Stephen Taran, a senior analyst at Moody's, said, ''It is our policy not to comment on the reaction of issuers to our ratings.''
