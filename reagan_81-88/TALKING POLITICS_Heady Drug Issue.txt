
The issue of illegal drugs has got so out of hand in the United States Senate race in Florida that the Coast Guard has answered a political distress signal.
Paul A. Yost, Commandant of the Coast Guard, has asked Gov. Bob Graham, the Democratic senatorial candidate, to withdraw a television commercial showing Mr. Graham conversing with Coast Guard personnel about Federal budget cuts that have hampered their operations against drug smuggling.
The commandant said in a letter to the Governor that the commercial, which he described as ''both improper and misleading,'' had dragged the Coast Guard into partisan politics.
The Graham campaign refused to withdraw the commercial, which criticizes his Democratic opponent, Senator Paula Hawkins, for voting for the budget cuts, and charged that it was Mrs. Hawkins who had drawn the Coast Guard into politics. 
Coast Guard officials acknowledged that Mrs. Hawkins, among others, had complained about the commercial, but they denied that she had applied political pressure.
Meanwhile, the Hawkins campaign is refusing to withdraw a television commercial that her aides concede is factually incorrect. 
The narration says Mrs. Hawkins ''travels to China and meets personally with Chinese leader Deng Xiaoping'' to stop the export of the drug methaqualone.
Hawkins aides have acknowledged that the Senator and Mr. Deng never discussed drugs when they met at a state dinner in 1981. 
Nor did the Chinese leader respond to her letter on the subject in 1982. 
Details aside, they say the point of the commercial is to show that Mrs. Hawkins is no newcomer to the drug issue.
Correction
Because of an editing error, the Talking Politics column yesterday misstated the political affiliation of Senator Paula Hawkins of Florida. 
She is a Republican.
Correction-Date: October 9, 1986, Thursday, Late City Final Edition
