
Chinese officials told the State
Department today that China
now intended to meet its obligation to buy a minimum of 6 million metric tons of grain from the
United States this year, according to American officials. 
Peking had indicated earlier
this month that it would purchase no more than 5 million
tons, despite a commitment to
buy between 6 million and 8
million tons of wheat and corn
annually through 1984.

China has traditionally linked
its purchases of grain with
American purchases of Chinese textiles. 
One American official speculated that the Chinese were
indirectly seeking to put pressure on the Commerce Department to reject American textile
industry demands for higher
duties on textile imports.
