Hotels
Even though new hotels seem to sprout every month and the variety is great, one is well advised to have a reservation. 
Of course, there usually is a vacant room a few miles north or south or east, even if locating it by telephone and then getting there cause a few gray hairs. 
But it isn't like being in the city.
The famous hotels deserve their imposing reputations. 
The Fairmont (415-772-5000), the Stanford Court (415-989-3500), the Four Seasons Clift (415-775-4700), the Hyatt Regency (415-788-1234), the Mark Hopkins (415-392-3434), the Huntington (415-474-5400) and others provide luxury that anyone could grow to love. 
Prices run about $65 for the least expensive single room to $190 for a room with twin beds.
A step down is still luxurious. 
Rooms at the San Franciscan (800-227-3050) are $95 top for two. 
The Westin Miyako (800-228-3000), with a Japanese flavor, has a $115 top. 
The Chancellor (415-362-2004) has a maximum rate of $55, and the El Cortez (800-223-5695) has kitchenettes at $50. 
There are even less expensive places. 
Bed and Breakfast International (415-525-4569) specializes in referrals.
Anyone planning to a visit cannot go wrong by writing the San Francisco Convention & Visitors Bureau, P.O. 
Box 6977, San Francisco 94010, and asking for the ''San Francisco Lodging Guide.''
 
Restaurants
First, let us understand the dark side of many San Francisco restaurants. 
Some close on Sunday. 
Outside of that I can think of nothing about them to criticize, except that a few will not take reservations.
Ernie's, 847 Montgomery Street (415-397-5969), is a truly splendid place where the captains and waiters come from central casting and the food comes from heaven. 
Others in this category are the Blue Fox, 659 Merchant Street (415-981-1177), La Bourgogne, 330 Mason Street (415-362-7352), L'Etoile, 1075 California Street (415-771-1529), or the Carnelian Room, atop the Bank of America World Headquarters at 555 California Street (415-433-7500). 
This is by no means the full list of top-drawer French and Continental restaurants where two people can scarcely feed themselves decently for under $75.
At Bardelli's, 243 O'Farrell Street (415-982-0243), and Alfred's, a steakhouse at 886 Broadway (415-781-7058), and similar places, two can dine well for $50 or $60. 
The Convention & Visitors Bureau will give you a copy of ''Restaurant and Night Life Guide.''
On those occasions when you have a group of eight or 10 - they will want to talk across and down the table during dinner - I always call on Jack's, a French restaurant, 615 Sacramento Street (415-986-9854), which has upstairs rooms of different sizes. 
You can order off the menu, and escape the fixed menu that you get saddled with even at Ernie's or the Blue Fox, both of which have basement rooms for large parties.
There are any number of Chinese restaurants, ranging in style from a half-dozen stools at a counter to places seating a hundred or so. 
The Chinese cuisine comes from all across Asia, a development of the last 20 years as Chinese from many nations have come into San Francisco. 
My favorite Cantonese restaurant is the Imperial Palace, Tommy Toy proprietor, at 919 Grant Avenue, (415-982-4440). 
The bill for four of us a month ago was $135. 
Hunan Village, 839 Kearny Street (415-956-7868), is less expensive. 
The Mandarin, at 900 North Point in Ghirardelli Square, offers excellent North China cuisine, and it takes reservations (415-673-8812), which Hunan Village does not.
There are several Japanese places, such as Yamato, 717 California Street (415-397-3456), and Nikko, 1450 Van Ness Avenue (415-474-7722).
There are Italian restaurants all around San Francisco, because the city has a big population descended from the fishermen and farmers who came from Italy a century ago. 
North Beach Restaurant, 1512 Stockton Street (415-392-1700), is northern Italian. 
Two people can eat for $40 to $50. 
Original Joe's, 144 Taylor Street (415-775-4877), is less expensive, and in the tomato sauce style.
Seafood is something special in San Francisco and generally I go to Fisherman's Wharf, straight to Castagnola's, 286 Jefferson Street (415-776-5015). 
Sometimes the sea lions will come into the Wharf, and if you have a window table you can watch them beg for scrap fish at the fish buyer's dock. 
There are other great places at the Wharf - Alioto's No. 8 Wharf (415-673-0183), and Scoma's on Pier 47 (415-771-4383), which is behind Castagnola's. 
Downtown, one of the best is Tadich Grill, 240 California (415-391-2373). 
Another is Sam's Grill, 374 Bush (415-421-0594).
 
Transportation
The cable cars are closed this year, to reopen in the summer of 1984 at the earliest. 
The system was about to fall apart from pure exhaustion.
However, San Francisco has an excellent system of buses, electric trolley coaches and streetcars called the San Francisco Municipal Railway. 
The fare is 60 cents, 25 cents for children under 17, five cents for the disabled or people over 65, who should be prepared to show a passport or drivers license or some other document, if the driver asks proof of age. 
The information telephone for the Muni, as the system is called, is 415-673-6864. 
It is attended 24 hours, seven days a week.
Even more good news is the weekend and holiday operation of 10 vintage streetcars on Market Street, beginning June 24 and running through Sept. 
26. The old cars have been gathered from Portugal, England, Melbourne, Milwaukee, Portland, Ore., and from the Muni's own back lot, which produced cars dating from 1912. 
During the week, the regular streetcars operate in a subway, but the old tracks and trolley wires have never been taken down from the surface and the San Francisco Chamber of Commerce helped the Muni put the streetcar package together as an answer to the loss of the cable cars.
Regional public transit is good, too. 
For Oakland, Berkeley, Richmond, San Leandro, Newark and Fremont, all across the Bay, you may use Bay Area Rapid Transit's trains (BART), or the AC Transit buses. 
BART schedules can be had by calling 415-788-2278, or AC Transit at 415-653-3535. 
Cities north of the Golden Gate Bridge in Marin and Sonoma county are served by the Golden Gate Bus system. 
The district also operates ferries to Sausalito and Larkspur. 
Information on all this can be obtained by calling 415-453-2100.
To go south down the San Francisco Peninsula to Burlingame, San Mateo, Palo Alto, or San Jose, call the Southern Pacific Railroad's commute schedule number, 415-495-4546, or the San Mateo County Transit District bus lines at 415-761-7000.
 
Tours
You can go on a tour to see or do almost anything around San Francisco, running typically around $20 a person. 
There are luncheon cruises (415-861-2165, China Basin Charter) and cocktail-dinner cruises (415-540-8352, San Francisco's Love Boat). 
There are helicopter tours (415-495-3333, China Basin tours) and you may trudge along in the steps of one of the great mystery writers by paying $4 to go on the Dashiell Hammett Tours (415-564-7021). 
Someone will even lead you through the War Memorial Opera House, the Louise M. Davies Symphony Hall and the Herbst Theatre on Monday from 10 A.M. to 2:30 P.M. or Wednesday at 1:30 and 2:30 P.M., all for $3.
There are horse-drawn buggies in Golden Gate Park (415-761-8272) or on Pier 39 (415-398-0857). 
There are city tours for an hour or so or all day. 
Gray Line (415-771-4000) and Dolphin Tours (415-441-6810) offer a broad range, with Gray Line ready to supply a Bay cruise and double decker bus, while Dolphin will take you to Muir Woods redwood grove and Sausalito. 
Lorrie's Travel & Tours (415-826-5950) offers Alcatraz Island, the long-vacant federal prison.
Upward of 20 percent of San Francisco's population is Oriental, which gives special meaning to the Chinatown and Japantown offerings of Dragon Tours (415-392-0244) and the Chinese Cultural Center (415-986-1822). 
Ding How Tours (415-981-8399) will take you through Chinatown and serve you dinner.
To the north, south and east of San Francisco Bay are the vineyards and wineries that produce the nation's premier vintages. 
There is an immense variety of wine tours, and the best way to pick the one you want is to get a copy of ''Visitors Guide to the Wine Country'' from the San Francisco Convention & Visitors Bureau (415-974-6900). 
The bureau maintains an office at Halliday Plaza, that sunken area where Powell Street runs into Market and where the Muni and BART trains stop.
