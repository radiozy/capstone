
The Reagan Administration called today for Senate ratification of the first treaty to be negotiated with China since full diplomatic relations between Peking and Washington were established in 1979.
The Administration hailed the treaty, a consular convention negotiated by the Carter Administration and signed last Sept. 
17, as an important symbol of the cooperative relationship being developed with China.
Reagan Administration urges Senate to ratify consular convention treaty with China
The appeal for the treaty by John H. Holdridge, Assistant Secretary of State for East Asian and Pacific Affairs, came less than a week before Secretary of State Alexander M. Haig Jr. flies to Peking as part of an Asian tour.
Mr. Holdridge, testifying at a hearing of a Senate Foreign Relations subcommittee, said that the convention was the most comprehensive such agreement ever concluded by China with another country.
 
Growth of Travel Seen
He said the convention would help United States and Chinese consuls aid their fellow nationals and should contribute to the growth of travel and commerce between the two countries.
Under agreements attached to the convention, each country has the right to open three new consulates in addition to the two already established.
Mr. Holdridge said there was agreement in principle that the United States would establish consulates in Shenyang, Wuhan and Chengdu, while the Chinese would have new consulates in New York, Chicago and Honolulu.
He said there were now about 6,000 Chinese students, scholars and researchers in the United States, and about 6,500 business visas were issued to Chinese citizens last year.
Meanwhile, 60,000 to 70,000 American tourists visited China in 1980 and perhaps 100,000 would do so this year, he said. 
Mr. Holdridge said that China ranked just behind South Korea as America's third largest export market in Asia, importing $3.8 billion worth of goods and exporting $1.1 billion to the United States.
