
To the sound of gongs and the sight of Chinese calligraphy blazing out in neon, Bloomingdale's ushered in ''The Year of the Dragon'' on Wednesday night. 
The 7-week, 17-store promotion celebrates the 10th anniversary of Bloomingdale's resumption of trade with China and its ongoing trade relations with Hong Kong and Macao.
On Wednesday night, 700 people wandered through five floors filled with merchandise: clothing in satin, silk and brocade; porcelains and furnishings, many of them antiques and reproductions exported from China, Hong Kong and Macao exclusively for the promotion. 
The gala, a benefit for the Creo Society Fund for Children With AIDS, culminated on the fifth floor, where model rooms inspired by China's dynastic history had been created by the store's design staff.
At the sound of the dinner gong, which resounded throughout the store, dancers led the way to the dining room on the third floor. 
Gone was the merchandise. 
In its place stood a room empty save for dinner tables and barren clothes pegs that stood out like abstract art against the wall. 
Dramatic lighting accented the centerpieces: Chinese lanterns suspended from coral branches in black vases.
Between courses, Bloomingdale's chairman, Marvin S. Traub, introduced Lydia Dunn, the chairwoman of the Hong Kong Trade Development Council, who announced that the council was establishing a new scholarship at the Fashion Institute of Technology.
After coffee and dessert on the main floor, guests tried their luck at a casino on the second floor. 
All winnings from the tables were also donated to the Creo Society Fund.
