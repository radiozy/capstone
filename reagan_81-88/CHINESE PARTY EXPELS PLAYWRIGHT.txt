
A leading Chinese playwright said today that he had been pressed to quit the Communist Party, suggesting that he had become the latest target of hard-line dissatisfaction against those who have spoken out against party policy.
The playwright, Wu Zuguang, 70 years old, said a Politburo member, Hu Qiaomu, visited him Aug. 
1 and asked him to withdraw. 
Mr. Wu said he decided to comply, but said he did not expect to lose his post as vice president of the All-China Dramatist Association.
Chinese sources said at least two other intellectuals known for liberal views were being expelled from the party in what appeared to be a show of hard-line strength only two months before a Communist Party congress to lay down party policies.
The two are Wang Ruoshui, former deputy editor of the party's official People's Daily, and Su Shaozhi, director of the Academy of Social Sciences' Institute of Marxism-Leninism-Mao Zedong Thought.
 
'Bourgeois Liberalization'
The expulsions would be the first involving well-known artists and writers since early this year. 
At that time, Fang Lizhi, an astrophysicist, and two other men were ousted for supposedly fostering ''bourgeois liberalization,'' or the favoring of capitalist policies.
Hu Yaobang was removed as party chief in January on the ground that he had failed to stop a pro-Western, pro-capitalist trend.
