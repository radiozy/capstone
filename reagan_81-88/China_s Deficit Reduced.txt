
Soaring textile exports and rising prices for Chinese oil and gold have improved the country's foreign-trade performance this year.
The China Daily said today that textile exports were worth $3 billion in the first five months of 1987, up 40 percent from the corresponding period last year. 
It attributed the boom to expanding world demand and the improved quality and prices of Chinese textiles, with domestic producers using designs and brands provided by foreign partners. 
China posted a trade deficit of $1.7 billion in the first four months of 1987, down from a $4.5 billion deficit in the corresponding period last year. 
In 1985 and 1986, the country's combined deficit totaled nearly $26 billion.
