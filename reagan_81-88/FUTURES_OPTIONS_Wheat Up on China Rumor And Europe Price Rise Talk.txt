
Rumors of a plan by China to buy American wheat and information that the European Community plans to raise its wheat export prices initiated a rally in the prices of wheat futures today on the Chicago Board of Trade.
In other markets, corn prices advanced, soybean prices declined, cattle prices finished mostly higher, hog prices were mostly lower, frozen pork bellies fell, gold prices rose, silver ended lower and oil prices dipped.
Higher wheat export prices by the European Community ''would make theirs less competitive and ours more competitive,'' said Victor Lespinasse, a trader with Dean Witter Reynolds Inc. 
Rumors that the Soviet Union bought a million tons of corn also created a buying wave in the corn market, he said.
The analyst added that soybean prices were under pressure from pessimistic crop estimates from Sparks Commodities Inc., a private analyst.
Wheat prices settled 3.75 cents to 6.5 cents higher, with the contract for delivery in December at $4.3275 a bushel; corn was 0.05 cent to 4.5 cents higher, with the December contract at $2.98 a bushel; oats were 2 cents lower to 0.05 cent higher, with December at $2.46.25 a bushel, and soybeans were 10.25 cents lower to 2.75 cents higher, with November at $8.12.75 a bushel.
On the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, cattle prices rose. 
Traders bought on ideas that supplies will tighten up in the fourth quarter. 
They appeared to ignore the near-term fundamentals of lackluster sales of boxed beef and slow feed-yard trade - indications that demand is slight, said Philip Stanley, an analyst at Thompson McKinnon Securities Inc. in Chicago.
The hog market was influenced by lower cash hog prices, while pork belly futures were under pressure because of large stocks in storage, he said.
Cattle prices settled unchanged to 0.57 cent higher, with the October contract for delivery at 72.47 cents a pound; feeder cattle were 0.07 cent lower to 0.43 cent higher, with the October contract at 81.77 cents a pound; hogs were 0.38 cent lower to 0.10 cent higher, with October at 40.02 cents a pound, and pork bellies were 0.20 cent to 0.78 cent lower, with February at 48.67 cents a pound.
On the Commodity Exchange in New York, gold prices rallied. 
The metal finished $2.70 to $3.10 higher, with the October contract at $403.80 an ounce, and silver was 1 cent to 1.5 cents lower, with October at $6.288 an ounce.
