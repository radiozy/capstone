
A Chinese businessman lowered himself out a window of the 20-story Chinese Consulate in Manhattan early yesterday in what is being investigated as an attempt to seek political asylum here, authorities said.
The man, Zhang Zhenggao, a 46-year-old petroleum technician, was using a fire hose to lower himself when it gave way and he fell, breaking his left ankle. 
It was unclear last night how many stories he had fallen.
The Immigration and Naturalization Service was questioning Mr. Zhang yesterday after officials at St. Vincent's Hospital and Medical Center - where he was taken for treatment - said he had asked for asylum.
''I understand he is seeking political asylum,'' said Dan Sorrenti, the director of public relations at St. Vincent's. Immigration officials ''transported him to his room and they will be with him all night and tomorrow,'' Mr. Sorrenti said yesterday.
A spokesman for the Immigration and Naturalization Service in Washington, Duke Austin, would not say whether Mr. Zhang had asked for political asylum.
''We cannot confirm or deny when anyone asks for asylum until they go public,'' Mr. Austin said.
At 2:30 A.M. the police found Mr. Zhang on the roof of a one-story New York Telephone Company garage, next to the Chinese Consulate at 520 12th Avenue, at West 42d Street. 
He was taken to St. Vincent's where he underwent one and a half hours of surgery to reconstruct his left ankle.
A spokesman for the Chinese Consulate, Zhang Qinpu, said Mr. Zhang had arrived in New York Sunday with four other Chinese businessmen on a trade mission.
The spokesman said Mr. Zhang had stayed at the consulate, which houses 80 staff members and ''a few'' Chinese guests, Sunday night, had left the building for two nights and then returned Wednesday.
Mr. Austin, the immigration spokesman, said that Mr. Zhang had entered the United States in San Francisco on April 4 with a businessman's visa, and that the visa entitled him to stay until June 6.
