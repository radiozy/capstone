
More than 15,000 Chinese students now study in American universities, the highest number of foreign students from any nation, according to Han Xu, Chinese Ambassador to the United States.
Fudan University, in Shanghai, is trying to found a Center for American Studies on Chinese soil.
And it is turning to Americans to raise the money.
Professor Xie Xide, the university's president and an alumna of Smith College and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, came to New York recently to meet with friends and supporters at the Lotus Club, at 5 East 66th Street.
''The purpose of establishing the center,'' she said, ''is to bring scholars from China and the United States together. 
Also, we would like to bring professionals from all disciplines to carry out research so a new generation of teachers in U.S.-China relations can be trained.''
Professor Xie noted that there had been a two-generation gap in educating American specialists during the regime of Chairman Mao Zedong.
Professor Xie, who is 80 years old, also used her visit to meet with Friends of Fudan University, alumni who now live in the United States. 
One, a writer, Diana Hsu, is the mother of Hugh H. Mo, deputy commissioner of the New York City Police Department. 
Mr. Mo, who recently returned from a hero's welcome in China, also spoke about the importance of such a center.
''Ultimately the program would encourage the exchange of American students,'' he said, ''and the Chinese scholars would come to universities in this country.''
The fund-raising goal is $90,000, which includes a new building, and the Foundation for Educational Futures is helping raise the money.
