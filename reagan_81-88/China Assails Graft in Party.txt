
The Communist Party harshly criticized undisciplined members today and said they were helping to increase inflation and corruption that threaten economic change.
A front-page editorial in the party newspaper, the People's Daily, accused local party organizations of trying to profit illegally from inflation and short supplies and ignoring orders from Beijing to reduce spending.
''Those who don't abide by the party's discipline must be punished,'' the editorial warned. 
The harsh indictment was probably approved by the party's policy-making Central Committee, which began a private, five-day session Monday.
The session is expected to approve a two-year moratorium on a program phasing out price subsidies and to also endorse measures to control inflation and capital construction.
China's Supreme Court, meanwhile, ordered lower courts to punish officials found guilty of profiteering and speculation. 
It ordered an end to fines and said that instead criminal charges should be brought against those found culpable.
Inflation has soared from about 7 percent last year to more than 20 percent this year. 
Overspending by Government offices and state-run factories, and widespread speculation, have been major factors, although the issuing of too much currency and too loose credit are also to blame.
 
An Active Black Market
Reports appear in the official media almost daily about local Government officials and factory managers, many of them party members, who bought goods at state-subsidized prices and resold them on the black market for huge profits.
The People's Daily editorial warned party members to put the collective good above individual profit. 
It emphasized that ''the individual must submit to the organization'' and lower-level party organs to higher ones.
''We must enforce party discipline to guarantee the benefit of the whole,'' it said. 
''Strict discipline and unanimous action are good traditions of our party.''
