OTTAWA, May 5 (Reuters) - China has agreed to buy $1.8 billion (Canadian) worth of wheat from Canada over the next three years, the Government announced today.
Grain industry experts attributed the deal, the second largest in Canadian history, to China's efforts to improve the diet of its vast population and the effects of a recent drought on domestic harvests. 
Last year, Canada agreed to ship the Soviet Union 25 million metric tons of wheat over a five-year period.
United States Agriculture Department officials in Washington have estimated that China will need to import up to 15 million metric tons annually through 1985. 
China currently buys between 6 million tons and 9 million tons annually from the United States.
