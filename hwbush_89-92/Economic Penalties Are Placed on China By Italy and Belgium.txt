
Italy and Belgium announced economic penalties against China on Friday, suggesting that sentiment exists in the European Community for broader, concerted action when the organization's 12 members meet next week in Madrid.
Italy, which does not often take the West European lead in such matters, announced that it had suspended grants and loans to China to protest the Beijing Government's ''unacceptable repression'' of its citizens.
It was not clear how much money was involved, but the amount probably was not large. 
The Italians had approved a $260 million program in grants and credits for the three years from 1987 to 1989, and presumably most of that has already been passed along.
Foreign Minister Giulio Andreotti noted in a statement that the European Community was about to examine its policies toward China, but said Italy on its own felt it had ''no choice'' but to suspend consideration of future aid ''as a sign of our grave dissent from current executions and unacceptable repression.''
 
Belgium Freezes Loans
Belgium said it had frozen Government loans to China and halted financing for new development projects. 
The action affected a $7.5 million loan that had already been agreed to, as well as any new loans under consideration.
In Brussels, Reuters quoted a Foreign Ministry spokesman as saying the Chinese Ambassador had been summoned to the ministry on Thursday and told that ''the armed suppression of the legitimate aspirations of the Chinese people was a flagrant violation of human rights.''
It remained unclear, however, how much of an appetite the entire European Community has for unified action against China. 
On Thursday, Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher of Britain said she opposed sanctions as being ''too precipitate,'' creating the risk of ''a situation which could cause great panic in Hong Kong.''
