
In the biggest Japanese loan participation to China since Beijing's crackdown on pro-democracy protests last year, 11 leading Tokyo banks have joined in an $82 million syndication loan for a television-tube plant in China, banking officials said Saturday.
''This loan has an important meaning,'' said a Hong Kong banker dealing with China.
''It shows that the big Japanese banks are prepared to resume lending to China on a large scale.''
China's bloody military crackdown on the demonstrations in June 1989 prompted commercial banks worldwide to freeze or cut lending to the country.
The officials said the loan group consisted of 11 Japanese banks and several Chinese financial institutions. 
The company receiving the loan, the Shenzhen SEG Hitachi Color Displays Devices Company, is 25 percent owned by the Japanese electronics giant Hitachi and the rest by Chinese companies.
