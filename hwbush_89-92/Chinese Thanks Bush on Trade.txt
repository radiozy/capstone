
Prime Minister Li Peng said today that he welcomed President Bush's recommendation to extend most-favored-nation trading status to China for another year, and he suggested that Beijing would try to improve relations with Washington.
In conciliatory comments carried on the evening television news and later distributed by the official New China News Agency, Mr. Li said China ''appreciates and welcomes'' the decision to extend low tariffs to Chinese exports.
While the White House decision, announced on Thursday, is subject to review by Congress, the expectation is that Congress would not override the President on the trade benefits.
''This is not only in the interests of China, but in the interests of the United States, too,'' Mr. Li was quoted as saying about Mr. Bush's announcement. 
Mr. Li spoke during a meeting with Takamaru Morita, chairman of the Japanese Association for the Promotion of International Trade.
The brief press item added that China ''will, as always, make due efforts to restore and promote Sino-U.S. relations.''
That may be an indication that Beijing is considering new steps to ease strains with Washington. 
In the past, Chinese officials have insisted that the United States caused the strains and that it is up to the United States to resolve the problems.
