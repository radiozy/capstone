
China's top leaders decided in principle last week to revoke martial law, which now applies to most of Beijing, but the exact date has not been set, according to a Communist Party official.
The decision came at a secret meeting of the Politburo attended by Deng Xiaoping and by other senior leaders who are nominally retired, the official said. 
He said the meeting was convened as part of preparation for the Central Committee meeting that is expected to be convened within the next two weeks.
Martial law was imposed in most areas of the capital on May 20, and troops remain stationed on bridges and other key locations.
In a report that may be intended to prepare people for withdrawal of the troops, People's Daily said today that martial law would be lifted before long. 
The newspaper quoted the Government spokesman, Yuan Mu, as telling a Japanese interviewer that ''martial law is definitely for a limited time'' and that ''it seems that it will be lifted before very long.''
 
Reduced Presence of Troops
Previously, Mr. Yuan had refused to give any indication of when martial law might be lifted, and some officials had said it might last until next fall, when Beijing is to be the site of the Asian Games.
''The Chinese Government is already shrinking the areas where martial law is enforced, as order is restored in Beijing,'' People's Daily said, paraphrasing Mr. Yuan. ''This process will continue step by step.''
Martial-law troops, whose presence has been greatly reduced from heavy numbers this summer, are a visible reminder to the Chinese people of the violent crackdown on democracy protesters on June 4.
Moreover, foreign businesses and embassy officials say martial law must be lifted before their relations with China can begin to return to normal.
Another incentive to lift martial law is that it would encourage foreign tourism, which earned China $2.2 billion in 1988. 
The Government has said tourism is expected to bring in $1.4 billion this year.
