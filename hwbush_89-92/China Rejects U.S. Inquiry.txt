
China on Friday rejected a United States investigation of trade barriers that Washington has accused it of maintaining, saying the United States has ignored efforts it has already made to open its markets.
The investigation, which was announced in Washington Thursday and could put billions of dollars in Chinese exports at risk, is a further sign that once healthy Chinese-United States relations are getting worse, analysts and business executives said.
"We feel strong dissatisfaction at the United States' unilateral investigation," said a spokesman for the Ministry of Foreign Economic Relations and Trade, quoted on state radio.
The spokesman said the United States had ignored positive, substantial efforts made by China in opening its markets. 
"We absolutely cannot accept the accusations of the United States," he said. 
"We must express our extreme regret."
The United States invesigation could lead to 100 percent duties on some Chinese exports to the United States if Beijing does not open its markets in line with Washington's demands by the end of the yearlong investigation.
Sun Zhenyu, director of the ministry's Department of American and Oceanic Affairs, blamed United States politics for the Bush Administration's action.
