
About 2,000 opposition supporters marched downtown in Taiwan's capital today in a noisy but peaceful Constitution Day demonstration to demand swifter political change.
About 2,000 riot policemen, backed by water-cannon trucks, guarded the National Assembly building.
Earlier, 11 legislators from the opposition Democratic Progressive Party scuffled with guards at the National Assembly who had tried to prevent them from carrying protest banners into the building. 
They were finally allowed to take the banners into the National Assembly, where President Lee Teng-hui appealed for greater tolerance toward dissent.
''In a more open society we need to have the magnanimity to tolerate dissent,'' Mr. Lee told the assembly.
The opposition assemblymen unfurled their banners that demanded ''Aging Assemblymen Step Down,'' ''We Want Universal Suffrage for the Presidency'' and ''Please Proclaim Taiwan an Independent State.''
Both the Chinese Nationalist Government here and the Communist Government in Beijing oppose independence for Taiwan, which they say is a Chinese province. 
Each says it is the sole legitimate government of China.
