
Stock prices fell broadly yesterday, pulled down by a late wave of selling. 
The Dow Jones industrial average, off 19 points about an hour before closing, ended 37.13 points lower. 
The drop of 1.5 percent, to 2,480.70, was the biggest decline since the indicator fell 48.57, or 2.1 percent, to 2,292.14, on March 17.
Traders attributed much of the decline to concern about the violence in Beijing. 
This concern was evident in in Hong Kong, where the Hang Seng index fell 582.77 points, or 21.7 percent, to 2,093.61.
Reverberations from the political turmoil in China extended to the Tokyo stock market, where the Nikkei index of 225 selected stocks lost 210.34 points, closing below the 33,500 mark for the first time since April 26.
In American trading, the Windmere Corporation plunged 3 3/4, to 15 1/4, on the Big Board, spurred by worry over the status of the personal-care products company's operations in Hong Kong and China. 
Windmere said, however, that those plants continued to run normally. 
The stock was the second most active issue on the Big Board with nearly two million shares traded.
Hong Kong Telecommunications was also hit hard, dropping 2 1/8 to 16 7/8.
Among publicly traded investment companies specializing in Asian markets, the Taiwan Fund dropped 3 3/8, to 36 5/8; the Korea Fund 2 3/4, to 31, and the Asia Pacific Fund 1 3/4, to 9 1/8.
Volume on the New York Stock Exchange fell to 163.4 million, from 229.1 million on Friday. 
Declines outnumbered advances, 941 to 609.
Traders and analysts appeared generally confident that the stock market would weather the current storm. 
Some said program trading contributed to the decline.
''Given the extent of the news events that faced the market, the drop of 37 points on the Dow was relatively contained,'' said Robert B. Ritter, chief technical analyst at Ladenburg, Thalmann & Company. 
''Most of the damage was exacerbated by sell programs particularly during the afternoon. 
A year ago the events that transpired in China and Iran would probably have done considerably more damage.'' he said.
In the broader market, the Standard & Poor's 500-stock index fell 3.49 points, to 322.03, while the New York Stock Exchange composite index slipped 1.60 points, to 179.89.
A reduction in the prime lending rate to 11 percent, from 11 1/2 percent, by most big banks in the United States appeared to have little impact on equities yesterday.
In the technology sector, International Business Machines dropped 1 1/8, to 109; Hewlett-Packard was unchanged at 54 5/8, and Digital Equipment edged up 1/8, to 93 1/4.
Among other stocks, American Medical International rose 1/4, to 21 3/4. 
The company received an offer from an investor group for $21 a share in cash and $3 a share in securities.
American Telephone and Telegraph lost 7/8, to 35 3/8; General Electric fell 5/8, to 55 1/8, and Eastman Kodak dropped 1/2, to 46 5/8..
On the American Stock Exchange, the Amex market value index slipped 0.89, to 359.58, while in over-the-counter trading, the Nasdaq composite index fell 3.79 points, to 447.84. 
3Com Corporation plummeted 7 7/8, to 19 3/4, in the over-the-counter market. 
The company said its earnings for its fourth fiscal quarter, ended this week, would fall short of analysts' estimates.
