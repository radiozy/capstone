
A House Appropriations subcommittee approved a measure today that would set aside $20 million for the United Nations Family Planning Agency, the same type of financing decision that drew a Bush Administration veto in 1989.
The amendment to a larger spending bill was sponsored by Representative Bill Green, Republican of New York, and would reverse American policy that has withheld financing from the United Nations group since 1986.
The measure approved today would allow American funds to be spent only on "contraceptive commodities," and would specifically prohibit its use for abortions or abortion counseling.
The Bush Administration has contended that the United Nations group promoted forced abortions in China and was not deserving of support. 
Mr. Green said the Administration should not object to the new measure in its current form.
"The only thing the U.N.F.P.A. does in China is to give the Chinese help in developing demographic data," he said. 
The Administration, he said, should be willing to pay for contraceptives in a country that it is interested in conducting business with under most-favored-nation trade status.
