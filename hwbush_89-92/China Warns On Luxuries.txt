
China's ruling State Council warned today that spending on luxury goods by the leaders of debt-ridden state companies and other operations threatened to undermine the country's official austerity program and increase inflation.
"The desire for high consumption is once more on the rise," the council said through the state news media. 
It added that too many organizations were "lusting after the new and seeking out the foreign."
China's corporate leaders are on a luxury buying spree, snapping up new cars, video recorders, motorbikes and air-conditioners, often charging their purchases to the company account. 
Many of these goods find their way into the private possession of company employees, the council said.
The council demanded that enterprises submit all buying requests for primary items to the relevant provincial or municipal authorities, and ordered local purchasing officers to remain vigilant against abuses.
"They must be as firm as a tree in the ideological battle and thoroughly complete the job of controlling expenditure," the council said.
