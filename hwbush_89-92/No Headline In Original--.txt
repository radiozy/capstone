Three people were arrested over the weekend in New York City on charges of heroin trafficking that officials said resulted from an unusual tip by law-enforcement authorities in China.
The Chinese officials had cooperated with American officials in a drug arrest once before, in a case last year involving a shipment bound for San Francisco, said an assistant United States attorney in Manhattan, Samuel W. Seymour.
Law-enforcement officials in New York have said they are troubled by increasing heroin shipments from Asia. 
Mr. Seymour said he could not say whether the Chinese cooperation represented a change of policy.
The Drug Enforcement Administration said that the case involved an 87-pound shipment of heroin for New York on March 21 and that it was detected by customs officials in Guandong. 
The Chinese seized much of the heroin, which was concealed in 5 of 300 umbrella boxes, but allowed 6.1 pounds to remain in the boxes.
The Chinese officials contacted drug-enforcement authorities in Hong Kong, who passed the information to the police in the United States and Canada.
A Canadian, Veng-Tat Lau, 36 years old, was arrested Saturday at 42d Street and Broadway. 
Officials said he had followed the heroin from Hong Kong through Canada to New York. 
Ka Yiu Mak, 35, was arrested near his home in Elmhurst, Queens. 
Officials said that he was carrying seven and a half pounds of heroin and that two handguns were found in a search of his home. 
Christopher Chau, 34, was arrested Sunday morning near his home in Whitestone, Queens.
All three suspects, who have not entered pleas, face possible life terms if convicted. 
A Federal magistrate ordered them held without bail.
