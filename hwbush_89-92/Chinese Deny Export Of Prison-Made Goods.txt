
The Government denied today that it allowed goods made by prison inmates to be sold for export, and assailed two American news organizations for reports on the issue.
Both Newsweek's latest issue and a CBS News "60 Minutes" program carried accounts of convict labor by Harry Wu, a former prisoner who is now a scholar at the Hoover Institute. 
He visited China recently posing as a Chinese-American businessman interested in exporting goods made in prison factories to the United States. 
He reported that traders told him that prisoners could be beaten as a means of quality control.
A spokesman for the Foreign Ministry, Wu Jianmin, said today that China did not condone prison exports. 
"If some labor products flowed into the U.S. market, that's not the result of China's policy," the spokesman said. 
"The U.S. side should also be held responsible for itself."
