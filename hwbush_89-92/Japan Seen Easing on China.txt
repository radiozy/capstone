
The former Japanese Prime Minister, Noboru Takeshita, said on Wednesday that he expected Japan to lift its freeze on loans to China soon.
Mr. Takeshita, a leading figure in the ruling Liberal Democratic Party, said, ''I expect we can do something by the time of the summit.''
The leaders of seven of the leading industrial nations are to hold their annual summit meeting in Houston in early July.
Officials here have hinted that Japan may push at the meeting for a relaxation of the sanctions against China that were endorsed at last year's session. 
The sanctions were a response to Beijing's crackdown last year on a pro-democracy movement.
Japan, the largest provider of aid to China, froze $5.6 billion in loans promised for 1990-95 in an agreement drawn up during Mr. Takeshita's administration.
China badly needs new loans to service its foreign debt of $44 billion, some 50 percent of which is owed to Japanese banks and government agencies. 
Loans are also required for crucial projects like power stations, railways, ports and fertilizer plants.
Mr. Takeshita's remark comes as Japanese business and political leaders have been stepping up pressure on the Government to increase trade by lifting the freeze.
