
Chinese state bankers said today that there was no reason for China's international credit rating to be diminished and expressed confidence over the country's ability to repay its mounting debts. 
According to the latest official figures, China's debts totaled $40 billion at the end of 1988.
Representatives from Moody's Investors Service left China over the weekend after meeting Chinese officials to discuss the country's credit risk after the military crackdown in June. 
A lower rating would make it more expensive for China to raise money in the world market.
A Bank of China official was quoted as saying that ''it was not correct to give China a lower rating'' and that ''it was wrong to apply Western formulas to China and consider political factors more than economic ones when assessing credit risk.''
The Ministry of Finance reacted angrily in July 1988 when Moody's awarded an A-3 debt rating to a bond issue of 300 million marks that China had sold in Frankfurt in November 1987.
So far one Japanese rating agency has lowered its China rating, while the World Bank has suspended new loans and many commercial banks have avoided extending new credit, at least temporarily.
