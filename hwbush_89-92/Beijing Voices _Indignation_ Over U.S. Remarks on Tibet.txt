
In an unusually harsh rebuke, China today condemned as ''gross interference'' a United States Senate resolution that criticized recent violence in Tibet.
The Foreign Affairs Committee of the National People's Congress, China's legislature, expressed ''utmost indignation'' and lodged a strong protest in a long and comprehensive response to Western criticism of its handling of unrest in Tibet.
The Senate resolution, passed by a voice vote Thursday night, condemned the ''use of violence against unarmed Tibetan demonstrators'' and called on the Bush Administration to make Chinese actions in Tibet a major part of Washington's policy toward Beijing.
China imposed martial law in Tibet on March 8 after three days of rioting by pro-independence demonstrators. 
At least a dozen people were killed and 100 were wounded in the disturbances.
