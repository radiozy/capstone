
China's tough austerity policies have cooled off the overheated economy but have made few inroads in solving basic flaws in the system, the Government said last week.
In a report on economic performance through September, Zhang Zhongji, spokesman for the State Statistical Bureau, said the year-old austerity program had held the gross national product to $30 billion, up 5 percent from a year earlier.
Mr. Zhang said consumer demand was down, the rise in retail prices had been brought under control and ''the whole economy is taking a favorable turn.''
 
But he acknowledged that
''no major breakthroughs had been achieved'' in resolving chronic supply-demand imbalances. 
He said there was serious overstocking of some products because of market slumps and poor circulation of money because many companies delayed debt payments.
Since last fall the Government has sharply reduced credit available for investment and ordered the suspension of thousands of building projects.
