
China has issued new regulations limiting the amount of Chinese money foreigners may change back into hard currency, officials said today. 
For every $100 a foreigner changes into Chinese currency upon entering, he can leave China with no more than $50.
An official of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange Control said the rule applied to foreign visitors and residents including employees of embassies, news organizations and representative offices. 
They had previously been allowed to reconvert all the foreign currency they had changed on entering.
In addition, embassies and foreign companies with representative offices in China can no longer change foreign exchange certificates into hard currency, the official said.
The foreign exchange currency was invented in 1980 to be used by foreigners. 
Officially at par with the renminbi, the local currency, it now trades for up to 45 percent more on the black market and is widely used by Chinese to buy scarce and imported goods.
The new regulations were issued last week by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange Currency, a unit of the central bank. 
They have not been published by China's official newspapers.
