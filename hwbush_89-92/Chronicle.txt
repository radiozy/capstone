
"Our Town," Thornton Wilder's classic drama about life in turn-of-the-century New Hampshire, may seem the quintessential American play, but to ALAN ALDA, star of a production that opened in London last night, "It is universal."
Between preview performances this week Mr. Alda said in a telephone interview: "It is not so much American as it is about people. 
Even though Wilder gave Grover's Corners a latitude and longitude, a time of day and year, leading us to think that this was one specific little town, the audiences here realize that it is about them, about here, too. 
I would like someday when it is possible to direct it in China with Chinese actors."
The play is a staple of American high school repertories, but this is the first professional version to be put on in England since 1946, so London audiences do not know what to expect as they do in the United States, Mr. Alda said. 
"It is completely fresh to them and really hits them."
Mr. Alda's enactment of the stage manager who narrates the story of birth, life and death is his first stage role after a 20-year hiatus. 
His next stop is Broadway, where he is to star in Neil Simon's "Jake's Women," scheduled to open in March.
