
Two leaders of the Chinese democracy movement who escaped to France last month arrived in the United States yesterday, and they are to announce the formation of a new opposition movement at a conference in Chicago this weekend.
The two leaders are Wuer Kaixi, a 21-year-old organizer of the student demonstrations in Tiananmen Square in Beijing, and Yan Jiaqi, 47, a political scientist who was a close adviser to Zhao Ziyang, ousted General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party.
Mr. Wuer and Mr. Yan escaped to Hong Kong and traveled secretly to France after being put on the Chinese Government's most-wanted list after the Communists suppressed the protests in Beijing on June 4.
A State Department official said the Administration had approved visas for the two men despite the likelihood that the action will prompt protests by Chinese Government officials, already enraged over the refuge granted in the American Embassy in Beijing to another leading dissident, Fang Lizhi.
Mr. Wuer and Mr. Yan plan to attend a meeting at the University of Chicago tomorrow through Sunday. 
There, they are to announce the establishment of a new umbrella group, another of the five founding members, Su Shaozhi, said.
As envisioned by its founders, the worldwide umbrella group will give impetus to the fragmented Chinese opposition, which includes Communist intellectuals in China and abroad and anti-Communists living in the United States and Taiwan.
While the Chinese Government has publicly admitted the arrest of about 2,000 people, Mr. Su said they were told that 10,000 people have been arrested in Beijing alone, with 120,000 arrested nationwide.
Correction
An article yesterday about the arrival of two Chinese student leaders in the United States misidentified the campus where a congress of Chinese students is to begin today. 
It is the University of Illinois at Chicago.
Correction-Date: July 28, 1989, Friday, Late Edition - Final
