It said the number of executions this year was the highest since 1983, when an anti-crime campaign resulted in 5,000 to 10,000 executions.
Those executed this year included people convicted of ''counterrevolutionary'' religious activities and violence during pro-democracy protests last year, the report said.
''The dramatic increase in death sentences is particularly disturbing because China's trial system falls far below international standards,'' Amnesty said. 
It said trials were often a formality with the verdict decided in advance and with little chance for appeal or pardon.
Almost all those sentenced to death this year were tried under emergency regulations that gave them virtually no opportunity to prepare a defense, Amnesty said. 
Some were executed within two weeks of their arrests.
Amnesty said courts had been ordered to crack down on crimes like drug trafficking, embezzlement, gang robbery and theft. 
But despite more death sentences, crime has not declined in the last decade.
Amnesty said a Chinese legal journal noted last October that several people tried under the emergency regulations believed that a death sentence was inevitable and did not try to appeal.
 
Beijing Frees Student Leader
BEIJING, Sept. 
12 (Reuters) -China has freed a student leader held for 13 months at a maximum security prison on charges of organizing pro-democracy protests last year, university students and instructors said today.
Yang Tao, prominent in the uprising crushed by tanks and troops in June last year, was quietly released from China's main jail for political prisoners, Qincheng, said the students and instructors at Beijing University.
