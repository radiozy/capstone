
President Mikhail S. Gorbachev expressed regret today over the violent culmination of the mass student protests in China. 
But he also defended Moscow's ''balanced'' response, asserting that the information was still vague and that the priority was to safeguard the fledgling Chinese program of change.
Mr. Gorbachev's comment at a news conference at the end of his visit to West Germany was his first extensive statement on China, to which he made a milestone visit last month when the protests were already under way.
After the crackdown in early June, the Soviet Congress of People's Deputies condemned outside attempts to put pressure on Beijing and termed the army's violent crushing of the demonstrations an internal affair.
''We don't know everything yet, neither we nor the West German Government have full information, so I believe that for now we must display great responsibility and balance in our assessment,'' Mr. Gorbachev said.
''I believe we are all worried, we are all concerned not to break the process of reforms in this enormous state. 
I think we all understand that this would cause major damage to the whole process of recovery in the world.
''So however we view this, we want the Chinese people and Government to find a solution that would answer the expectations of China and of us.''
Mr. Gorbachev said that these consideratons, ''and no others,'' determined Moscow's ''balanced, responsible and serious position.''
