
Prime Minister Li Peng said today that China opposed any military involvement in the Persian Gulf by ''big powers,'' but officials reportedly told a visitor that it would not oppose a United Nations resolution allowing the use of force to back up the embargo of Iraq.
Sheik Sabah al-Ahmed al-Sabah, the Foreign Minister and Deputy Prime Minister of the Kuwaiti Government in exile, said that China's leaders had assured him that if China did not support a United Nations Security Council resolution on policing the embargo, it would at least not veto it.
That suggested that China might abstain from the vote as a way of showing its concern about American military involvement in the region.
Sheik Sabah spoke at a news conference in Beijing after meetings with Prime Minister Li and with Wu Xueqian, a deputy prime minister. 
Sheik Sabah is on a tour of world capitals to call for support for efforts to oust Iraqi forces from Kuwait. 
He has already been to Washington, London, Paris and Yugoslavia, and will next visit Moscow and Iran, he said.
China has generally had good relations with Kuwait and Iraq, and after the invasion it supported the Security Council resolutions calling for the embargo.
It also said it would halt weapons sales to Iraq, and Sheik Sabah said today that he was sure that China would also halt all other trade with Iraq.
While supporting the trade embargo, China has also expressed unease at the American military buildup in the region.
