
Wuer Kaixi, the Chinese student leader who fled China after the Government crackdown on the democracy movement in June, said yesterday that he believed the Chinese Government would fall within two to three years but that student leaders still welcomed dialogue and contact with Beijing.
''We need to put an immediate end to the fascist regime now in power,'' said Mr. Wuer through a translator.
Speaking at a news conference at the offices of Asia Watch, a human rights organization based in New York, he called for the overthrow of the Government, but added that he welcomed recent moves by Beijing to curb nepotism and abuses by high-ranking officials and members of their families. 
Such steps are a major demand of democracy movement leaders, but Mr. Wuer said reforms had to be quicker and more radical.
Mr. Wuer traveled from a three-day conference of Chinese students in the United States that was held at the University of Illinois at Chicago over the weekend. 
He said the newly formed group unifying overseas Chinese student groups would remain independent of all government organizations.
Later yesterday afternoon, with the Statue of Liberty as a backdrop, Mr. Wuer and several other students who had participated in the protests at Tiananmen Square in Beijing before the crackdown addressed a small crowd of supporters and called upon Chinese abroad to continue the fight for democracy.
''These men are our heroes,'' said Li Weng, a chemical engineering student who came from China three years ago. 
''We need people like them to get democracy. 
It will take time but it will come.''
