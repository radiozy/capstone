
Closing out the dozen years in which he guided New York City's independent and home-brewed foreign policy, Mayor Edward I. Koch yesterday issued one of those communiques for which his tenure was renowned.
In answer to Ambassador Weng Fupei of China, who had remonstrated with him over renaming the intersection in front of the Chinese Consulate as ''Tiananmen Square Corner,'' Mayor Koch urged the diplomat ''to defect and seek asylum here in the United States and then to tell the truth.''
Last month Mr. Koch signed a bill that renamed the corner of 42d Street and 12th Avenue in memory of ''peaceful pro-democracy demonstrators'' who died at Tiananmen Square in Beijing ''as a result of the regime's barbaric decision to unleash armed forces on its own citizens.''
This seemed consonant with an approach to foreign relations that included the Mayor's description of the United Nations as a ''cesspool'' and the Soviet Government as ''the pits.''
Mr. Weng, who is the Chinese consul general, wrote a letter to the Mayor on Sept. 
28 in which he denounced ''the insulting scheme'' to rename the intersection and reiterated his Government's position that during the ''clearing operation'' in Tiananmen Square in June, ''there was no casualty.''
Mayor Koch likened the ambassador's statements to Orwellian double speak, but said there were ''too many witnesses to what happened for the lies to take hold.''
He closed his letter, ''All the best.''
