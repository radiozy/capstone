
The Bank of China has issued nearly $145 million in bonds in Tokyo in its first such financing outside China since the 1989 military crackdown on the pro-democracy movement, a Japanese underwriter said Friday.
"All of the funds to be raised by the bond issue will be used for projects in developing China's petrochemical industry," said Shen Yue, an official with the Yamaichi Securities Company, the leading underwriter on the financing.
The bonds, with an issue price of 100.8, will mature in five years and carry a coupon of 7.7 percent, Mr. Shen said, adding that the sale was the seventh such issue by the Bank of China in Japan since 1984, with the last occurring in April 1989.
Japan joined many Western nations in imposing sanctions to protest Beijing's military crackdown in June 1989, which left hundreds of people dead. 
But last year, Japan said the economic assistance would start flowing again as China worked to regain international good will.
Ten Chinese banking institutions have been authorized by the Chinese Government to issue bonds overseas. 
Between 1982 and 1989, they floated $4.25 billion in bonds in Tokyo, Hong Kong, Frankfurt, London and Singapore.
China's Government does not plan to issue bonds itself this year, according to The China Daily newspaper.
