
Taiwan sought United States support today for its bid to enter GATT, the international trade organization.
The request came as the two sides opened four days of talks on such issues as tariff cuts, protection for patents and copyrights, and United States demands for greater access to Taiwan's agricultural, insurance and banking markets.
Sheu Ke-seng, director general of Taiwan's Board of Foreign Trade, urged the United States to support Taiwan's application to rejoin the Geneva-based General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade. 
Mr. Sheu said in opening remarks that as the world's 13th-largest trading nation, Taiwan was entitled to membership in GATT.
The Government on Taiwan applied for GATT membership in January 1990 but is still waiting to be admitted to the organization, which sets rules for world trade and provides a forum for settling trade disputes. 
The Chinese Government in Beijing, which applied for GATT membership in 1987, has denounced Taiwan's move and vowed to block it.
Nationalist China was one of GATT's 23 founding members, but it was forced to leave in 1950, a year after its forces lost a civil war on the China mainland.
