
Forty Chinese nationals were ordered dismissed from Grumman's Long Island plant under President Bush's suspension of military dealings with China, a Grumman spokesman said today.
The Chinese nationals, mostly engineers, were working on a project to modernize electronics in the Chinese fighter plane.
The Chinese were notified on Wednesday at a meeting at the Grumman corporate headquarters that they would not be permitted on Grumman property after 5 P.M. Wednesday, said the spokesman, Robert Harwood.
They were told to turn in the identification badges allowing them on the premises in Bethpage. 
They were awaiting instructions from their Government, Mr. Harwood said.
Grumman is the prime contractor on the largest American-Chinese military project.
The company was formally told by the Pentagon on Wednesday that under President Bush's order no Chinese nationals were to be allowed into the plant.
