
With an area of about 600,000 square miles, Mongolia is slightly smaller than Arizona, New Mexico, Texas and Louisiana combined. 
The capital is Ulan Bator, with a 1986 population of about 500,000.
Mongolians account for 90 percent of the population of about two million. 
Some 2 percent of the population is Chinese and another 2 percent is Russian.
In the early part of the 20th century, Mongolia passed back and forth from Chinese to Russian dominion; in 1924, it became, in effect the first Soviet satellite state. 
Until now, the country has been ruled by the 10-member Politburo of the Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party Central Committee.
The People's Great Hural, or national assembly, meets about three days a year to enact the basic laws of the country.
 
Genghis Khan formed the first
Mongolian state from nomadic tribes in 1203, and went on to conquer much of Asia and European Russia. 
Mongol power declined after the Mongol dynasty in China was overthrown in 1368.
 
The Manchus, who conquered
China in 1644, brought the Mongols under their rule in 1691 and the country was a Chinese province for the next 220 years. 
In 1911, Mongol leaders declared their independence of Chinese rule.
Live animals and animal products account for half of Mongolia's output. 
Most trade has been with the Soviet Union and the Eastern bloc.
