
In China's first official response to criticism over the last week from the exiled dissident Fang Lizhi, a Foreign Ministry spokesman said today that his conduct was not worth discussing.
Mr. Fang, an astrophysicist who took refuge in the United States Embassy here last year during the military crackdown on the democracy movement, left for Britain in June after long diplomatic negotiations.
The Government had previously taken a bellicose tone in assailing Mr. Fang, describing him as ''scum'' and accusing him of engineering the demonstrations that led to the armed crackdown.
''Fang's conduct is not worth commenting on,'' the ministry spokesman, Jin Guihua, said at a briefing here. 
In its original translation of Mr. Jin's remarks, the Government had him saying that Mr. Fang was not worth ''refuting,'' but the translation was later changed.
The dissident has complained in recent days that Washington is not putting enough pressure on China to adopt a more democratic political system.
Beijing allowed Mr. Fang and his wife, Li Shuxian, also a prominent dissident, to fly to Britain after dropping its demand that he be turned over to the Chinese authorities to face criminal charges. 
Mr. Fang is to do research at Cambridge University.
