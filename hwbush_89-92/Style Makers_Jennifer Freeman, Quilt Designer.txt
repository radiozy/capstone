
The antique Amish crib quilts that fetch small fortunes at auctions these days often use color schemes dominated by rich reds and deep greens instead of conventional pale blues or pinks.
Jennifer Freeman is following in Amish footsteps in designing a line of quilts for infants. 
She is not morally opposed to pastels, she said, but she prefers more dramatic hues.
"I don't know if you could call this one shocking," she said, smoothing out a freshly sewn checkerboard in red, black and gold in her Upper West Side apartment and office. 
"But it's certainly not what people expect for babies. 
I want to avoid the country look. 
I want to create quilts for urban babies."
Her six-month-old company is called Three Peach Designs in tribute to time she spent in China after she graduated from college in 1983.
"The peach is a symbol of rebirth to the Chinese," she said. 
The name also appeals to her seamstresses, Chinese immigrants who work in their homes in Flushing, Queens.
Ms. Freeman, 29 years old, is working toward an M.B.A. at Columbia University and serving as an associate for a New York City concern that consults with American companies with business in Asia.
She shops for fabrics on weekends, and the patterns and textures she chooses are as varied as the colors.
Polka dots, pinstripes, florals and corduroy may be juxtaposed on a single 35-by-40-inch quilt. 
Her favorite backing material combines white stars and swirls on a navy background.
She uses cotton fabric in her quilts, which retail for $135 to $160.
