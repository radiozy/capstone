
Asian countries voted overwhelmingly tonight to exclude Iraqi athletic teams from the 11th Asian Games, a regional mini-Olympics scheduled to begin in Beijing on Saturday.
The vote in the 38-member Olympic Council of Asia was 27 for excluding Iraq and 3 against exclusion, with 5 abstentions and 1 invalid ballot. 
The decision was a particularly painful diplomatic defeat for Iraq because the members of the council are mostly third-world nonaligned nations, rather than Western powers whom Iraq blames for trying to isolate it.
As a result of the vote, Iraq has been suspended from the Olympic Council of Asia and lost its right to take part in the Asian Games, reportedly until it withdraws its troops from Kuwait. 
Iraq's soccer team had already arrived in China and had played Chinese teams, but now it will be unable to participate in the official athletic meet.
The Olympic Council of Asia has been particularly aggrieved by the Iraqi invasion of Kuwait because the council's president, Sheik Fahad al-Ahmad al-Sabah, was a Kuwaiti who was killed in the invasion. 
Some Arab countries from the Persian Gulf had said they might boycott the Asian Games unless Iraq was excluded.
 
China Sought Peacemaker Role
China had sought to reconcile the countries and had repeatedly said that it did not want politics to interfere with the Games. 
They form the biggest international conference ever held in China, and the leadership in Beijing has tried to use the event to mark its return to the international community after widespread criticisms of its military crackdown against the democracy movement last year.
For a time China succeeded in obtaining commitments from all 38 countries in the region to participate. 
But Jordan dropped out this week for unknown reasons and now Iraq will be excluded as well.
The Associated Press reported that Iraq, Yemen and the Palestine representatives opposed expulsion, and that Mongolia and Afghanistan did not vote.
