
The interpreter for a Chinese acrobatic troupe who left the group a day after four of the troupe's performers disappeared, emerged yesterday to say that she wanted to return home and had not sought political asylum in the United States.
''Contrary to some news reports, I have never said that I wanted asylum in the United States,'' the interpreter, He Lanrong, said.
''I have not applied for asylum in the United States. 
In fact, I miss my family very much, and I love my country and wish to return.''
Mrs. He said she had left the Nanjing acrobatic troupe, which had finished a 16-week tour with the Big Apple Circus, because the troupe's director had blamed her for the acrobats' disappearance. 
The remainder of the troupe left for China yesterday.
Mrs. He said the director, Lu Yi, had suggested that the disappearance of the four acrobats could create problems for Mrs. He's husband and daughter in China.
The four acrobats left the troupe Sunday night after their last performance in Shelburne, Vt. 
Mrs. He said she had learned of their departure at about midnight Sunday. 
Federal officials said yesterday that they did not know their whereabouts.
A day after returning to New York, Mr. He sought help from the Immigration and Naturalization Service, which called the Lawyers Committee for Human Rights in Manhattan.
