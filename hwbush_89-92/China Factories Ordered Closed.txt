
China's top leader, Deng Xiaoping, has ordered some rural factories to be closed because they use resources that should go to state companies, the official Economic Information newspaper said today.
It said that in recent years, because of the mistakes of the deposed Communist Party chief, Zhao Ziyang, the state sector had been held back, and its share of national industrial output had shrunk to 64 percent last year from 78 percent in 1982.
In the same period, the collective sector's share jumped to 32.6 percent from 22 percent, it said.
Rural factories, run by villages outside the state plan, are an important part of the collective sector, which was encouraged by Mr. Zhao, who believed they were more efficient and market oriented than state factories.
But, the newspaper said, collective factories, though producing a great deal and earning foreign exchange, are badly run and inefficient and use capital, transport and raw materials that should go to state companies.
