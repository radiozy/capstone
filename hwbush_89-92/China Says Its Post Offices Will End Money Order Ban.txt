
The Chinese post office will again accept money orders from abroad 39 years after it halted the practice for political reasons, The China Daily said today.
The decision will make it easier for Chinese to receive money from friends and relatives abroad, send tuition payments to foreign schools and conduct other transactions.
International money order services will resume on July 1 with Japan and on Aug. 
3 with the United States, the paper said. 
The amount of the money orders will be limited, but no details were immediately available.
The Communist Government halted such services in 1953 as part of a withdrawal from contact with the West. 
Chinese who wanted to send money abroad or receive it had to go through the Bank of China.
