
A Chinese court convicted three Hong Kong residents today on charges of helping pro-democracy campaigners and sentenced them to prison terms of four to five years, a Chinese newspaper reported.
The three were the first Hong Kong residents sentenced by China for involvement in the democracy movement that was crushed in China in June 1989.
The Yangcheng Evening News in nearby Canton said the three were charged with collaborating with a Hong Kong group in attempting to smuggle Wang Juntao and Chen Ziming, two well-known dissidents, out of China.
The dissidents were convicted on Feb. 
12 in Beijing of plotting to overthrow the Government. 
Each was sentenced to 13 years in prison.
The newspaper said the Canton Intermediate People's Court sentenced Lo Hoi-sing, a 42-year-old businessman, and Lai Pui-Shing each to five years in prison and Li Lung-hing to four years. 
The newspaper provided no further information on Mr. Lai and Mr. Li.
The paper said Mr. Lo supplied details of Mr. Chen's whereabouts to John Shum, a Hong Kong actor who is a member of the Hong Kong Alliance in Support of the Patriotic Democratic Movement in China. 
Beijing has branded the alliance "subversive" for helping wanted dissidents escape the country.
Mr. Lo's wife, Chau Mat-mat, said in Hong Kong that the sentence was harsh. 
She said she would appeal to Chinese authorities to reduce the sentence.
She said her husband, formerly a Hong Kong trade representative to Beijing and later a businessman specializing in China trade, was only involved in passing messages from some one in China to Mr. Shum, a friend of Mr. Lo for years.
