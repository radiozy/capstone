
The political turmoil in China has blocked a 15 billion yen, or about $100 million, fixed-rate bond issue, which was to have been offered on June 30 in Tokyo, an official of the lead underwriter, the Daiwa Securities Company, said today.
The Guangdong International Trust and Investment Corporation, among 10 Chinese financial institutions allowed to borrow on overseas markets, had completed the terms of its 10-year fixed-rate issue before the shootings last week in Tiananmen Square in Beijing.
''Under current market conditions, it's difficult to issue,'' the official, who asked not to be identified, said.
Last week, Japan's credit rating agencies said they might change the ratings of bonds issued by Chinese entities because of worries concerning debt payments prompted by the unrest in China.
''The postponement has nothing to do with the credit checks on Chinese entities being carried out by Japanese rating agenies,'' the Daiwa official said.
Guangdong International Trust's bonds have been rated with a high investment grade and have been among the most actively traded Chinese securities.
Ye Qinglin, the general manager of the company's international finance division, said the postponement was mainly because of documentation problems. 
''I'm not sure it is related to China's political condition,'' he said. 
''I've not yet received any formal explanation from Japan.''
The Daiwa official said he was unsure when the bonds would be issued. 
''Everything is ready,'' he said. 
''We have to just wait and see and find a window at the right moment.''
