
China and Indonesia agreed today to normalize relations, 22 years after they were suspended over allegations that China supported a Communist coup attempt against the Indonesian Government.
President Suharto of Indonesia and the Chinese Foreign Minister, Qian Qichen, reached agreement after meeting for more than two hours at a Tokyo hotel, said Max Lapod, spokesman for the Indonesian Embassy in Tokyo.
''We are going to go through the process of normalizing relations based on the five principles of noninterference, nonaggression, mutual respect, beneficial relations and peaceful coexistence,'' Mr. Lapod said in an interview.
President Suharto and Mr. Qian are in Tokyo to attend the funeral for Emperor Hirohito.
Indonesia suspended relations with China in 1967, two years after an attempted Communist coup in Jakarta that Indonesia said had Chinese backing. 
In 1985 the two countries resumed direct trade.
President Suharto and Mr. Qian accepted invitations for the Indonesian and Chinese presidents to exchange visits, Mr. Lapod said.
China's state-run press agency reported that Mr. Qian and President Suharto ''agreed upon future efforts for normalization of bilateral relations.''
The meeting with the Indonesian leader marked the beginning of the normalization process, the New China News Agency quoted Mr. Qian as telling reporters.
''They agreed that the two countries should make further efforts toward the normalization of bilateral relations,'' the agency quoted an unidentified official as saying.
China long has been pressing for normalization of relations with Indonesia. 
But Jakarta has held back largely because it fears China would use its influence among Indonesia's ethnic Chinese to foment unrest. 
About four million of Indonesia's 168 million people are ethnic Chinese.
