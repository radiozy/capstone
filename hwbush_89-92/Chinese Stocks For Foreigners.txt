
The stock exchange in the southern Chinese city of Shenzhen plans to offer another $100 million in special stocks to foreigners this year, an official newspaper reported today.
That would more than double the value of shares sold so far to foreigners on the Shenzhen Exchange, one of two fledgling stock markets in China.
The market in Shenzhen, one of China's special economic zones near Hong Kong, indicated the shares for foreigners might be available for 15 to 20 companies by the end of the year. 
So far, the exchange has 13 listed companies, with five issues of special shares for foreigners.
Percy Au-young, research chief at Sun Hung Kai Securities Ltd. in Hong Kong, said the amount of additional shares could be much higher than the $100 million cited by The China Daily.
Chinese news organizations have reported that the Shenzhen government plans to issue as much as $1.5 billion of the special shares in the next 10 years.
