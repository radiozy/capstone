
An entire neighborhood of hotels and apartments and sports facilities has been erected for the Games, and Chinese officials were excited that, for the first time in the history of the competition, all 38 member countries or territories said they would participate.
Zhu Zhangling, an official of the Games Organizing Committee, said today that 37 of the 38 countries have submitted complete or partial lists of athletes.
No list has been sent for a team from Palestine, which is recognized by China and by the Games as a sovereign state.
Officials explained that the Palestinian name list normally was sent from Kuwait, and so has been delayed because of the Iraqi invasion.
More worrying for China is a proposal to ban Iraq from the competition. 
The Olympic Council of Asia, which is coordinating the Games, has asked for the opinion of each country in the region to decide whether to exclude Iraq, and responses are supposed to be received by Thursday. 
Iraq has asked that any such decision be reached after a full meeting of all countries in the region.
Chinese sports officials said China itself opposed the banning of Iraq.
''We do not stand for bringing political considerations into the Asian Games,'' said Wu Zhongyuan, a spokesman for the event. 
However, China is said to be concerned that other Persian Gulf countries might boycott the competition unless Iraq is excluded.
