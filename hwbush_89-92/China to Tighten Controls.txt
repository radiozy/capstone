
Vice Premier Yao Yilin said today that the Government would take even tougher retrenchment measures in the next three years to reduce inflation and restore market order.
Finance Minister Wang Bingqian, meanwhile, predicted China's money shortage will worsen next year when more foreign loans start coming due.
The two officials delivered their sober assessments of China's economic situation to the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, the legislature, which began meeting Tuesday.
''The Government will take tougher measures in the coming three years to accomplish the ongoing economic retrenchment,'' Vice Premier Yao said.
Earlier today, the official press reported that the Government is taking additional steps to re-establish order in an economy that it said is reeling from speculation and profiteering in scarce materials and widespread tax evasion.
Xie Mingan, an official of the Ministry of Materials and Equipment, said the state would tighten controls on prices of some raw materials, including coal, rolled steel and non-ferrous metals. 
The state will also reassert control over distribution.
In addition, The China Daily reported the state would crack down on what it says is rampant tax evasion among China's 14.5 million privately owned enterprises.
Mr. Xie said unnecessary middlemen would be eliminated, illegal dealers and officials trading power for money would be disciplined and state supplies of materials for production would be maintained.
