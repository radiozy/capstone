
Former President Richard M. Nixon met with Education Minister Li Tieying today for what the Chinese described as ''frank and sincere'' talks on China's policy toward its students in the aftermath of the crushing of the student-led democracy movement.
Mr. Nixon, on the second day of a private visit as a guest of the Government, was briefed on China's ''practice of being kind-hearted toward the students while setting strict demands on them,'' the official news agency said.
Since the Government crushed the pro-democracy movement in early June, Mr. Li and other officials have said they will ''understand, educate and unify'' most of the hundreds of thousands of students who took part in the protests, and will punish only a small group of leaders. 
All students have been required to report their activities during the protests and undergo intense political education. 
Hundreds have been arrested.
The press agency did not say how Mr. Nixon responded to Mr. Li's briefing, but in diplomatic parlance, ''frank and sincere'' often are used to describe talks where the sides disagree.
Mr. Nixon's spokesman, John Taylor, said the former President did not intend to try to mediate a solution to the standoff between Washington and Beijing over China's crushing of dissent.
