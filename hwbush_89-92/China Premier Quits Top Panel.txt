
China is in its worst economic slowdown in a dozen years, and some foreign diplomats have said Mr. Li might be forced to take responsibility for it. 
Thus the latest announcement could be read as a blow to Mr. Li.
But diplomats noted tonight that Mr. Li might be preoccupied with foreign affairs and other responsibilities, and that the economic commission has diminished in importance since most of China's economic restructuring was put on hold two years ago.
The official New China News Agency gave no hint of a reason for the change. 
It mentioned the shuffle in a single paragraph, presenting it as a routine action by the standing committee of the National People's Congress. 
But The Associated Press quoted the Government-sponsored China News Service as saying that Mr. Li had asked to be replaced so that he could concentrate on his duties as Prime Minister.
To add to the mystery, the man chosen to replace Mr. Li is a relatively low-ranking official. 
The new minister, Chen Jinhua, 61 years old, is president of the China National Petrochemical Corporation.
