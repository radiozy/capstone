
Tens of thousands of prospective investors lined up in the southern Chinese city of Xiamen over the weekend for applications to buy stock, even though the central Government has yet to allow a stock exchange to open there.
A report today by the official China News Service said more than 10,000 people had gathered at each of three locations visited by one reporter. 
In all, 26 offices in the city were issuing applications.
Four companies -- an automobile concern, a fishery, a real estate business and a development company -- were issuing applications to buy stock.
Stock shares are popular in China because bank interest rates are low and there are few other good investment opportunities. 
The two legal exchanges, in Shanghai and Shenzhen, have been open for more than a year. 
Economists estimate that the Chinese save as much as 43 percent of their salaries. 
Official reports have estimated that as much as $183 billion is languishing in low-interest bank accounts in China.
