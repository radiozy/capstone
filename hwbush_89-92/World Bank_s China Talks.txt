
The World Bank is in ''close consultations'' with China about a resumption of lending to China ''in the not too distant future,'' the bank's president, Barber B. Conable Jr., said today.
In June, after a violent crackdown on pro-democracy demonstrators in Beijing, the bank suspended seven loans totaling $780 million.
''We believe there are 1.1 billion poor people in China, and it is desirable to try to maintain a relationshiop there, but it must be the result of careful consultations,'' Mr. Conable told a news briefing in his office.
He said he expected there would be more discussions next week when a high-level Chinese delegation is due in Washington to attend the joint annual meeting of the bank and its affiliated global finance organization, the International Monetary Fund.
China has borrowed about $7.64 billion to modernize its economy since it joined the World Bank in 1980.
Mr. Conable also disclosed the bank's goal in negotiations among leading donor nations over fincancing for the International Development Association, a subsidiary of the World Bank that assists the world's poorest countries, chiefly in Africa. 
The negotiations begin tomorrow in Washington.
He said that for the three years beginning next July 1 the I.D.A. would need contributions totaling $14 billion to $14.5 billion just to equal the purchasing power of the last replenishment, which totaled $12.4 billion and which runs out next June 30.
