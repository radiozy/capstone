
A human rights group said today that China had sentenced 10 Tibetans, mostly monks and nuns, to prison terms of as long as nine years for taking part in independence protests this year.
The group, the Tibetan Information Network, said the longest sentence was given to Lobsand Dorje, a 20-year-old monk. 
He was sentenced on Sept. 
16 to nine years in prison on charges of encouraging 20 colleagues to demonstrate outside a temple in Lhasa in May, the group said.
The Tibet Information Network, which is based in London, said that those sentenced were not represented by lawyers in the trials and that it had received reports that several had been beaten or tortured. 
The group said it based its information on Chinese court documents. 
Officials in Lhasa could not be reached for comment.
In addition to the monk sentenced to nine years, one was sentenced to eight years, one to seven years and one 18-year-old monk to six years. 
One nun and one layman were sentenced to five years each, the group said.
