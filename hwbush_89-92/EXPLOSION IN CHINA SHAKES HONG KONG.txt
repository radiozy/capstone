
Soldiers blew up a mountain in southern China today in what was described as the world's biggest non-nuclear explosion, sending earthquake-like tremors through Hong Kong and Macao.
Chinese Army engineers detonated 12,000 tons of dynamite inside Paotai Mountain to clear land for the expansion of an airport in Zhuhai, an economic development zone.
Windows rattled in Macao, the Portuguese territory, but the police there said there were no reports of injuries or damage.
The official New China News Agency said 1,000 technicians had spent months preparing for the explosion. 
"Technical measures have been taken to buffer the magnitude of the shock from about 4 on the Richter scale to about 2.5," it added.
 
Tremor in Hong Kong
But seismologists in Hong Kong, 40 miles from Macao, said the explosion, at 12:50 A.M. Eastern standard time, had been felt by many people and measured 3.4 on the Richter scale.
Television news in Hong Kong showed film of Paotai disintegrating in an enormous cloud of debris, and Chinese military officers at the scene said the blast was the world's biggest non-nuclear explosion.
The blast shifted nearly 388 million cubic feet of earth and stone to make way for an expansion of Zhuhai's helicopter airport, which is to include runways capable of taking airliners as large as the Boeing 737, the Chinese news agency said.
 
Bigger Than Hiroshima
The Guinness Book of Records lists the biggest non-nuclear blast as the detonation of 7,122 tons of explosives at German naval fortifications on Helgoland, a island in the North Sea, at the end of World War II.
The atomic bomb that destroyed Hiroshima, Japan, in 1945 had a force equivalent to 11,800 tons of TNT.
China has carried out underground tests of nuclear weapons equivalent to one million tons of TNT.
