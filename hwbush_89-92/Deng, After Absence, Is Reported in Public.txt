
China's senior leader, Deng Xiaoping, who has not been seen in public for more than three months, met this morning with a visiting American professor, the official New China News Agency reported.
The one-paragraph item said that Mr. Deng had met with T. D. Lee, a professor of physics at Columbia University, in the Great Hall of the People.
It gave no further details, and Mr. Lee could not be reached for comment. 
A woman who answered the telephone in Mr. Lee's hotel room said she could not answer any questions and hung up.
Mr. Deng last appeared in public on June 9, when he spoke to military officers, praising troops after the June 3-4 crackdown on pro-democracy demonstrators.
The report about the meeting seemed intended to reassure the Chinese and foreigners that Mr. Deng is still relatively healthy. 
China's Foreign Ministry has denied rumors about the leader's ailments, but the reports gained credence after Mr. Deng did not appear early this month to meet the visiting head of state from the West African nation of Burkina Faso.
Chinese officials and East European diplomats say that Mr. Deng's health deteriorated last month while he was vacationing in the beach resort of Beidaihe. 
A medical team reportedly was sent to care for him, and one diplomat said that Mr. Deng underwent surgery late last month to search for signs of cancer of the prostate.
But officials characterized the ailment as not life threatening. 
Mr. Deng, who turned 85 years old last month, holds only one remaining position in China, as chairman of the central military commission. 
However, he retains his unofficial role as the ultimate arbiter of major economic and political decisions.
