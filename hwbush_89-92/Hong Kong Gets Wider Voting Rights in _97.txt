
The people of Hong Kong won a small battle today when China agreed to allow them a slightly greater degree of freedom to elect their own leaders as the territory passes to Chinese rule in 1997.
But the small concession by Beijing was unlikely to ease the tensions and alarm in Hong Kong over its future, and there have been calls for demonstrations to protest the overall restrictions and lack of democracy in the document that is now being prepared here in Beijing.
The final draft of the document, called the Basic Law, will be voted on Friday by the 51-member Drafting Committee. 
It then is to be sent to the National People's Congress, China's legislature, for approval.
A main issue in the draft of the Basic Law, which will govern Hong Kong after 1997, has been the composition of the territory's future legislature. 
China wanted to limit the number of directly-elected legislative seats to 18, or 30 percent of the legislature, while various Hong Kong leaders called for up to 30.
Today, the Chinese side agreed to allow 20 directly elected seats. 
China also decided to increase the limit on the number of foreign passport holders who would be allowed to serve in the legislature to 20 percent, from 15.
