
The Government tonight rejected as "unacceptable" a United States Senate vote attaching conditions to the extension of trade benefits for China.
In a statement read on the evening television news, the Foreign Ministry said the Congressional action constituted "interference in China's internal affairs."
The brief item on the news did not mention that the Senate's 55-to-44 vote on Tuesday was short of the two-thirds majority needed to override a Presidential veto. 
Because President Bush is almost certain to veto the measure, it appears likely that China will get an extension of its "most-favored-nation" trade benefits without any conditions attached.
Earlier today, A Chinese Government official said at a news conference that Beijing welcomed Mr. Bush's support for unconditional renewal of the trade status.
