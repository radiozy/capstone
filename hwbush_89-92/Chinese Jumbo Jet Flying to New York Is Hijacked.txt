
A Chinese jumbo jet bound for New York was hijacked today and forced to land in western Japan, Japanese authorities said.
The Boeing 747 with 223 people aboard was on a flight from Beijing to New York when it deviated from its course and flew toward South Korea, a Japanese Foreign Ministry official said.
 
Tried to Land in Seoul
Flight 981 of the Civil Aviation Administration of China, China's flagship airline, tried to land in South Korea but was rejected by South Korean authorities, said the Foreign Ministry official, who by custom would not be identified by name.
South Korea and China have no diplomatic relations, and there are no commercial flights between the two nations.
Japan's Defense Agency and air authorities said the Chinese aircraft landed at Fukuoka's international airport, on Japan's westernmost main island of Kyushu.
In Beijing, an official at the Beijing headquarters of the Civil Aviation Administration of China, the state airline administration, confirmed that Flight 981 from Beijing to New York had been hijacked. 
He said he had no further information.
The flight was scheduled to leave Beijing at 10 A.M. (9 P.M. Friday in New York) and stop at Shanghai before going on to New York. 
Chinese sources said it was hijacked after the Shanghai stop.
Earlier, South Korea's Yonhap news agency quoted unidentified Government sources as saying that a Chinese plane on a domestic flight was hijacked and flew over South Korea's southern-most island of Cheju at about 1:40 p.m.
In 1983, six Chinese hijacked a plane to South Korea. 
They were imprisoned for less than a year and resettled in Taiwan, where they received heroes' welcomes.
Two men hijacked another domestic flight to Taiwan in May 1988. 
A hijacking attempt in April failed when the pilot pretended to comply with the hijacker's demand to go to Taiwan, but instead landed at the mainland city of Fuzhou. 
The hijacker committed suicide by detonating explosives strapped to his back.
