
A tough austerity policy this year slowed China's runaway economy, but it will take at least three years to put it back on course, the Government says.
 
The People's Daily quoted
Yuan Mu of the State Council as saying on Tuesday that industrial output grew in the first nine months by 8.9 percent from the like period last year, down from a 17.7 percent gain in 1988 over the previous year.
Inflation climbed to a more than 30 percent rate in the first quarter, a 40-year record, before cooling off somewhat to a 17 percent rate in July. 
The austerity program was introduced in September last year.
But Mr. Yuan warned that it would take at least three years to cure the serious economic sicknesses that had worsened since 1984 - low efficiency and enormous waste, runaway capital construction and an irrational structure, with loss of control in some areas.
