
The police in Beijing last week detained three more pro-democracy activists, including Shen Tong, a leader of the Tiananmen movement in 1989 and a graduate student at Boston University who had returned to China to set up a chapter of a pro-democracy organization that he heads.
Also detained were two associates, Qi Dafeng and Qian Liyun. 
They were taken from the family home of Mr. Shen about 4 A.M. Tuesday. Li Yixian, Mr. Shen's mother, said the police passed her a note written by her son that said he was being questioned at a hotel but giving no location.
Mr. Shen, apparently the most prominent democracy leader to return from exile, had planned to meet with foreign reporters to discuss his plans for the United States-based Democracy for China Fund.
The police gave no reason for the detentions but they confiscated computer disks, notebooks and other materials Mr. Shen had collected on travels throughout China during the last month interviewing intellectuals, workers, peasants and bureaucrats for the fund.
Also last week, a letter smuggled from Lingyuan Prison in northeast China was made public, saying that pro-democracy inmates were often beaten, shocked with electric cattle prods and forced to work 12 hours or more at a time. 
The letter, released by the human rights group Asia Watch, said that Liu Gang, the best known political prisoner at Lingyuan, has been beaten frequently, kept in 20-pound leg irons, shocked repeatedly and forced to sit motionless on a bench from 8 A.M. to 9 P.M. each day.
