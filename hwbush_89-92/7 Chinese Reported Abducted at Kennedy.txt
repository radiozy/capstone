
Seven Chinese peasants were abducted at Kennedy International Airport last night minutes after arriving to take part in an agricultural project, the Port Authority police said.
The seven peasants, all men, were part of a group of 16 who came to the United States to study organic farming under the auspices of the World Energy Foundation, a nonprofit group.
The organization's president, Molly Aitken, told a Port Authority Police officer, Kevin Devlin, that the seven peasants were hustled away at the terminal shortly before 6 P.M. by three Asian men shouting orders in Chinese.
The three men, who were not brandishing weapons, shoved the peasants into a compact car with New Jersey license plates, Officer Devlin said.
Ms. Aitken told the police that the State Department had warned her that Chinese peasants have occasionally been kidnapped and used for cheap labor in Chinatown.
After the incident, Ms. Aitken and the nine remaining members of the group headed to the Green Gables farm in Athol, Mass., where they are to study organic farming for 11 months.
The group is from the Fujian Province in southern China, Ms. Aitken said.
