
A Chinese official vowed today that any democratic reforms carried out in Hong Kong by the British Government would be dismantled when Beijing takes over the colony in 1997.
Members of the National People's Congress said at a meeting that China would reorganize legislative, judicial and administrative branches of the Hong Kong Government if the British authorities took steps to introduce democratic safeguards before the takeover, the national radio said.
China has attacked plans by the Governor of Hong Kong, Christopher Patten, to expand the number of elected members of the territory's legislature. 
The radio quoted Li Ho, the Chinese official who drafted the Basic Law, a form of mini-constitution for Hong Kong after it reverts to Chinese rule.
If Mr. Patten "continues to break his promises and stubbornly press his own ideas and make improper arrangements contravening the Basic Law and sabotage the peaceful transition of power," Mr. Li said, "the Chinese side will establish its own legislative, judicial and administrative organs" in 1997. 
Beijing has steadily criticized even modest moves to introduce more popular participation in Hong Kong's Government.
