
Probably every New Yorker has at some time, somewhere in the city, felt like a stranger in a foreign land. 
At the Chinese opera, for example. 
For years, any Westerner who attended its infrequent presentations and who did not understand the language and conventions of the genre was decidedly an outsider, not necessarily welcomed.
The Taipei Theater, administered by the Chinese Information and Culture Center, has changed all that with its regular presentations of opera. 
It supplies not only supertitles but also superbly informative program booklets and occasional demonstrations. 
This weekend and next, the theater is offering one of its most appealing productions to date, "The Monkey King Raids the Heavens," performed by Li Bao-chun and His Peking Opera Troupe.
The mischievous Monkey King, one of the most famous characters in Chinese folk tradition, has had other artistic representations in New York recently, including a production early this year at the same theater by the New Generation Drama Association of Taipei. 
But Mr. Li gives the tale the full operatic treatment, Chinese-style, with exquisite costumes, highly stylized drama and even acrobatics.
One scene in particular, "The Battle of the Gods," is as lavish and vigorous an exercise as could possibly be crammed onto so small a stage, with bodies hurtling in all directions. 
The music, too, offers rich rewards once the exotic idiom has begun to settle into the ear. 
This is truly something (to salvage a much-abused word) special.
"The Monkey King" will be performed today and tomorrow at 7:30 P.M. and Sunday afternoon at 2, at the Taipei Theater, 1221 Avenue of the Americas, at 49th Street, in the concourse level of the McGraw-Hill building. 
Performances will also be given the same times next weekend. 
Tickets are $8; $5 for students and the elderly.
