
China has extended a commodity loan valued at $730 million to the Soviet Union, to buy Chinese grain, meat, peanuts, tea and other products, an official newspaper said today.
A Soviet Embassy official confirmed the report but declined to discuss details such as the terms of the loan. 
The Chinese report said only that the terms were "favorable."
Foreign diplomats said the loan appeared intended not only to bolster trade and friendship with the Soviet Union, but also as a small contribution to help preserve stabilityI there.
Prime Minister Li Peng was reported today to have told Deputy Prime Minister Yuri Masylukov of the Soviet Union, who is visiting Beijing, that China hoped that the Soviet Union would enjoy political stabilityI and that its economy would recover soon.
 
Loan Was 'Very Timely'
"The loan is part of the effort to bolster the relationship," a foreign diplomat said, adding that a political collapse of the Soviet Union could have enormous implications for China. 
"They don't want to see that happen. 
They want to see Gorbachev maintain socialism."
China Daily, an official newspaper, valued the loan at one billion Swiss francs, which at present exchange rates is equal to about $730 million. 
The newspaper quoted Prime Minister Li as saying that the assistance was "rather limited" and that Mr. Masylukov had expressed thanks and said the loan was "very timely."
The loan had been expected, and some diplomats say they believe it is part of a larger package that will include significant sales by Moscow of weapons and fighter planes to China.
A little less than a year ago, China extended a commodity loan valued at $333 million to the Soviet Union, to purchase clothing and light consumer goods that are now plentiful in Chinese shops but much sought after in the Soviet Union. 
While China is much poorer than the Soviet Union by most measures, the supply of goods on the market is much greater in China.
