
Soaring imports contributed to a $3.8 billion deficit in trade and tourism for China last year, official reports said Friday.
 
The State Administration of
Foreign Exchange recorded an increase, however, in China's foreign currency reserves, excluding gold, to $17.3 billion, from $15.2 billion in 1987, The China Daily said.
The report said China posted a $7.13 billion surplus in its capital accounts, including direct investment and international loans.
The $3.8 billion deficit in the current account, which includes trade and tourism, was largely a result of a doubling of the nation's trade deficit because of sluggish export growth and soaring imports.
 
According to International
Monetary Fund accounting procedures, China's exports in 1988 totaled $41 billion, up 18.2 percent over 1987, while its imports grew 27.4 percent, to $46.3 billion, the report said.
China's Customs Administration on Thursday reported a $700 million trade deficit for July, the official New China News Agency said. 
The nation's import growth slowed from 37.7 percent in June to 16 percent in July. 
Imports totaled $5.18 billion, while exports grew one-tenth of 1 percent over June, to $4.4 billion.
From January to June, China posted a trade deficit of $5.79 billion, up from $1.22 billion in the first half of 1988, according to Customs statistics.
