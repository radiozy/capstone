
China today denied reports that it had held secret trials of those involved in last year's democracy movement and also appeared to deny that it had arrested as many as 10,000 people in connection with the movement.
The official New China News Agency quoted a senior Chinese judge, whom it did not identify, as sharply criticizing a foreign news account that some 800 Chinese had been sentenced recently for their role in the democracy movement.
The article, which appeared in The Washington Post last week, attributed the report to Communist Party members.
The judge said this was ''sheer fabrication'' and in particular denied that any protesters had been tried secretly. 
But the dispatch did say that there had been some cases ''arising from the anti-Government riots'' that were tried openly.
 
Post Stands by Its Reports
The Post said it stood by its reports by Daniel Southerland, its Beijing correspondent.
Mike Getler, the Post's assistant managing editor for foreign news, said, ''The attack on Southerland's reporting by Chinese authorities contains no actual evidence to discredit his reports.''
The authorities have released almost no information about those arrested or the disposition of their cases. 
This was the first time an official denied that the number of protesters detained had been as high as generally believed in the West, where most estimates start at 10,000 and go higher, although some have been released. 
In the past, Communist Party and Government officials, including General Secretary Jiang Zemin, have declined to comment on questions about arrests and detentions.
The Government's denial, like other recent measures such as the lifting of martial law earlier this month, seemed aimed at improving China's image abroad, particularly before the United States Congress deliberates on sanctions against Beijing.
