
Whatever the changes sweeping the rest of the Communist world, dissent for Chinese is best practiced at a distance. 
Thus did Chai Ling, one of the top student leaders of the Beijing democracy movement a year ago, reportedly turn up in France last week.
She and her husband, Feng Congde, went underground 10 months ago when they and 19 others were accused of leading the ''counterrevolutionary rebellion,'' as the authorities called it, that ended bloodily in Tiananmen Square. 
In a taped television interview broadcast in Hong Kong, Miss Chai said, ''I am safe and sound now. 
Please put your minds to rest.''
In a subsequent interview published in The China Times in Taiwan, she was quoted as saying the democracy movement might have gotten further if it had happened around the time of similar movements in Poland, East Germany and elsewhere, rather than preceding them by six months. 
''We might have had a good chance to succeed if our movement had come after the pro-democracy trend in Eastern Europe,'' she said. 
Though out of China, she and her husband remained in hiding.
