
Japan announced Friday that it was resuming a development loan program for China that has been frozen since the brutal suppression of pro-democracy protests in Beijing last year.
Japan will make available up to $283 million to China for seven projects "to contribute to economic stabilityI and to promote the economic modernization efforts of the country," the Foreign Ministry said.
It said in a statement that Japan's Ambassador to China, Hiroshi Hashimoto, and Qi Huaiyuan, a Chinese Foreign Ministry official, had exchanged a memorandum of understanding on the plan.
The money is part of a five-year package of development loans to China totaling $6.28 billion.
In July, Prime Minister Toshiki Kaifu promised to resume the loans gradually. 
Other industrialized democracies have also been gradually easing sanctions on China in recent months.
The terms of the loans to China include an annual interest rate of 2.5 percent and a 30-year repayment period. 
Japan will not require that Japanese companies be involved in the programs.
