
Before June 5, 1989, Fang Lizhi's fame was generally limited to the scientific community, where he was known as a distinguished Chinese astrophysicist. 
But then came the crackdown on pro-democracy demonstrators in Beijing, and Mr. Fang, who believes Marxism had failed in China, was accused of being an instigator. 
Mr. Fang and his wife, Li Shuxian, took asylum in the United States Embassy, where they lived as China's most well-known dissidents until last week, when China allowed them to take an American plane to London.
A Chinese Government statement said Mr. Fang was being allowed to leave to seek medical treatment, and mentioned, as if it were news, that he and Miss Li had admitted opposing the Four Cardinal Principles of the Chinese Constitution, which include adherence to Marxism-Leninism. 
Most diplomats believe the United States' extension of most-favored-nation trading status to China, shortly before the release, played an important part in the negotiations. 
For now, Mr. Fang said at Cambridge University, where he will continue his research, ''We look forward to a period of peace and quiet.''
