
Two-thirds of Americans approve of the way President Bush is dealing with recent events in China, and majorities concur with several of the Administration's recent policies toward China, according to the latest Gallup Poll.
Public reactions to events in China are one of several foreign policy developments that have helped boost Mr. Bush's overall approval rating in the last month.
Seventy percent in the new survey approved of the way Mr. Bush is handling his job as President, up from 56 percent who approved in Gallup's previous poll, taken May 4 to 7.
The latest telephone survey, based on interviews with 1,235 adults nationwide, was conducted from June 8 to 11 and had a margin of sampling error of plus or minus 4 percentage points.
In reacting to the situation in China, 67 percent of the public approved of the way President Bush is dealing with events there. 
Majorities of the public also supported several of the Administration's recent policies toward the Chinese Government.
For example, 75 percent favored suspending the sale of American arms to China, a step already taken by the Administration. 
Seventy-one percent favored permitting Chinese students now in the United States to remain here after their visas expire.
But 72 percent opposed breaking off diplomatic relations with China, and 49 percent opposed recalling the American Ambassador from China. 
The Bush Administration has also opposed both these steps.
