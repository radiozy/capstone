
China today formally invited the Soviet leader, Mikhail S. Gorbachev, to come to Beijing for their nations' first summit meeting in 30 years, and the Soviet Union accepted.
Mr. Gorbachev will meet with China's senior leader, Deng Xiaoping, in an effort to improve relations that turned hostile in 1960 when their predecessors, Nikita S. Khrushchev and Mao Zedong, quarreled over the border and leadership of the world Communist movement.
''The coming Chinese-Soviet summit will be an event of great significance,'' the official New China News Agency quoted Prime Minister Li Peng as telling the Soviet Foreign Minister, Eduard A. Shevardnadze.
''It's official,'' a Soviet Embassy spokesman said. 
He said Mr. Shevardnadze, who has been in China since Wednesday, would make an announcement about the date on Saturday after consulting with Mr. Deng.
