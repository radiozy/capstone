
China will open what it says is the first futures market in a socialist country this summer, an official newspaper reported today.
The market will open in August in the central city of Zhengzhou in Henan Province and will handle wheat transactions, The China Daily said.
If the market is successful, the Government may open futures markets for corn in the northeast city of Changchun, rice in the central city of Wuhan and pigs in southwest China's Sichuan Province, the paper said.
In the past, China has branded futures markets a form of capitalist speculation. 
The nation's conservative leaders have stressed that market forces must play a subsidiary role in China's planned economy.
A futures market allows traders and producers to negotiate a set price for a commodity at a specified future date. 
The seller is guaranteed that price regardless of subsequent fluctuations in the market.
Although most Chinese agricultural products must be sold to the state for a set price, an estimated 65 million tons of agricultural commodities circulate in the free market every year.
Poor regulation of the free market has led to wild price fluctuations for produce and livestock and serious gaps in supply and demand.
The paper said foreign dealers will not be allowed to participate in the Zhengzhou market at the outset.
