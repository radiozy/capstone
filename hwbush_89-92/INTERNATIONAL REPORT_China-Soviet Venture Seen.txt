
The cautious process of rebuilding the strained relations between the Soviet Union and China have taken another modest step forward with a report here today that the first Chinese-Soviet joint venture in China will soon be established.
The China Daily reported that the countries are close to signing an agreement to build a linen mill in the remote town of Batou, Inner Mongolia, near the Soviet border.
The mill would produce four million meters of printed linen a year from flax, the article quoted Ji Jin, an official of the Ministry of Textiles, as saying.
Nearly two dozen joint ventures between the countries are in the Soviet Union, mostly restaurants.
The chill between the Communist giants, which has lasted about 30 years, began to thaw last year with the visit here of President Mikhail S. Gorbachev. Li Peng, China's Prime Minister, made the first visit by a Chinese prime minister to Moscow in 25 years in April.
The article said the Soviets were also considering a similar project in a small town in Sichuan Province, in western China. 
Such steps would be decidedly cautious, keeping the Soviets in remote regions of the country and away from the cities.
Nonetheless, trade between China and the Soviet Union has been growing steadily, enlivening border towns that were walled off from the other side for decades.
For instance, a new highway was opened in March connecting the Chinese town of Suifenhe, in Heilongjiang Province, making it one of the country's busiest trade centers. 
Last year, the town handled a quarter of the freight passing between the countries, and enterprises throughout China and the Soviet Union have set up offices there, according to reports in The China Daily.
